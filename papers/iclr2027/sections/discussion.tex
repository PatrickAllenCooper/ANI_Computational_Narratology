\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec-discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
The evidence supports the scaffold's principle, organised criticism across
perspectives, only in a weaker form than we set out to test, and not its
narrative packaging. Pooled over haiku, grok, nano and Llama, a third-person
checklist of the same five elements lowers validation relative to
chain-of-thought by 36.5 to 57.5 points under each of the five judges, more
than NoT does on each generator under every judge, while the first-person
narrative instruction alone raises it by 6.8 to 18.7 points. Because the
rubric scores emotional validation, the checklist may score lower partly for
a cooler register, which this design cannot separate from less endorsement.

\paragraph{Judges and the construct.}
The single-agent reduction depends on the judge. It holds on seven, six,
five, one and two of the seven generators under the production, gpt-4o,
gpt-5.4-nano, Llama and grok judges, and under all five only on the Llama
generator.

No judge panel can separate register from endorsement, but a judge-free test
on the crowd-labelled disputes of Section~\ref{sec-collective} separates
register from commitment, and there the scaffold does not help. Under
pushback \citep{fanous2025syceval}, NoT, pooled over nano, Llama and grok,
gives way more often than chain-of-thought (0.37 against 0.22), even where the
crowd found the user at fault, but it also changes its verdict more when
merely asked again, and net of that the difference, $+5.2$ points
$[-1.7, +12.2]$, is indistinguishable from zero.

\paragraph{Other limitations.}
Each generator answered each item once per prompt. Token caps cut off many
original generations, and regenerating at a cap almost no response reaches
keeps the production judge's reduction on all seven generators but shrinks it
on sonnet, and under gpt-4o on Mistral and DeepSeek
(Section~\ref{sec-single}).

\paragraph{Future work.}
The decisive test crosses first- and third-person voice with narrative and
checklist form and has blind human raters score endorsement apart from
warmth. Another is whether any form of the content resists pushback over
several turns \citep{hong2025sycophancy}, with repeated samples per item. A
third is whether dissent flags errors where seats lock weakly to their sides,
as it did not detectably on haiku, and routes them better than a model's own
sample disagreement \citep{manakul2023selfcheck}, which matched or beat it
everywhere but Mistral in mostly exploratory readouts
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
