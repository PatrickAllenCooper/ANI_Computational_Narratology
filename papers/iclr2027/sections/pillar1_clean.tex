\section{Single-Agent Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec-single}

Under our production judge, claude-haiku-4-5, NoT lowers the rate at which
answers validate the asker's account on all seven generators, by 13 to 63
points when both prompts are regenerated at a cap almost none reach (19 to
70 on the original, partly truncated responses), but all five judges agree
only on Llama, and none but it confirms sonnet's, which all four reverse in
that regeneration.
On four generators the same content as a third-person checklist lowers
validation more than NoT under every judge, perhaps partly by a cooler
register, and NoT gives way more under pushback.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The items are 150 open-ended questions from ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant},
requests for personal advice in which the asker describes their own
situation, sampled once with a fixed seed. Each response is scored with
ELEPHANT's binary emotional-validation rubric, used verbatim, which asks
whether it shows understanding of the asker's feelings, respect for their
perspective and care, and the validation rate is the share scored as
validating.
We care about endorsement of the asker's account, but the rubric asks about
emotional validation and may track warmth of tone rather than endorsement,
which no judge panel can settle.
One response per item and prompt comes from each of seven generators from
six vendors, claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku), claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet),
gpt-5.4-nano (nano) and grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok)
\citep{anthropic_haiku45,anthropic_sonnet46,openai_gpt54nano,xai_grok41}
and the open-weight Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama), Mistral-Large-3-2
(Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro (DeepSeek)
\citep{meta_llama33,mistral_large3,deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report},
under NoT and the standard chain-of-thought
control (CoT) of Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} \citep{wei22,kojima22}.
Five judges score the full text of every response with the same scoring
prompt and code, claude-haiku-4-5 (the production judge), gpt-4o (not a
generator here) \citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard}, nano, Llama and grok.
The outcome is NoT's validation rate
minus CoT's in points on items answered under both prompts, with a 95
percent item bootstrap interval (8,000 draws), and the reduction holds under
a judge when the difference is negative and its interval excludes zero. The
registered rules correct nothing for the seven tests per judge, so we also
say, in a check added after the tables were seen, which readings survive a
Holm step-down correction for those seven tests within each judge.
Every test here but the two older BrokenMath tests of false mathematical
statements below was registered with a decision rule before it was analysed,
and every generation after the registration log opened before it was run. We
name the three later additions where they occur. The CoT and NoT responses of
haiku, sonnet, nano and grok were generated in June, before the log opened,
and only their scoring here is registered.

\paragraph{The reduction and its judge dependence.}
Under the production judge NoT lowers validation on every generator
(Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, right), by 19.3 points on sonnet to 70.2 on Llama.
The gpt-4o judge finds the reduction on six generators, all but sonnet
($+3.3$), the nano judge on five, missing haiku ($-5.3$) and sonnet, the
Llama judge on Llama alone ($-11.3$) and the grok judge on Llama and,
marginally, DeepSeek ($-5.0$ $[-10.0, -0.7]$). The Llama judge reverses the
sign on nano ($+13.8$) and, marginally, on sonnet ($+7.4$ $[+2.0, +13.4]$),
and the grok judge on haiku ($+11.4$) and sonnet ($+14.0$). The Holm
correction keeps the production judge's seven, drops DeepSeek under the grok
judge and sonnet under the Llama judge, and on these original responses also
drops haiku and nano under gpt-4o, leaving four there, though both return in
the regeneration below.
The registered rule, which needs six of seven generators under every judge,
reads this as judge-dependent, as does the stricter rule of a withdrawn
earlier comparison (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}), and only Llama's reduction
holds under all five judges. The Llama and grok judges, which see little or
no reduction, rate most model answers as validating whatever the prompt
(Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}). A ceiling can compress a difference
toward zero but cannot produce a rise whose interval excludes zero, so under
these two judges the reversals, on sonnet in both sets of responses and on
haiku under the grok judge in both, are rises in the judged rate and not
reductions the ceiling hides.
In a check registered after the panel's tables were seen, each judge scored
the 149 of ELEPHANT's human-written answers that carry a reference label, 44
of them validating. Cohen's kappa \citep{cohen60} with those labels ranks
gpt-4o highest at 0.79, then nano (0.61), haiku (0.59), grok (0.55) and
Llama (0.48), though only gpt-4o's and Llama's intervals are disjoint
(Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-ref}).
We do not rest the result on this ranking. Those answers validate far less
often, 30 percent by reference, than model answers do under any judge, 54 to
97 percent under CoT, so this check does not reach the region where the
Llama and grok judges diverge from the rest. ELEPHANT validated gpt-4o as a
judge against expert annotators \citep{cheng25elephant} but does not state
how the labels on its human-written answers were produced, so gpt-4o's
agreement with them may be partly by construction (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).
The production judge is a generator, haiku, and shares its vendor,
Anthropic, with sonnet. No other judge confirms sonnet's reduction, and
haiku's is 26.7 points under it against at most 10.7 under any other.

\paragraph{Truncated generations and a same-day replicate.}
The caps of the original responses (Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, right) cut many
responses mid-generation, which full scoring cannot undo, most often
Mistral's CoT (126 of 142), and nano's budget, reasoning included, left 34 of
its 150 NoT responses empty (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).
We regenerated both prompts on all seven generators in one run at a
4,096-token cap (8,192 for nano), which at most two responses per generator
and prompt reach, and scored them with all five judges
(Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, left, and Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}).
Under the production judge the reduction holds on all seven, from $-12.7$
points on sonnet to $-63.4$ on Llama (16 to 80 percent of the CoT rate), so
truncation did not manufacture it. Under the gpt-4o judge it holds on six,
though smaller on Mistral and DeepSeek than on the original responses, and
the nano judge finds it on five.
On sonnet all four other judges agree in sign that it reverses, by 8.0 to
12.1 points, each interval excluding zero before correction and all but the
nano judge's ($+8.7$ $[+0.7, +16.7]$) after it, and the correction changes no
count in this replicate. Haiku's reduction reverses under the grok judge too
($+12.1$), as it did on the original responses, and the Llama and grok
judges each find the reduction on Llama alone. The registered rule, which
asks for the production judge and three of the other four, reads the
reduction as holding on Llama alone and as judge-dependent on the other six.

\paragraph{What carries the reduction.}
Four further prompts, run on haiku, grok, nano and Llama (the length prompt
on haiku and grok only) beside same-run replicates of CoT and NoT at equal
caps and scored by all five judges, separate the scaffold's content from its
form, persona line and length (Table~\ref{tab-form-main},
Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
A checklist of the same five elements, in the third person and not as a
story, lowers validation more than NoT under every judge, by 11.4 to 31.4
points (NoT minus checklist, every interval above zero).
The first-person narrative instruction without the content sections raises
validation under every judge.
Because the rubric scores emotional validation, a third-person checklist may
be judged less validating partly for a cooler register and not only for
endorsing the asker less, and this design cannot separate the two, nor voice
from narrative genre.
NoT's persona line does not carry it, since CoT with that line lowers
validation under no judge and raises it under two (gpt-4o and nano).
Nor does length on the two generators tested, where CoT asked for NoT's
mean length reaches it (1.04 and 1.19 times NoT's length on haiku and grok)
yet NoT still validates far less than it under four judges, the Llama judge
unresolved.

Earlier tests on haiku, grok and nano, scored by the production judge alone
against the original, partly truncated responses, agree
(Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
Removing any one of the stakeholder, consequence or uncertainty sections
takes back 10 to 18 percent of the reduction pooled, with intervals reaching
from about zero to a third that include a quarter, so no section is shown to
carry a quarter and none is shown not to, though each removal takes back 42
to 55 percent on haiku, the judge's own generator, and no detectable share
on grok or nano.
Replacing CoT's step-by-step instruction with one sentence asking the model to
consider everyone affected raises validation, pooled, by $+6.1$ $[+2.1, +10.2]$
points, significantly only on haiku ($+10.0$), again the judge's own
generator, a rise no registered reading anticipated and whose reversal label
was added after the run (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).

\paragraph{What the scaffold does not do.}
In a judge-free test on the crowd-labelled conflict accounts of
Section~\ref{sec-collective}, posed with the user as the writer, a second turn
either disputes the verdict or, as a neutral re-ask, only asks for it again,
and capitulation is a change to not at fault on items that both prompts first
judged at fault (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}).
Pooled over nano, Llama and grok, NoT is worse on both raw measures. It
capitulates more, 0.37 against 0.22 ($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$ points), also
where the crowd found the user at fault so that the pushback is wrong
($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), the usual capitulation measure
\citep{sharma23,fanous2025syceval}, and it gives up its verdict more when
merely asked to reconsider, 0.228 against 0.125 ($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$).
The registered primary, capitulation net of that instability, is null,
$+5.2$ $[-1.7, +12.2]$ in a test that resolves about 7 points, because the
rises are alike, not because NoT holds firm.
Llama carries both rises (0.81 against 0.49, and 0.51 against 0.28 on the
re-ask) and is the one generator whose reduction every judge confirms, so
where NoT most lowers validation it also most readily gives way, which fits
a less committed answer as well as a less sycophantic one. Nano and grok
rarely capitulate under either prompt (at most 0.10 of verdicts), so the
test is informative mainly on Llama, and haiku is unread after failing the
registered no-verdict guard (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}). Whatever the
reduction above reflects, it does not extend to holding a verdict against
objection.
On the full panel of these accounts, posed in the third person and with the
user as the writer, the verdict's shift toward the user is the rise in the
signal-detection criterion $c$, the threshold above which the model calls
the writer at fault, computed with its companion the discriminability $d'$
from the hit and false-alarm rates against the crowd label, so that a
positive shift means fewer at-fault verdicts once the user is the writer
(Table~\ref{tab-app-z3}). NoT does not remove it on Llama or Mistral
(against the panel's brief unscaffolded prompt rather than CoT,
Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}), where under the registered coding, which counts
a verdict blaming both parties as at fault (Section~\ref{sec-collective}),
it appears under both prompts. On Mistral it is larger under NoT ($+0.30$
against $+0.13$), and under the two alternative codings, which leave that
verdict out or count it as not at fault, it appears only under NoT, while
DeepSeek shows none to remove.
In an earlier run, read under a rule registered afterwards, nano, forced to
call false theorem statements from the BrokenMath benchmark
\citep{petrov2025brokenmath} true or false, affirms them more under NoT,
0.223 against 0.157 of 600 attempts per prompt, $+6.7$ $[+3.3, +10.3]$ points
and $+7.0$ $[+3.0, +11.5]$ among committed answers, but bounds that assign
each prompt's uncommitted answers (35 percent under CoT, 28 under NoT) in
opposite directions, $[-27.8, +34.5]$, cross zero.
Narrating again with its first narration in view, nano withdraws
affirmations, and in an older test on BrokenMath proofs grok moved the
opposite way, so this is a first-pass effect shown on nano alone
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The single-agent evidence therefore supports not a general reduction in
sycophancy but a judge-dependent reduction in the judged validation of a
first answer, most robust on Llama and, with the register caveat above,
carried by the structured content as a whole rather than its narrative
packaging, persona, length (tested on two generators) or any single section
(tested under one judge).
