\section{Single-Agent Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec-single}

Under our production judge NoT lowers the validation rate on all seven
generators, by 13 to 63 points once both prompts are regenerated at a token
cap almost no response reaches. All five judges find the reduction only on
Llama, and on sonnet the other four reverse it in that regeneration.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The items are a random 150 of the 3,027 open-ended requests for personal
advice about the asker's own situation in ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant}.
One response per item and prompt comes from each of seven generators from
six developers, haiku, sonnet, gpt-5.4-nano (nano) and
grok \citep{anthropic_haiku45,anthropic_sonnet46,openai_gpt54nano,xai_grok41}
and the open-weight Llama, Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro
(DeepSeek) \citep{meta_llama33,mistral_large3,deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report},
under NoT and CoT (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}).
Five judges, haiku (the production judge), gpt-4o (the only judge not also a
generator) \citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard} and the nano, Llama
and grok judges, read each question with the whole response, its written
reasoning included, and score it 1 if emotionally validating and 0 if not
under ELEPHANT's rubric, used verbatim (Appendix~\ref{app-prompts}), which
asks whether it shows understanding of the asker's feelings, respect for their
perspective and care.
The outcome, the drop, is NoT's validation rate minus CoT's in points on the
items the judge scored under both prompts, 116 to 150 per generator and judge,
and the reduction holds under a judge when its 95 percent item-clustered
interval, the middle 95 percent of 8,000 drops on resampled items with judge
and generation run fixed, lies below zero.
Every test here but two older ones on false mathematical statements, one
below, was registered with its decision rule before analysis in our own
append-only log, not a public registry, and every generation after the log
opened was registered before it ran.
The June
2026 original responses of haiku, sonnet, nano and grok predate the log, so
only their scoring is registered (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
No registered rule corrects for multiple comparisons, so a Holm step-down
over the seven generators per judge, added after the tables were seen, shows
which results survive (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}), and pooled, pushback and
collective results stay uncorrected.

\paragraph{The original responses under five judges.}
On the original, partly truncated responses (Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, right)
only the production judge finds the reduction on every generator, by 19.3
points on sonnet to 70.2 on Llama.
The gpt-4o judge finds it on six, all but sonnet, the nano judge
on five, missing haiku and sonnet, the Llama judge on Llama alone
and the grok judge on Llama and, marginally, DeepSeek. The Llama judge
reverses the sign on nano and, marginally, on sonnet, and the grok judge on
haiku and sonnet (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}). The Holm correction keeps
the production judge's seven reductions and drops those two marginal
results and, on these responses only, haiku and nano under gpt-4o.
The registered rule, a reduction on at least six of seven generators under
every judge, reads this as judge-dependent. We wrote that rule after a
since-withdrawn comparison had shown a generator failing a stricter
every-generator rule, which reads this panel the same way
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The Llama and grok judges rate most model
answers as validating whatever the prompt, and a registered screen for such
judges, removed as circular before this scoring, would have excluded the Llama
judge (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).
A ceiling can compress a difference toward zero but cannot produce a rise
whose interval excludes zero, so their reversals on sonnet, and on haiku under
the grok judge, each repeated in the regeneration, are rises in the judged
rate, not hidden reductions.
A check registered after these results were seen tests each judge
against ELEPHANT's validation labels on 149 human-written answers to the same
questions, and Cohen's kappa \citep{cohen60}, a chance-corrected agreement,
ranks gpt-4o highest at 0.79 and the Llama judge lowest at 0.48, the only
pair of judges whose intervals do not overlap.
We do not rest any result on this ranking, since those answers, far shorter
than the NoT answers that divide the judges most, validate far less often, 30
percent by reference, than CoT's under any judge, 54 to 97 percent, and
ELEPHANT does not state how its labels were produced
(Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).
The production judge, itself the generator haiku and an Anthropic model like
sonnet, alone finds sonnet's reduction and puts haiku's at 26.7 points, the
others at most 10.7.

\paragraph{The regeneration without truncation.}
The original caps, 1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT, cut many
responses, Mistral's CoT most often (126 of 142), and nano, whose hidden
reasoning counted against its 2,048 tokens, left 34 of its 150 NoT
responses empty (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).
Judging a cut response in full cannot restore its lost ending, so we
regenerated both prompts on all seven in one run at an equal 4,096-token cap
(8,192 for nano), which at most two non-empty responses per generator and
prompt reach, and, as registered, take the headline numbers from it
(Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, left).
Under the production judge the reduction holds on all seven, from $-12.7$
points on sonnet to $-63.4$ on Llama (a relative reduction of 16 to 80
percent), so truncation did not manufacture it. The gpt-4o judge finds it on
six, smaller than before on Mistral and DeepSeek, and the nano judge on five.
On sonnet all four other judges reverse it, by 8.0 to 12.1 points, each
interval excluding zero, all but the nano judge's after the Holm
correction, which changes no count of reductions. Haiku's reduction
reverses under the grok judge ($+12.1$), as before, and the Llama and grok
judges find it on Llama alone, so by this run's registered rule, a reduction
under the production judge and at least three of the other four, it holds
across judges on Llama alone and is judge-dependent on the other six.
A second such run on sonnet and haiku, whose reductions were the smallest,
registered before its calls but after the first was read, repeats the four
rises on sonnet ($+7.3$ to $+10.1$, the nano judge's interval reaching zero)
and puts the production-judge drop at $-32.7$ on haiku and $-4.7$
$[-13.3, +3.3]$ on sonnet, the one run where that judge's interval includes
zero, which the rule would read as failing, though as registered it revises
no conclusion above (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k} pools the runs after the
fact).

\paragraph{What carries the reduction.}
NoT differs from CoT in its sections, narrative form, persona line (``thoughtful
advisor'' for ``helpful assistant'') and length, so four further prompts
(Figure~\ref{fig-app-prompts}) each target one, on haiku, grok, nano and
Llama, length on the first two, against CoT and NoT regenerated in the same
run at 2,048 tokens, 8,192 for nano, a cap few reached, so no contrast also
changes date or cap (Table~\ref{tab-form-main}, Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
A third-person checklist of NoT's five sections under its persona line
lowers validation more than NoT under every judge, by 11.4 to 31.4 points
(each interval above zero).
As the rubric scores emotional validation, the checklist may be judged
less validating partly for a cooler tone, or register, not only for
endorsing the asker less, which this design cannot separate, nor voice from
genre.
First-person narration under NoT's persona line without the sections raises
validation above CoT under every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points.
The persona line does not carry the reduction, as CoT
with only that line swapped in lowers validation under no judge, raising
it under two (gpt-4o and nano).
Nor does length, as NoT validates less under four judges than CoT asked for
NoT's mean length in words, at 1.04 and 1.19 times NoT's characters on
haiku and grok, on haiku half or more because the request raises validation
above CoT, the Llama judge's interval on grok including zero.
Earlier tests on haiku, grok and nano, scored by the production judge alone
against the original, partly truncated responses, credit no single section,
each pooled interval straddling a quarter, the registered bar. Removing the
stakeholder, consequence or uncertainty section takes back no detectable
share of NoT's drop on grok or nano and 42 to 55 percent on haiku, the
judge's own generator.
Swapping CoT's step-by-step sentence for a request to consider everyone
affected raises validation, pooled, by $+6.1$ $[+2.1, +10.2]$ points, a rise
no registered outcome predicted.

\paragraph{What the scaffold does not do.}
A register-blind, judge-free pushback test, reading each reply's required
verdict label on the crowd-labelled Scruples accounts of
Section~\ref{sec-collective} posed as the user's own, finds that NoT gives up
a disputed verdict more often than CoT, and also more often when merely asked
to reconsider.
The same first answer is followed by a request to reconsider, after a pushback
(``I really don't think I was in the wrong here'') or alone as a neutral
re-ask (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}). Where both
prompts first blamed the user, capitulation is the share of verdicts moving to
blame only the other party or no one after the pushback, instability the same
share after the re-ask, and the registered primary, capitulation net of
instability, isolates what the disagreement adds.
Haiku is left out of the pool by a rule fixed after its results were seen, 14.1
percent of its NoT pushback replies lacking a verdict against a registered 5
percent limit.
Pooled over nano, Llama and grok, NoT capitulates more, 0.371 against 0.216
($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$
points), also where the crowd found the user at fault so that the pushback is
wrong ($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), and is less stable, 0.228 against 0.125
($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$). The net primary is null, $+5.2$ $[-1.7, +12.2]$
with a half-width of about 7 points, because the rises are alike, not because
NoT holds firm.
Llama, the one generator whose reduction every judge confirms, carries both
rises, so where NoT most lowers validation it also most readily
gives way, which fits a less committed answer as well as a less sycophantic
one. Nano and grok rarely capitulate under either prompt (at
most 0.10 of verdicts).
A second test on the same accounts asks whether NoT removes the verdict's
shift toward the user, blaming the writer less when the writer is the user
than in the third person, measured as the rise in the signal-detection
criterion $c$, the model's threshold for calling the writer at fault
(Table~\ref{tab-app-z3}). Reusing answers from Section~\ref{sec-collective},
it compares NoT with the brief unscaffolded prompt there, not with CoT.
NoT does not remove the shift on
Llama or Mistral, where under the registered coding, which counts blame on
both parties as at fault, it appears under both prompts. On Mistral it is
larger under NoT ($+0.30$ against $+0.13$) and, under the two alternative
codings, which leave that verdict out or count it as not at fault, appears
only under NoT.
DeepSeek shows none to remove (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
In an older test on nano alone, read under a rule registered afterwards, NoT
also affirms more false theorem statements from the BrokenMath benchmark
\citep{petrov2025brokenmath}, by $+6.7$ $[+3.3, +10.3]$ points, an effect
that a second NoT pass largely withdraws and whose worst-case bounds over
answers giving no verdict cross zero
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The single-agent evidence therefore supports not a general reduction in
sycophancy but a judge-dependent reduction in the judged validation of a
first answer, most robust on Llama and, with the register caveat above,
carried by the structured content as a whole rather than its narrative
form, persona, length (tested on two generators) or any single section
(tested under one judge).
