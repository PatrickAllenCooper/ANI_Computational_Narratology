\section{Single-Agent Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec-single}

Under our production judge, claude-haiku-4-5, NoT lowers the rate at which
answers validate the asker's own account on all seven generators, by 13 to 63
points when both prompts are regenerated
under a cap almost no response reaches (19 to 70 on the original, partly
truncated responses), but all five judges agree only on Llama, and in that
regeneration the other four reverse it on sonnet.
On four generators the same content as a third-person checklist lowers
validation more than NoT under every judge, perhaps partly through a cooler
register, and NoT does not protect against pushback.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The items are 150 open-ended questions from ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant},
requests for personal advice in which the asker describes their own
situation, sampled once with a fixed seed. Each response is scored with
ELEPHANT's binary emotional-validation rubric, used verbatim, which asks
whether it shows understanding of the asker's feelings, respect for their
perspective and care, and the validation rate is the share scored as
validating.
We care about endorsement of the asker's account, but the rubric asks about
emotional validation and may track warmth of tone rather than endorsement,
which no judge panel can settle.
One response per item and prompt comes from each of seven generators from
six vendors, claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku), claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet),
gpt-5.4-nano (nano) and grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok)
\citep{anthropic_haiku45,anthropic_sonnet46,openai_gpt54nano,xai_grok41}
and the open-weight Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama), Mistral-Large-3-2
(Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro (DeepSeek)\todo{PI: no bib keys for Llama,
Mistral or DeepSeek-V4-Pro}, under NoT and the standard chain-of-thought
control (CoT) of Figure~\ref{fig:prompt} \citep{wei22,kojima22}.
Five judges score the full text of every response with the same scoring
prompt and code, claude-haiku-4-5 (the production judge), gpt-4o (not a
generator here), nano, Llama and grok. The outcome is NoT's validation rate
minus CoT's in points on items answered under both prompts, with a 95
percent item bootstrap interval (8,000 draws), and the reduction holds under
a judge when the difference is negative and its interval excludes zero. Every test
here but the two older BrokenMath tests of false mathematical statements at
the end of this section was registered with a decision rule before it was
run or analysed, and we name the three later additions where they occur. The
CoT and NoT responses of haiku, sonnet, nano and grok were generated in June,
before the registration document opened, and only their scoring here is
registered.

\paragraph{The reduction and its judge dependence.}
Under the production judge NoT lowers validation on every generator
(Figure~\ref{fig:judges}), by 19.3 points on sonnet, 26.7 on haiku, 35.2 on
grok, 37.9 on nano, 46.1 on DeepSeek, 57.6 on Mistral and 70.2 on Llama,
close to the 22 to 70 points (31 to 87 percent of the CoT rate) of an
earlier, unpaired scoring by this judge that mixed stored and fresh scores.
The gpt-4o judge finds the reduction on six generators, all but sonnet
($+3.3$ $[-4.7, +11.3]$), the nano judge on five, missing haiku ($-5.3$
$[-15.3, +4.7]$) and sonnet, the Llama judge on Llama alone ($-11.3$
$[-19.1, -3.5]$) and the grok judge on Llama and DeepSeek. The Llama judge
reverses the sign on sonnet ($+7.4$ $[+2.0, +13.4]$) and nano ($+13.8$), and
the grok judge on haiku ($+11.4$ $[+5.4, +18.1]$) and sonnet ($+14.0$).
The registered rule, which needs six of seven generators under every judge
and was set after a since-withdrawn comparison had shown sonnet failing under
one judge, reads this as judge-dependent, as does that comparison's stricter
rule, and only Llama's reduction holds under all five judges. The Llama and grok judges, which see
little or no reduction, rate most model answers as validating whatever the
prompt (11 and 9 of the 14 generator and prompt rates above 0.85, against at
most one under each other judge), a description computed on the rates whose
difference is tested.
In a check registered after the panel's tables were seen and before it was
computed, each judge scored the 149 of ELEPHANT's human-written answers that
carry a reference label, 44 of them validating. Cohen's kappa \citep{cohen60}
with those labels ranks gpt-4o highest at 0.79 $[0.67, 0.89]$, then nano
(0.61), haiku (0.59), grok (0.55) and Llama (0.48), though only gpt-4o's and
Llama's intervals are disjoint, and on these answers the grok judge validates
less often than the production judge (35 against 39 percent).
We do not rest the result on this ranking. ELEPHANT validated gpt-4o as a
judge against expert annotators, with at least 0.83 accuracy and 0.65 kappa
on every metric \citep{cheng25elephant}, but the ELEPHANT paper does not
state how the labels on its human-written answers were produced, so gpt-4o's
agreement with them may be partly by construction.
The production judge is a generator, haiku, and shares its vendor,
Anthropic, with sonnet. No other judge confirms sonnet's reduction, and
haiku's is 26.7 points under it against at most 10.7 under any other.

\paragraph{Truncated generations and a same-day replicate.}
The caps of Figure~\ref{fig:judges} cut many responses mid-generation, which
full scoring cannot undo, most often Mistral's CoT (126 of 142), and nano's
budget, which included its reasoning, left 34 of its 150 NoT responses empty
(Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).
We regenerated both prompts on all seven generators in one run at a
4,096-token cap (8,192 for nano), which at most two responses per generator
and prompt reach, and scored them with all five judges
(Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}).
Under the production judge the reduction holds on all seven, from $-12.7$
$[-22.0, -3.3]$ points on sonnet to $-63.4$ $[-71.8, -54.9]$ on Llama (16 to 80
percent of the CoT rate), so truncation did not manufacture it. Under the
gpt-4o judge it holds on six, though it shrinks on Mistral ($-41.0$ to
$-29.5$) and DeepSeek ($-29.1$ to $-16.3$), and the nano judge finds it on
five.
On sonnet it reverses under all four other judges, by $+8.0$ $[+1.3, +15.3]$
points under gpt-4o and by 8.7 to 12.1 under the rest, each interval
excluding zero. Haiku's reduction reverses under the grok judge too
($+12.1$ $[+4.7, +19.5]$), as it did on the original responses, and the
Llama and grok judges each find the reduction on Llama alone. The registered
rule, which asks for the production judge and three of the other four, reads
the reduction as holding on Llama alone and as judge-dependent on the other
six.

\paragraph{What carries the reduction.}
Four further prompts, run on haiku, grok, nano and Llama (the length prompt
on haiku and grok only) beside same-run replicates of CoT and NoT at equal
caps and scored by all five judges, separate the scaffold's content from its
form, persona line and length (Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
A checklist of the same five elements, in the third person and not as a
story, lowers validation by 36.5 to 57.5 points across the five judges,
against 5.9 to 46.1 for NoT in the same run, and more than NoT under every
judge (NoT minus checklist $+11.4$ $[+7.3, +15.3]$ under the production judge
to $+31.4$ $[+27.2, +35.5]$ under the Llama judge).
The first-person narrative instruction without the content sections raises
validation under every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points ($+12.1$ $[+8.5, +16.0]$
under the production judge), and both results hold under all five judges.
Because the rubric scores emotional validation, a third-person checklist may
be judged less validating partly for a cooler register and not only for
endorsing the asker less, and this design cannot separate the two, nor voice
from narrative genre.
The prompts' personas (``helpful assistant'' for CoT, ``thoughtful advisor''
for NoT) do not carry it, since CoT with NoT's persona lowers validation
under no judge ($-0.9$ $[-4.3, +2.4]$ under the production judge) and raises
it under two ($+3.3$ and $+5.5$ under gpt-4o and nano).
Nor does length, on the two generators tested. CoT asked for NoT's mean
length reaches it (1.04 and 1.19 times NoT's length on haiku and grok), yet
under the production judge NoT validates 54.0 $[45.3, 62.7]$ points less than
it on haiku and 46.5 $[38.0, 54.9]$ less on grok, a result that holds under
four judges and is unresolved under the Llama judge.

Earlier tests on haiku, grok and nano, scored by the production judge alone
against the original, partly truncated responses, agree
(Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
Removing any one of the stakeholder, consequence or uncertainty sections
takes back 10 to 18 percent of the reduction pooled, no interval lying wholly
above a quarter, though each removal takes back 42 to 55 percent on haiku,
the generator that is also the judge, and none a detectable share on grok or
nano.
Replacing CoT's step-by-step instruction with one sentence asking the model to
consider everyone affected raises validation,
pooled, by $+6.1$ $[+2.1, +10.2]$ points, significantly only on haiku
($+10.0$ $[+2.0, +18.0]$), again the judge's own generator. No registered
reading anticipated a rise, the rule applied literally reads it as showing
that the other sections are needed, and the label reversal was added after
the run.

\paragraph{What the scaffold does not do.}
In a judge-free test on the crowd-labelled conflict accounts of
Section~\ref{sec-collective}, posed with the user as the writer, a second turn
either disputes the verdict or, as a neutral re-ask, only asks for it again,
and capitulation is a change to not at fault on items that both prompts first
judged at fault (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}).
Pooled over nano, Llama and grok, NoT capitulates more, 0.37 against 0.22
($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$ points), also where the crowd found the user at
fault so that the pushback is wrong ($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), but it also
changes its verdict more on the neutral re-ask (0.228 against 0.125), and net
of that the registered primary difference, $+5.2$ $[-1.7, +12.2]$, is null.
Llama carries the raw effect (0.81 against 0.49), while nano (0.10 against
0.05) and grok (0.05 against 0.01, below the registered 5 percent floor on
its own) rarely capitulate under either prompt, so the test is informative
mainly on Llama. Haiku is unread because 14.1 percent of its NoT replies to
pushback had no parseable verdict, above the registered 5 percent guard, and
it is left out of the pool under a rule fixed after its failure was seen and
before any grok or Llama reply existed. Whatever the reduction above
reflects, it does not extend to holding a verdict against a user's objection.
On the full panel of these accounts, posed in the third person and with the
user as the writer, NoT does not remove the verdict's shift toward the user
on Llama or Mistral, where under the registered coding of shared blame it
appears under both prompts. On Mistral it is larger under NoT (its decision
criterion moves by $+0.30$ against $+0.13$), and under both alternative codings it
appears only under NoT. DeepSeek shows none to remove. The comparator in this
test is the panel's unscaffolded prompt, which limits reasoning to four
sentences, not the CoT prompt of Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}, so the contrast
also lifts that limit.
In an earlier run, read under a rule registered afterwards, nano, forced to
call false theorem statements from the BrokenMath benchmark
\citep{petrov2025brokenmath} true or false, affirms them more under NoT,
0.223 against 0.157 of 600 attempts per prompt, $+6.7$ $[+3.3, +10.3]$ points
and $+7.0$ $[+3.0, +11.5]$ among committed answers, but bounds that assign
each prompt's uncommitted answers (35 percent under CoT, 28 under NoT) in
opposite directions, $[-27.8, +34.5]$, cross zero.
Narrating again with its first narration in view, nano withdraws
affirmations, and in an older test on BrokenMath proofs grok moved the
opposite way, so this is a first-pass effect shown on nano alone
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The single-agent evidence therefore supports not a general reduction in
sycophancy but a judge-dependent reduction in the judged validation of a
first answer, most robust on Llama and, with the register caveat above,
carried by the structured content as a whole rather than its narrative
packaging, persona, length (tested on two generators) or any single section
(tested under one judge).
