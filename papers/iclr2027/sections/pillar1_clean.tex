\section{Single-Agent Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec-single}

Under our production judge NoT lowers the validation rate on all seven
generators, by 13 to 63 points once both prompts are regenerated at a token
cap almost no response reaches. All five judges find the reduction only on
Llama, and on sonnet the other four reverse it in that regeneration.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The items are a random 150 of the 3,027 open-ended requests for personal
advice about the asker's own situation in ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant}.
One response per item and prompt comes from each of seven generators from
six developers, haiku, sonnet, gpt-5.4-nano (nano) and
grok \citep{anthropic_haiku45,anthropic_sonnet46,openai_gpt54nano,xai_grok41}
and the open-weight Llama, Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro
(DeepSeek) \citep{meta_llama33,mistral_large3,deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report},
under NoT and CoT (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}).
Five judges, haiku (the production judge), gpt-4o (the only judge not also a
generator) \citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard} and the nano, Llama
and grok judges, read each question with the whole response, its written
reasoning included, and score it 1 if emotionally validating and 0 if not
under ELEPHANT's rubric, used verbatim (Appendix~\ref{app-prompts}), which
asks whether it shows understanding of the asker's feelings, respect for their
perspective and care.
The drop is NoT's rate minus CoT's in points on the 116 to 150 items per
generator and judge scored under both prompts, and a reduction holds under a
judge when its 95 percent item-clustered interval, the middle 95 percent of
8,000 drops on resampled items with judge and generation run fixed, lies
below zero.
Every test here but two older ones on false mathematical statements, one
below, was registered with its decision rule before analysis in our own
append-only log, not a public registry, as was every generation after the
log opened, but the June 2026 original responses of haiku, sonnet, nano and
grok predate it, so only their scoring is registered
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
No registered rule corrects for multiple comparisons, so a Holm step-down
over the seven generators per judge, added after the tables were seen, shows
which results survive (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}), and pooled, pushback and
collective results stay uncorrected.

\paragraph{The original responses under five judges.}
On the original, partly truncated responses the production judge finds
the reduction on every generator, gpt-4o on six, the nano judge on five and
the Llama and grok judges on one and two, those two reversing it on three
generators (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}).
The registered rule, six of seven generators under every judge, set after a
withdrawn comparison failed a stricter every-generator rule, reads this as
judge-dependent, as does the stricter rule (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The Llama and grok judges rate most answers
validating whatever the prompt, and a registered screen for such judges,
removed before scoring, would have excluded the Llama judge
(Appendix~\ref{app-judges}), but a ceiling cannot produce a rise whose
interval excludes zero, so their reversals on sonnet, and on haiku under the
grok judge, are rises in the judged rate, not hidden reductions.
The production judge, itself the generator haiku and an Anthropic model like
sonnet, alone finds sonnet's reduction and puts haiku's at 26.7 points,
others at most 10.7.

\paragraph{The regeneration without truncation.}
The original caps, 1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT, cut many
responses (Mistral's CoT 126 of 142) and left 34 of nano's 150 NoT responses
empty (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).
We therefore regenerated both prompts on all seven in one run at an equal
4,096-token cap (8,192 for nano), which almost no response reaches, and, as
registered, take the headline numbers from it (Figure~\ref{fig:judges}a).
Under the production judge the reduction holds on all seven, from $-12.7$
points on sonnet to $-63.4$ on Llama (a relative reduction of 16 to 80
percent), so truncation did not manufacture it. The gpt-4o judge finds it on
six and the nano judge on five, while
on sonnet all four other judges reverse it, by 8.0 to 12.1 points, each
interval excluding zero, all but the nano judge's after the Holm
correction, as they do in every run by point estimate and pooled over runs
($+6.2$ to $+12.0$, intervals above zero).
Haiku's reduction
reverses under the grok judge ($+12.1$) and by this run's
registered rule, a reduction under the production judge and at least three
of the other four, it holds across judges on Llama alone and is
judge-dependent on the other six.
A second such run on sonnet and haiku, whose reductions were the smallest,
registered before its calls but after the first was read, revises no
conclusion above as registered.
Pooled after the fact (Table~\ref{tab-app-runs}), the production judge's
drop is $-27.0$ $[-33.5, -20.3]$ on haiku and $-12.2$
$[-18.0, -6.7]$ on sonnet, but $-4.7$ $[-13.3, +3.3]$ in that run
alone, which the rule would read as failing.

\paragraph{What carries the reduction.}
NoT differs from CoT in its sections, narrative form, persona line (``thoughtful
advisor'' for ``helpful assistant'') and length, so four further prompts each
target one, on haiku, grok, nano and Llama, length on the first two, against
CoT and NoT regenerated in the same run at 2,048 tokens (8,192 for nano), a
cap few reached, so no contrast changes date or cap
(Table~\ref{tab-form-main}, Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
A third-person checklist of NoT's five sections under its persona line
lowers validation more than NoT under every judge, by 11.4 to 31.4 points
(each interval above zero).
As the rubric scores emotional validation, the checklist may be judged
less validating partly for a cooler tone, or register, not only for
endorsing the asker less, which this rubric cannot separate, nor this design
voice from genre.
First-person narration under NoT's persona line without the sections raises
validation above CoT under every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points.
The persona line does not carry it, as CoT with only that line
swapped in lowers validation under no judge and raises it under two.
Nor does length, as NoT validates less under four judges than CoT asked for
NoT's mean length in words, at 1.04 and 1.19 times NoT's characters on
haiku and grok, on haiku half or more because the request raises validation
above CoT, the Llama judge's interval on grok including zero.
Earlier tests against the original responses, scored by the production
judge alone, credit no single section, and a request to consider everyone
affected in place of CoT's step-by-step sentence raises validation by
$+6.1$ $[+2.1, +10.2]$ points, which no registered outcome predicted
(Appendix~\ref{app-form}).

\paragraph{Accepting the asker's framing.}
A second ELEPHANT rubric asks whether an answer accepts the asker's framing
or challenges its premise, and the production judge and gpt-4o scored it on
the same responses in a test registered after the validation results.
NoT lowers premise acceptance, the share accepting the framing, on all seven
generators under both judges, with pooled drops of $-24.5$
$[-28.4, -20.6]$ and $-30.9$ $[-35.1, -27.0]$ points
(Figure~\ref{fig:judges}b, Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}, Appendix~\ref{app-framing}).
Pooled over the four generators of Table~\ref{tab-form-main}, the checklist
and narration alone each lower it by 0.72 to 0.86 of NoT's drop,
every interval above one half, and NoT by 4.1 to 8.1 points more than either,
every interval excluding zero, so content, not form, carries the
reduction only in judged validation.
ELEPHANT's instruction not to be emotionally validating lowers validation
more than NoT under all five judges, and a sceptical persona of ours, CoT
with its persona line replaced by a sceptical advisor, under four, but
that instruction, ELEPHANT's instruction to validate only where
appropriate and its third-person rewrite of the question, whose answers
concern a third party, leave premise acceptance at or above CoT's, while
the sceptical persona cuts it by 57.5 and 56.0 points, 34.1 and 27.6 more
than NoT.
All four ran one to two days after CoT, and ELEPHANT's instruction to
challenge the premise was not run (Table~\ref{tab-app-baselines}).
In a descriptive comparison registered after these results,
against the 149 human answers to our items that serve as ELEPHANT's
reference, scored by our judges, CoT is above the human rate on both
rubrics under every judge and NoT on validation under four judges, but NoT
is 9.0 points below it under the production judge and 9.7 and 11.1 below
on premise acceptance, where the sceptical persona is 43.7 and 39.5
below (Table~\ref{tab-app-human-anchor}).
Human answers are far shorter than model answers, and the human rate as
target is the benchmark's premise, not our result.

\paragraph{What the scaffold does not do.}
A register-blind, judge-free pushback test on the crowd-labelled Scruples
accounts of Section~\ref{sec-collective}, posed as the user's own, finds that
NoT gives up
a disputed verdict more often than CoT, and also when merely asked to
reconsider.
Where both prompts first blamed the user, capitulation is the share of
verdicts no longer blaming the user after a pushback and a request to
reconsider, instability that share after the request alone, and the
registered primary, capitulation net of instability, what the disagreement
adds (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}).
Haiku is left out of the pool by a rule fixed after its results were seen, 14.1
percent of its NoT pushback replies lacking a verdict against a registered 5
percent limit.
Pooled over nano, Llama and grok, NoT capitulates more, 0.371 against 0.216
($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$
points), also where the crowd found the user at fault so that the pushback is
wrong ($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), and is less stable, 0.228 against 0.125
($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$). The net primary is null, $+5.2$ $[-1.7, +12.2]$,
because the rises are alike, not because NoT holds firm.
Llama, the one generator whose reduction every judge confirms, carries both
rises, so where NoT most lowers validation it also most readily
gives way, which fits a less committed answer as well as a less sycophantic
one. Nano and grok rarely capitulate under either prompt (at
most 0.10 of verdicts).
A second test on the same accounts, against the brief unscaffolded prompt
of Section~\ref{sec-collective}, finds that NoT does not remove the verdict's
shift toward the user, blaming the writer less when the writer is the user
than in the third person, on Llama or Mistral, DeepSeek showing none to
remove, and on Mistral it is larger under NoT and, under two alternative
codings, present only under NoT (Table~\ref{tab-app-z3},
Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
In an older test on nano alone, read under a rule registered afterwards, NoT
also affirms more false theorem statements from BrokenMath
\citep{petrov2025brokenmath}, by $+6.7$ $[+3.3, +10.3]$ points, though a
second NoT pass largely withdraws it and its worst-case bounds over answers
giving no verdict cross zero (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The single-agent evidence thus supports not a general reduction in
sycophancy but, in a first answer, less acceptance of the asker's framing on
every generator under both judges scoring it, pooled over four to below the
human rate, and a judge-dependent drop in judged validation, smaller than a
one-line instruction not to validate gives, carried, with the register
caveat, by structured content rather than narrative form, persona, length
(tested on two generators) or any single section (tested under one judge).
