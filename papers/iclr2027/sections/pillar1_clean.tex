\section{Single-Agent Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec-single}

Under our production judge, haiku, NoT lowers the rate of answers scored as
emotionally validating on all seven generators, by 13 to 63 percentage points
once both prompts are regenerated at a token cap that almost no response
reaches, in place of original responses that lower caps often cut short.
All five judges agree only on Llama, and on claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet) no
other judge confirms the reduction, and in that regeneration each of the
other four scores NoT as more validating than CoT.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The items are a random 150 of the 3,027 open-ended requests for personal
advice about the asker's own situation in ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant}.
A judge model reads each
question with the whole response, its written reasoning included, and scores
it under ELEPHANT's binary emotional-validation rubric, used verbatim, which
asks whether it shows understanding of the asker's feelings, respect for their
perspective and care.
One response per item and prompt comes from each of seven generators from
six developers, haiku, sonnet, gpt-5.4-nano (nano) and
grok \citep{anthropic_haiku45,anthropic_sonnet46,openai_gpt54nano,xai_grok41}
and the open-weight Llama, Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro
(DeepSeek) \citep{meta_llama33,mistral_large3,deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report},
under NoT and CoT (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}).
Five judges score every response, haiku (the production judge),
gpt-4o (the only judge not also a generator)
\citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard}, nano, Llama and grok.
The outcome, the drop, is NoT's validation rate minus CoT's in points on the
items the judge scored under both prompts, with a 95 percent item-clustered
bootstrap interval (8,000 resamples of items, judge and generation run
fixed), and the reduction holds under a judge when the drop is negative and
its interval excludes zero.
Every test here but two older ones on false mathematical statements was
registered with its decision rule in our append-only log before analysis, and
every generation after the log opened was registered before it ran. The June
2026 original responses of haiku, sonnet, nano and grok predate the log, so
only their scoring is registered (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The registered rules make no multiple-comparison correction, so a Holm
step-down over the seven generators within each judge, added after the tables
were seen, shows which results survive (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).

\paragraph{The original responses under five judges.}
On the original, partly truncated responses (Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, right)
only the production judge finds the reduction on every generator, by 19.3
points on sonnet to 70.2 on Llama.
The gpt-4o judge finds it on six generators, all but sonnet, the nano judge
on five, missing haiku and sonnet, the Llama judge on Llama alone
and the grok judge on Llama and, marginally, DeepSeek. The Llama judge
reverses the sign on nano and, marginally, on sonnet, and the grok judge on
haiku and sonnet (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}). The Holm correction keeps
the production judge's seven reductions and drops those two marginal
results and, on these responses only, haiku and nano under gpt-4o.
The registered rule, a reduction on at least six of seven generators under
every judge, reads this as judge-dependent. So does the stricter
every-generator rule of a withdrawn earlier comparison, which a generator had
failed before the looser rule was written (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}). The Llama and grok judges rate most model
answers as validating whatever the prompt (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}).
A rate near that ceiling can compress a difference toward zero but cannot
produce a rise whose interval excludes zero, so their reversals, on sonnet in
the original and regenerated responses and on haiku under the grok judge in
both, are rises in the judged rate and not reductions the ceiling hides.
A check registered after the judges' tables were seen tests each judge
against ELEPHANT's labels on 149 human-written answers to the same
questions, and Cohen's kappa \citep{cohen60}, a chance-corrected agreement,
ranks gpt-4o highest at 0.79 and the Llama judge lowest at 0.48, the only
pair of judges whose intervals do not overlap.
We do not rest the result on this ranking, since those answers validate far
less often, 30 percent by reference, than CoT's answers under any judge, 54
to 97 percent, so the check does not reach the high rates at which the Llama
and grok judges diverge, and ELEPHANT does not state how its labels were
produced (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).
The production judge is itself a generator, haiku, and shares its developer,
Anthropic, with sonnet. No other judge confirms sonnet's reduction, and
haiku's is 26.7 points under that judge against at most 10.7 under any other.

\paragraph{The regeneration without truncation.}
The original caps, 1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT, cut many
responses, Mistral's CoT most often (126 of 142), and nano, whose hidden
reasoning counted against its 2,048 tokens, left 34 of its 150 NoT
responses empty (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).
Judging a cut response in full cannot restore its lost ending, so we
regenerated both prompts on all seven generators in one run at an equal cap of
4,096 tokens (8,192 for nano), which at most two responses per generator and
prompt reach, and, as registered, take the headline numbers from it
(Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, left).
Under the production judge the reduction holds on all seven, from $-12.7$
points on sonnet to $-63.4$ on Llama (a relative reduction of 16 to 80
percent), so truncation did not manufacture it. The gpt-4o judge finds it on
six, smaller than before on Mistral and DeepSeek, and the nano judge on five.
On sonnet all four other judges reverse it, by 8.0 to 12.1 points, each
interval excluding zero, all but the nano judge's also after the Holm
correction, which changes no judge's count of reductions. Haiku's reduction
reverses under the grok judge too ($+12.1$), as before, and the Llama and grok
judges each find it on Llama alone, so by the registered rule, a reduction
under the production judge and at least three of the other four, it holds
across judges on Llama alone and is judge-dependent on the other six.
A second such run on sonnet and haiku, whose reductions were the smallest,
registered before its calls but after the first was read, repeats the four
rises on sonnet ($+7.3$ to $+10.1$, the nano judge's interval reaching zero)
and puts sonnet's production-judge drop at $-4.7$ $[-13.3, +3.3]$, the one
run where that judge's interval includes zero, which the rule would read as
failing, though as registered this run revises none of the first run's
readings (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k} pools the runs after the fact).

\paragraph{What carries the reduction.}
NoT differs from CoT in its sections, story form, persona line (``thoughtful
advisor'' for ``helpful assistant'') and length, so four further prompts
(Figure~\ref{fig-app-prompts}) each target one of these, on haiku, grok, nano
and Llama, against CoT and NoT regenerated in the same run at 2,048 tokens,
8,192 for nano, so that no contrast also changes date or cap
(Table~\ref{tab-form-main}, Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
A third-person checklist of the same five sections under NoT's persona line
lowers validation more than NoT under every judge, by 11.4 to 31.4 points
(every interval of that difference above zero).
Because the rubric scores emotional validation, the checklist may be judged
less validating partly for a cooler tone, or register, and not only for
endorsing the asker less, and this design cannot separate the two, nor voice from narrative
genre.
The first-person narrative instruction without the sections raises
validation above CoT under every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points.
NoT's persona line does not carry the reduction, since CoT
with only that line swapped in lowers validation under no judge and raises
it under two (gpt-4o and nano).
Nor does length, where CoT asked for NoT's mean length in words reaches it
(1.04 and 1.19 times in characters on haiku and grok) yet NoT validates less
than it under four judges, on haiku partly because the request raises
validation above CoT, the Llama judge's interval on grok including zero.
Earlier tests on haiku, grok and nano, scored by the production judge alone
against the original, partly truncated responses, find no single section
carrying the reduction. Removing the stakeholder, consequence or uncertainty
section takes back no detectable share of NoT's drop on grok or nano and 42
to 55 percent on haiku, the judge's own generator, each pooled interval
including a quarter, the registered bar for crediting a section. One sentence
asking the model to consider everyone affected raises validation above CoT,
pooled, by $+6.1$ $[+2.1, +10.2]$ points, a rise no registered outcome
anticipated (Appendix~\ref{app-form}).

\paragraph{What the scaffold does not do.}
A judge-free test, reading each reply's required verdict label, asks whether
NoT keeps a verdict the user disputes, on the high-consensus Scruples accounts
\citep{lourie2021scruples} of Section~\ref{sec-collective} posed as the
user's own. The same first answer is followed by a pushback (``I really don't
think I was in the wrong here'' before a request to reconsider) or, as a
neutral re-ask, by the request alone (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}). Where both
prompts first blamed the user, capitulation is the share of verdicts moving to
blame only the other party or no one after the pushback, instability the same
share after the re-ask, and the registered primary, capitulation net of
instability, isolates what the disagreement adds to a bare request.
Pooled over nano, Llama and grok, NoT is worse on both raw measures,
capitulating more, NoT 0.37 against CoT 0.22 ($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$
points), also where the crowd found the user at fault so that the pushback is
wrong ($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), and less stable, 0.228 against 0.125
($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$). The net primary is null, $+5.2$ $[-1.7, +12.2]$
with a half-width of about 7 points, because the rises are alike, not because
NoT holds firm.
Llama carries both rises and is the one generator whose reduction every
judge confirms, so where NoT most lowers validation it also most readily
gives way, which fits a less committed answer as well as a less sycophantic
one. Nano and grok rarely capitulate under either prompt (at
most 0.10 of verdicts), and haiku is excluded for too many replies without a
verdict.
A second test on the same accounts asks whether NoT removes the verdict's
shift toward the user, blaming the writer less when the writer is the user
than in the third person, measured as the rise in the signal-detection
criterion $c$, the model's threshold for calling the writer at fault
(Table~\ref{tab-app-z3}). Reusing answers from Section~\ref{sec-collective},
it compares NoT with the brief unscaffolded prompt there, not with CoT.
NoT does not remove the shift on
Llama or Mistral, where under the registered coding, which counts blame on
both parties as at fault, it appears under both prompts, on Mistral larger
under NoT ($+0.30$ against $+0.13$) and, under the two alternative codings,
which leave that verdict out or count it as not at fault, only under NoT.
DeepSeek shows none to remove (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
In an older test on nano alone, read under a rule registered afterwards, NoT
also affirms more false theorem statements from the BrokenMath benchmark
\citep{petrov2025brokenmath}, by $+6.7$ $[+3.3, +10.3]$ points, an effect
that a second narration largely withdraws and whose worst-case bounds over
noncommittal answers cross zero
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The single-agent evidence therefore supports not a general reduction in
sycophancy but a judge-dependent reduction in the judged validation of a
first answer, most robust on Llama and, with the register caveat above,
carried by the structured content as a whole rather than its narrative
form, persona, length (tested on two generators) or any single section
(tested under one judge).
