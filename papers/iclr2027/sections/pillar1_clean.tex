\section{Single-Agent Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec-single}

Under our production judge, haiku, NoT lowers the rate of answers scored as
emotionally validating on all seven generators, by 13 to 63 percentage points
when both prompts are regenerated at a length cap almost none reach, but all
five judges agree only on Llama, and on claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet) no other
judge confirms the reduction, which all four reverse in that regeneration.

\paragraph{Setup.}
The items are 150 open-ended requests for personal advice about the asker's
own situation from ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant}. A judge model reads each
question with the whole response, its written reasoning included, and scores
it under ELEPHANT's binary emotional-validation rubric, used verbatim, which
asks whether it shows understanding of the asker's feelings, respect for their
perspective and care.
One response per item and prompt comes from each of seven generators from
six developers, haiku, sonnet, gpt-5.4-nano (nano) and
grok \citep{anthropic_haiku45,anthropic_sonnet46,openai_gpt54nano,xai_grok41}
and the open-weight Llama, Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro
(DeepSeek) \citep{meta_llama33,mistral_large3,deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report},
under NoT and CoT (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}).
Five judges score every response this way, haiku (the production judge),
gpt-4o (the only judge not also a generator)
\citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard}, nano, Llama and grok.
The outcome, the drop, is NoT's validation rate minus CoT's in points on the
items the judge scored under both prompts, with a 95 percent item-clustered
bootstrap interval (8,000 resamples of items), and the reduction holds under a judge when
the drop is negative and its interval excludes zero.
The registered rules make no multiple-comparison correction, so a Holm
step-down over the seven generators within each judge, added after the tables
were seen, shows which readings survive (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).
Every test here but two older ones on false mathematical statements, one
below, was registered with its decision rule in our append-only log before
analysis, as was, before it ran, every generation after the log
opened, but the June 2026 original responses of haiku, sonnet, nano
and grok predate the log, so only their scoring is registered
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).

\paragraph{The original responses under five judges.}
On the original responses (Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, right) only the
production judge finds the reduction on every generator, by 19.3 points on
sonnet to 70.2 on Llama.
The gpt-4o judge finds it on six generators, all but sonnet, the nano judge
on five, missing haiku and sonnet, the Llama judge on Llama alone
and the grok judge on Llama and, marginally, DeepSeek. The Llama judge
reverses the sign on nano and, marginally, on sonnet, and the grok judge on
haiku and sonnet (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}). The Holm correction keeps
the production judge's seven reductions and drops those two marginal
readings and, on these original responses only, haiku and nano under gpt-4o.
The registered rule, a reduction on at least six of seven generators under
every judge, reads this as judge-dependent, as does the
stricter every-generator rule of a withdrawn earlier comparison
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}). The Llama and grok judges, which see little or
no reduction, rate most model answers as validating whatever the prompt
(Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}), but a rate near its ceiling can compress a
difference toward zero and cannot produce a rise whose interval excludes
zero, so their reversals, on sonnet in the original and regenerated responses
and on haiku under the grok judge in both, are rises in the judged rate and
not reductions the ceiling hides.
In a check registered after the five judges' tables were seen, each judge
scored the 149 labelled human-written answers ELEPHANT gives to the same
questions, and Cohen's kappa \citep{cohen60}, a chance-corrected agreement,
with those labels ranks gpt-4o highest at 0.79 and Llama lowest at 0.48, the
only pair of judges whose intervals do not overlap.
We do not rest the result on this ranking, since those answers validate far
less often, 30 percent by reference, than model answers under any judge, 54
to 97 percent under CoT, so the check does not reach the region where the
Llama and grok judges diverge, and ELEPHANT does not state how
their labels were produced (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}).
The production judge is itself a generator, haiku, and shares its developer,
Anthropic, with sonnet. No other judge confirms sonnet's reduction, and
haiku's is 26.7 points under that judge against at most 10.7 under any other.

\paragraph{The regeneration without truncation.}
The original caps, 1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT, cut many
responses, Mistral's CoT most often (126 of 142), and nano, whose hidden
reasoning counted against its 2,048 tokens, left 34 of its 150 NoT
responses empty (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).
Scoring cut-off text in full cannot undo truncation, so we regenerated both
prompts on all seven generators in one run at 4,096 tokens (8,192 for nano),
which at most two responses per generator and prompt reach, and, as
registered, restate the headline from it (Figure~\ref{fig:judges}, left).
Under the production judge the reduction holds on all seven, from $-12.7$
points on sonnet to $-63.4$ on Llama (a relative reduction of 16 to 80
percent), so truncation did not manufacture it. Under the gpt-4o judge it holds on six,
though smaller than before on Mistral and DeepSeek, and the nano judge finds
it on five.
On sonnet all four other judges reverse it, by 8.0 to 12.1 points, each
interval excluding zero before correction and all but the nano judge's
($+8.7$ $[+0.7, +16.7]$) after it, and the correction changes no judge's
count of reductions. Haiku's reduction reverses under the grok judge too
($+12.1$), as before, and the Llama and grok judges each
find it on Llama alone, so by this
regeneration's registered rule, a reduction under the production judge and
at least three of the other four, it holds across judges on Llama alone and
is judge-dependent on the other six.
A second such run on sonnet and haiku, registered before its calls but after
the first was read, repeats the four rises on sonnet ($+7.3$ to $+10.1$,
the nano judge's interval reaching zero) and puts sonnet's production-judge
drop at $-4.7$ $[-13.3, +3.3]$, the one run where that judge's interval
includes zero. Pooled after the fact over sonnet's three runs, the original
responses included, its drop is $-12.2$ $[-18.0, -6.7]$ (haiku's in
Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}).

\paragraph{What carries the reduction.}
NoT differs from CoT in its sections, story form, persona line and length, so
four further prompts (Figure~\ref{fig-app-prompts}) each change one of these,
on haiku, grok, nano and Llama (the length prompt on haiku and grok only),
against CoT and NoT regenerated in the same run at 2,048 tokens, 8,192 for
nano (Table~\ref{tab-form-main}, Appendix~\ref{app-form}).
A third-person checklist of the same five elements under NoT's persona line
lowers validation more than NoT under every judge, by 11.4 to 31.4 points (every
interval above zero).
Because the rubric scores emotional validation, the checklist may be judged
less validating partly for a cooler tone, or register, and not only for
endorsing the asker less, and this design cannot separate the two, nor voice from narrative
genre.
The first-person narrative instruction without the sections raises
validation above CoT under every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points.
NoT's persona line (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}) does not carry the reduction, since CoT
with only that line swapped in lowers validation under no judge and raises
it under two (gpt-4o and nano).
Nor does length, where CoT asked for NoT's mean length in words reaches it
(1.04 and 1.19 times on haiku and grok) yet NoT still validates far less than
it under four judges, on haiku partly because the request raises
validation, the Llama judge's interval on grok including zero.
Earlier tests on haiku, grok and nano, scored by the production judge alone
against the original, partly truncated responses, find no single element
carrying the reduction. Removing the stakeholder, consequence or uncertainty
section takes back no detectable share of NoT's drop on grok or nano and 42
to 55 percent on haiku, the judge's own generator, each pooled interval
including the registered bar of a quarter, and one sentence asking the
model to consider everyone affected raises validation, pooled, by $+6.1$
$[+2.1, +10.2]$ points, a rise no registered reading anticipated
(Appendix~\ref{app-form}).

\paragraph{What the scaffold does not do.}
A judge-free test asks whether NoT keeps a verdict the user disputes, on the
crowd-labelled Scruples conflict accounts \citep{lourie2021scruples} of
Section~\ref{sec-collective} posed as the user's own. After the same first
verdict comes either a pushback (``I really don't think I was in the wrong
here'' before a request to reconsider) or, as a neutral re-ask, the request
alone (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}), and capitulation is a change from
blaming the user to blaming only the other party or no one, on items where
both prompts first blamed the user.
Pooled over nano, Llama and grok, NoT is worse on both raw measures,
capitulating more, NoT 0.37 against CoT 0.22 ($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$
points), also where the crowd found the user at fault so that the pushback is
wrong ($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), and showing more instability, the same
change after the re-ask, 0.228 against 0.125 ($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$).
The registered primary, capitulation net of that instability, which
isolates the disagreement's effect, is null, $+5.2$ $[-1.7, +12.2]$ with a
half-width of about 7 points, because the rises are alike, not because NoT
holds firm.
Llama carries both rises and is the one generator whose reduction every judge confirms, so
where NoT most lowers validation it also most readily gives way, which fits
a less committed answer as well as a less sycophantic one, while nano and
grok rarely capitulate under either prompt (at most 0.10 of verdicts) and
haiku is excluded for too many replies without a verdict
(Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}).
On all of these accounts, the verdict's shift toward the user,
blaming the writer less when the writer is the user than in the third
person, is the rise in the signal-detection criterion $c$, the model's
threshold for calling the writer at fault (Table~\ref{tab-app-z3}). Against a
brief unscaffolded prompt rather than CoT, NoT does not
remove it on Llama or Mistral, where under the registered coding, which
counts blame on both parties as at fault (Section~\ref{sec-collective}), it
appears under both prompts,
on Mistral larger under NoT ($+0.30$ against $+0.13$) and under the two
alternative codings, which leave that verdict out or count it as not at
fault, only under NoT, while DeepSeek shows none to remove
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
In an older test on nano alone, read under a rule registered afterwards, NoT
also affirms more false theorem statements from the BrokenMath benchmark
\citep{petrov2025brokenmath}, by $+6.7$ $[+3.3, +10.3]$ points, an effect
that a second narration largely withdraws and whose worst-case bounds over
noncommittal answers cross zero
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The single-agent evidence therefore supports not a general reduction in
sycophancy but a judge-dependent reduction in the judged validation of a
first answer, most robust on Llama and, with the register caveat above,
carried by the structured content as a whole rather than its narrative
form, persona, length (tested on two generators) or any single section
(tested under one judge).
