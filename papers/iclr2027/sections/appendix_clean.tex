\section{Appendix}
\label{app}

This appendix gives the full evidence behind the single-agent validation and
pushback results, the narrated-versus-plain-seat comparison of the collective
and the collective's dissent mechanism and routing against the solo model, and
records every correction and deviation from the pre-registration.
Model names are shortened after first use. claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku)
\citep{anthropic_haiku45} and claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet)
\citep{anthropic_sonnet46} are Anthropic models, gpt-5.4-nano (nano)
\citep{openai_gpt54nano} and gpt-4o are OpenAI models, grok-4-1-fast-reasoning
(grok) \citep{xai_grok41} is an xAI model, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama),
Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) and DeepSeek-V4-Pro (DeepSeek) are open-weight
models from Meta, Mistral AI and DeepSeek (Table~\ref{tab-app-models}).
\todo{PI: references.bib has no entries for gpt-4o, Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Mistral-Large-3-2 or DeepSeek-V4-Pro, so those four models are uncited.}
NoT is the Narration-of-Thought system prompt and CoT the one-line standard
chain-of-thought prompt (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}). The single-agent outcome is
the validation rate, the share of responses a judge model scores as
emotionally validating under the rubric of the ELEPHANT benchmark
\citep{cheng25elephant}, which asks whether a response shows understanding,
validates the person's perspective and expresses care. The items are 150 of
the benchmark's open-ended personal-advice questions, drawn with seed 44.
A drop is the NoT rate minus the CoT rate in percentage points, so a negative
drop means NoT validates less. Intervals are 95 percent percentile bootstraps
that resample items and keep every response to an item together, with 8,000
draws unless stated.

\subsection{Five judges and agreement with the benchmark's labels}
\label{app-judges}

The reduction depends on the judge. When the full generated text of every one
of the 2,004 non-empty CoT and NoT responses is scored by each of five judges,
NoT lowers
validation with an interval excluding zero on all seven generators under the
production judge (haiku), on six under gpt-4o, five under nano, one under the
Llama judge and two under the grok judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}). The pre-declared
reading is therefore JUDGE-DEPENDENT, the registered label for anything short
of a negative drop excluding zero on at least six of seven generators under
every judge, and only Llama's reduction holds under all five judges.
Under nano the interval includes zero on haiku
($-5.3$) and sonnet ($+2.0$), and under gpt-4o it includes zero on sonnet
($+3.3$ $[-4.7, +11.3]$). The Llama judge finds a reduction only on Llama
($-11.3$ $[-19.1, -3.5]$) and a rise on sonnet ($+7.4$ $[+2.0, +13.4]$) and
nano ($+13.8$ $[+7.8, +20.7]$). The grok judge finds a reduction on Llama
($-49.6$) and DeepSeek ($-5.0$ $[-10.0, -0.7]$) and a rise on haiku ($+11.4$
$[+5.4, +18.1]$) and sonnet ($+14.0$ $[+8.7, +20.0]$).

Each judge scored each response through the same full-text path, so the
scores differ only in which model judges them, and each contrast is paired on
the items with
a response in both conditions, 116 to 150 per generator
(Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}), with bootstrap seed 20260822.
Unparsed judge outputs were 0.0 percent for haiku, gpt-4o and nano and 0.3
percent for the Llama and grok judges, and one Llama-judge call was lost to a
rate limit.
Scored this way, the production judge reproduces the registered drops
closely, haiku $-26.7$ exactly, grok $-35.2$ against $-36.0$, Llama $-70.2$
against $-69.9$, Mistral $-57.6$ against $-58.2$, DeepSeek $-46.1$ against
$-45.5$, nano $-37.9$ on its 116 paired items against $-41.4$ unpaired, and
sonnet $-19.3$ against $-22.3$. Apart from nano, whose gap reflects the
pairing, sonnet's 3.0 points is the largest difference.

The two judges that find little or no reduction are the two that rate most
responses validating. Of the fourteen generator-by-prompt validation rates,
each over all responses scored in its condition, 11 (79 percent) exceed 0.85
under the Llama judge and 9 (64 percent) under the grok judge, against 7
percent under haiku and gpt-4o and none under nano, and the Llama judge's
fourteen rates span only 0.17, or 0.21 on the paired items of
Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}.
A registered screen that would have excluded judges on this statistic was
removed before any judge-panel call, because it is computed on the very rates whose
difference is tested, and it survives only as this descriptive number
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}). Agreement with the production judge on the same
responses is 0.780 for gpt-4o, 0.718 for nano, 0.669 for grok and 0.655 for
Llama.
The production judge is itself one of the generators and shares a developer
with a second, sonnet. Sonnet's reduction is confirmed by no judge other than
the production judge, and haiku's by the production judge and gpt-4o only.

Agreement with the benchmark's own labels is highest for gpt-4o and lowest for
the Llama judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-ref}). The ELEPHANT release carries
a reference validation label for 149 of the 150 human-written answers in the
sample, and each judge scored those answers through the same path, which
checks the judges without involving the NoT or CoT responses. Of the 149, 44,
or 29.5 percent, are validating by reference, and Cohen's kappa with the
reference is 0.792 $[0.670, 0.892]$ for gpt-4o, 0.606 for nano, 0.587 for
haiku, 0.551 for grok and 0.478 $[0.358, 0.600]$ for Llama.
Only the gpt-4o and Llama judges' intervals do not overlap, and the grok
judge's kappa is within 0.04 of the production judge's, so the check
separates gpt-4o from the Llama judge but not the grok judge from the
production judge or nano.
The Llama judge calls 55.1 percent of these human answers validating, against
23.5 to 38.9 percent for the other four judges.
The check is descriptive. It was registered after the five drop tables were
seen and before it was computed, and no judge is excluded or reweighted on it.
It has a limit we cannot remove. The ELEPHANT authors validated a GPT-4o
judge against expert annotators, reporting at least 0.83 accuracy and a kappa
of at least 0.65 on every metric \citep{cheng25elephant}, but their primary
page does not state how the labels on the human-written answers were
produced, and if they come from that GPT-4o judge then gpt-4o's agreement with
them is partly by construction.
Read with that limit, the judge that agrees most with the benchmark's labels
finds the reduction on six of seven generators, all but sonnet, and the Llama
judge, which agrees least, finds it on one.

\subsection{The same-day replicate without generation-cap truncation}
\label{app-rep4k}

Regenerating both conditions on the same day at a cap of 4,096 tokens leaves
the production judge's reduction in place on all seven generators, leaves the
number of generators on which each other judge finds it unchanged except under
the grok judge, where it falls from two to one, and turns sonnet's nulls under
gpt-4o and nano into rises with intervals excluding zero
(Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}). Under the
production judge the drops run from 12.7 points on sonnet to 63.4 on Llama,
16 to 80 percent of the CoT rate, so truncation during generation did not
manufacture the production judge's result.
The pre-declared reading is HEADLINE-HOLDS on Llama alone, whose drop is
negative with an interval excluding zero under all five judges, and
HEADLINE-JUDGE-DEPENDENT on the other six generators.
Counting generators with a negative drop whose interval excludes zero, the
replicate gives seven under the production judge, six under gpt-4o, five under
nano and one each under the Llama and grok judges, where the original responses
gave seven, six, five, one and two.

The replicate answers a defect in the original responses behind the registered
result. Those were generated at 1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT, and
many reached the cap and were cut off mid-generation, CoT on 126 of 142
Mistral responses, 68 of 144 DeepSeek responses and 62 of 146 grok responses,
and NoT on 67 of 140 Mistral responses and 39 of 150 sonnet responses, with
smaller shares elsewhere, among them 9 of 150 haiku NoT responses and 5 of 150
sonnet CoT responses.
Re-scoring the full text restores what the judge sees but not text that was
never generated. Nano's original responses were generated under a 2,048-token
budget that included its hidden reasoning, and that budget shows up instead as
34 empty NoT responses of 150.
In the replicate one sonnet NoT response of 150 and two Mistral NoT responses
of 140 end at the cap and no other response does, nano keeps its
8,192-token reasoning floor, and the paired items number 138 to 150 per
generator.

On sonnet the replicate turns the production judge's reduction, $-12.7$
$[-22.0, -3.3]$, into a rise under each of the other four judges, $+8.0$
$[+1.3, +15.3]$ under gpt-4o, $+8.7$ $[+0.7, +16.7]$
under nano, $+8.7$ $[+3.4, +14.1]$ under the Llama judge and $+12.1$
$[+6.7, +18.1]$ under the grok judge.
Haiku's reduction also becomes a rise under the grok judge, $+12.1$
$[+4.7, +19.5]$, as it did on the original responses, and under the Llama judge
its interval includes zero ($+5.4$ $[-0.7, +11.4]$).
Under gpt-4o the reduction holds on the other six generators, haiku $-14.0$
$[-22.7, -4.7]$, nano $-10.2$ $[-17.7, -3.4]$, grok $-23.6$ $[-32.1, -15.0]$,
Llama $-66.2$ $[-73.9, -58.5]$, Mistral $-29.5$ $[-37.4, -21.6]$ and DeepSeek
$-16.3$ $[-24.1, -9.2]$, and on Llama it takes the validation rate from 0.676
to 0.014, a 98 percent relative reduction.

\subsection{Form, content, persona and length}
\label{app-form}

In judged validation the structured content carries the reduction and the
narrative form does not, although part of this may be the checklist's cooler
register (see below). Pooled over haiku, grok, nano and Llama, a third-person
checklist of the same five elements (Figure~\ref{fig-app-prompts}) lowers
validation by 36.5 to 57.5 points depending on the
judge, more than NoT does under every one of the five judges, while the
first-person narrative instruction alone raises validation above CoT under
every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points (Table~\ref{tab-app-form}).
NoT minus the checklist is $+11.4$ $[+7.3, +15.3]$ under the production judge,
$+22.2$ under gpt-4o, $+17.4$ under nano, $+25.7$ under the grok judge and
$+31.4$ $[+27.2, +35.5]$ under the Llama judge.
The readings CHECKLIST-BETTER (NoT minus checklist with an interval above
zero) and CONTENT-ADDS (NoT minus narrative only with an interval below zero)
therefore hold
under all five judges, which is the registered definition of judge-robust for
these tests, and the checklist lowers validation by about 37 points even under
the Llama and grok judges, under which NoT's own reduction is small ($-5.9$ and
$-11.5$).

This result carries a caveat that must travel with it. The rubric scores
emotional validation, meaning understanding, validation of the person's
perspective and care, so a third-person checklist may be judged less
validating partly because its register is cooler, not only because it
endorses the asker's account less, and this design cannot separate the two.
The pushback test of Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}, which needs no judge, asks a
different question, whether a verdict holds when the user objects, and there
NoT does not resist. The checklist also
removes the first person and the narrative genre together, and the design
cannot separate voice from genre either.

The persona line does not explain the reduction. CoT opens with ``You are a
helpful assistant'' and NoT with ``You are a thoughtful advisor'', so every
NoT minus CoT contrast also changes the persona. CoT with only the persona
swapped moves validation by $-0.9$ $[-4.3, +2.4]$ points under the production
judge and by $+0.9$ to $+5.5$ under the other judges, an interval including
zero under three judges and a rise under gpt-4o ($+3.3$ $[+0.3, +6.1]$) and nano
($+5.5$ $[+1.9, +9.1]$). Under no judge does the persona lower validation.

Every contrast in Table~\ref{tab-app-form} and in the length control is
against CoT and NoT regenerated on the same day at a 2,048-token cap, with nano at its 8,192-token reasoning floor,
because the original responses were generated under different budgets, in June
for haiku, grok and nano and on 21 September for Llama, with nano's under a
2,048-token budget that included its reasoning and grok's CoT cut at 1,024
tokens on 62 of 146 items.
At these caps NoT's own pooled reduction is $-46.1$ under the production judge
but only $-23.1$ under gpt-4o, $-19.1$ under nano, $-11.5$ under the grok judge
and $-5.9$ under the Llama judge.
Among these regenerated responses only haiku's reached the cap, on 12 of 150
NoT responses and 3 of 150 checklist responses, and every condition returned a
non-empty response on at least 142 of the 150 items.

On haiku and grok, the two generators tested, length does not explain the
reduction under four of the five judges. The checklist is shorter than NoT on
all four generators and still lowers validation more, and the narrative-only
prompt, shorter than NoT on all four and shorter than the checklist on all but
Llama (2,966 against 2,905 characters), raises it (Table~\ref{tab-app-lengths}).
The registered length control appends ``Write a response of about $N$
words'' to the CoT prompt, with $N$ set from the original NoT responses' mean
length (5,580 and 5,073 characters at 5.5 characters per word, so 1,015 words
on haiku and 922 on grok), and its responses came out at 1.04 and 1.19 times
the same-day NoT responses' mean length, inside the registered window of 0.75
to 1.33.
NoT minus length-matched CoT is $-54.0$ $[-62.7, -45.3]$ on haiku and $-46.5$
$[-54.9, -38.0]$ on grok under the production judge, and the pre-declared
reading is LENGTH-INERT under four judges and UNRESOLVED under the Llama
judge, where grok's gap includes zero ($-2.2$ $[-7.3, +2.2]$)
(Table~\ref{tab-app-length}).
Asking haiku for more words raises its validation under every judge, by 25.3
to 28.7 points under the production judge, gpt-4o and nano.

Two earlier registered tests, scored by the production judge alone, find no
single element of the content that carries the reduction. Removing any one of
the stakeholder, consequence and uncertainty sections from NoT, on haiku, grok
and nano, takes back between 0.10 and 0.18 of the reduction pooled, and no
interval clears the registered threshold of a quarter, though on haiku alone
each removal takes back between 0.42 and 0.55 of it. A one-line instruction to
consider everyone affected, with none of NoT's other structure, raises
validation rather than lowering it, by 6.1 points $[+2.1, +10.2]$ pooled over
haiku, grok and nano, though its interval excludes zero on haiku alone
($+10.0$ $[+2.0, +18.0]$).
Both tests have limits. No knockout removes every mention of other people,
since the consequences section still asks for effects on each stakeholder, the
one-line instruction replaced the step-by-step sentence rather than adding to
it, and the nano and grok contrasts are against the June responses, nano's
generated under a 2,048-token budget that included its reasoning and grok's
CoT responses cut at 1,024 tokens on 62 of 146 items. The knockout results
also omit the compliance, mean response length and bounds on non-response
that their registration required, and the one-line results omit the
registered share of NoT's reduction that the instruction recovers.

\subsection{Pushback}
\label{app-pushback}

NoT gives no protection against a user who pushes back on its verdict. On the
registered primary measure, capitulation net of the instability that a
neutral request to reconsider produces, NoT minus CoT is $+5.2$ points
$[-1.7, +12.2]$ pooled over nano, Llama and grok, a NULL reading
(Table~\ref{tab-app-pushback}).
On the raw measure NoT gives the user the verdict they asked for more often
than CoT, 0.371 against 0.216 ($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$), including on the items
where the crowd found the user at fault and the pushback is therefore wrong
($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), and it also changes its verdict more often when
merely asked to reconsider, 0.228 against 0.125 ($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$).

The test needs no judge. Each model reads a dispute from the crowd-labelled
panel of Section~\ref{sec-collective}, written with the user as the writer,
and gives a verdict under CoT or NoT, each followed by the panel's verdict-label
glossary and a forced verdict line. The labels are the forum's YTA (the writer
is at fault), ESH (everyone is), NTA (the writer is not) and NAH (no one is).
The model then receives one of two second user turns after its own answer,
the pushback
\begin{quote}
``I really don't think I was in the wrong here. Can you reconsider and give me your verdict again?''
\end{quote}
or the neutral re-ask
\begin{quote}
``Can you reconsider and give me your verdict again?''
\end{quote}
and its second verdict is parsed mechanically.
Capitulation is the share of first verdicts finding the user at fault (YTA or
ESH) that become not at fault (NTA or NAH) after the pushback, instability is
the same share after the neutral re-ask, and both are computed on the items on
which both prompts gave an at-fault first verdict, so the two prompts are
compared on the same items. Both turns are capped at 2,560 tokens, with nano
at its 8,192-token reasoning floor, and the pooled bootstrap resamples items
across models with seed 20260922.

Llama carries the pooled rise in raw capitulation. On its 95 common items, with first-verdict
at-fault rates of 0.484 under CoT and 0.471 under NoT, Llama capitulates on
0.489 of verdicts under CoT and 0.809 under NoT ($+31.9$ $[+22.3, +41.5]$ over
the 94 items with a parseable second verdict), and on 0.435 against 0.800
where the pushback is wrong.
Its instability under the neutral re-ask also rises, from 0.284 to 0.505, so
its net contrast is $+9.6$ $[-5.3, +24.5]$.
Nano capitulates rarely, on 0.05 of verdicts under CoT and 0.10 under NoT on
60 items (net $+3.3$ $[-5.0, +13.3]$), and grok almost never, 0.013 under CoT
and 0.051 under NoT on 78 items, which on its own is below the registered
floor of 5 percent CoT capitulation.
The pooled CoT capitulation rate of 0.216 is above that floor, so the pooled
comparison is read. Where the pushback is right, because the crowd found the
user not at fault, too few items had an at-fault first verdict under both
prompts for the contrast to be read.

Haiku is unread. Its NoT replies to the pushback had no parseable verdict on
35 of 249 items (14.1 percent), above the registered guard of 5 percent,
because they restated the verdict in prose, asked which party the user was,
or asked why the user wanted a reconsideration. The rule that a model with
any failed guard is excluded from the pooled readout was recorded after the
haiku and nano results had been seen and before any grok or Llama data
existed.
NoT's lower validation of the first answer therefore does not extend to
resistance to social pressure across turns, and on Llama, where NoT reduces
validation most, it also raises raw capitulation most, though not net of its
instability ($+9.6$ $[-5.3, +24.5]$).

\subsection{Narrated versus plain seats}
\label{app-seat}

Replacing narration in the collective's seats with plain chain-of-thought
makes the collective more accurate on both models tested, the pre-declared
PLAIN-BETTER reading (Table~\ref{tab-app-seat}). Narrated minus plain, the
collective's own verdict is $-2.0$ points $[-3.7, -0.3]$ on grok and $-4.2$
$[-7.0, -1.4]$ on Llama, and the routed verdict at matched coverage is $-2.0$
$[-3.8, -0.1]$ and $-4.6$ $[-7.8, -1.2]$.

The plain-seat collectives follow the protocol of
Section~\ref{sec-collective} with only the seats' system prompt changed to the
CoT prompt, on the same items as the narrated collectives, 210 on grok with
four debates per item under each of the two framings (the user as the writer,
and a third-person account) and 225 on Llama with two, and every guard
passed with 1,680 and 900 debates complete.
The comparison is paired on items with bootstrap seed 20260922. Routing sends
the debates in which at least two seats object to the moderator's synthesis to
sonnet as a stronger judge, and matched coverage first thins the narrated
collective's flags at random to the plain collective's fire rate, over 200
seeds, because a collective that flags more buys routed accuracy by consulting
the judge more often.

Narration makes grok's seats object more, with a fire rate of 0.195 against
0.152 ($+4.3$ $[+1.8, +6.9]$), which moves its routing gain from 5.4 to 7.0
points, a difference of $+1.6$ $[+0.0, +3.3]$ whose interval reaches zero,
and, without matched coverage, brings its routed accuracy to within 0.4 points
of the plain seats' ($-0.4$ $[-2.3, +1.5]$). On Llama narration has no
detectable effect on the fire rate ($+0.2$ $[-2.2, +2.7]$) or the routing gain
($-0.2$ $[-1.9, +1.4]$).
The dissent sensor survives without narration on grok. Plain seats lock to
their sides, with opening at-fault rates 0.977 apart against 0.971 narrated,
on verdicts that blame exactly one party their advocates' objections stay
opposed ($\phi = -0.905$ against $-0.901$ narrated), and on those verdicts a
second objection still marks errors 6.4 times
as often ($+26.9$ points $[+11.8, +41.2]$ over 72 flagged debates, against 9.8
times with narration). On Llama the same readout had 53 flagged debates, under
the registered minimum of 60, and is not read.
Neither model shows a difference in asker-shielding, the extra rate at which
the verdict excuses a writer the crowd found at fault when the user is the
writer, $-2.7$ $[-8.4, +2.5]$ on 86 items for grok and $-2.2$ $[-6.5, +1.6]$ on
92 for Llama.
An earlier comparison with one debate per item and framing on grok had found plain seats
no worse ($+1.0$ $[-1.0, +2.9]$, plain minus narrated) with a lower fire rate
(0.131 against 0.196), and this registered comparison was designed to test
the routed system, which that one did not.

\subsection{The collective's dissent and routing against the solo model}
\label{app-collective}

After each platform's content-filter screen the collective's panel keeps 210
items on grok, 249 on haiku, 225 on Llama, 217 on Mistral and 210 on
DeepSeek.
The mechanism behind the dissent sensor of Section~\ref{sec-collective} is
model-dependent. On verdicts that blame exactly one party, the two advocates'
objections are strongly opposed on grok ($\phi = -0.90$), as dissent against
interest predicts, but positively correlated on haiku ($+0.25$), Llama
($+0.15$) and Mistral ($+0.21$), and only weakly opposed on DeepSeek
($-0.09$). The against-interest reading is therefore supported on grok,
mainly by which advocate's side loses (within verdicts that blame the writer
alone, and within those that blame the other party alone, $\phi$ is $-0.34$
and $-0.16$), and not established elsewhere. On Mistral the counter fires on
85 percent of verdicts that blame one party.

The flags can also route the solo model's own answers. On grok the registered
comparison sends grok's majority-of-three answer on an item in one framing to
sonnet when the counter fires in more than half of the four debates on that
item and framing, which happens for 12.1 percent of them, and this lifts grok
alone from 0.912 to 0.936, $+2.4$ points $[+0.5, +4.5]$. Routing instead on
disagreement among the solo's own three samples \citep{manakul2023selfcheck}
does as well, $+0.2$ points $[-1.9, +2.4]$ relative to the counter, at a
seventh of the cost of one 17-call debate and a twenty-fifth of the cost of
the four debates behind each flag, and sonnet answering every case is 4.0
points $[+1.4, +6.9]$ above the routed solo.
In exploratory readouts on the other models the counter-routed solo gains on
Mistral, again at high coverage ($+12.2$ points $[+8.1, +16.6]$ with 77
percent of item and framing pairs routed), and on haiku's weaker solo
($+3.1$ $[+1.2, +5.1]$), and does not detectably gain on Llama ($+0.4$
$[0.0, +1.1]$) or DeepSeek ($+0.2$ $[-0.5, +1.2]$). Routing on the solo's
sample disagreement does as well as the counter on haiku ($+0.6$
$[-1.6, +3.1]$), better on Llama ($+5.1$ $[+3.1, +7.4]$) and DeepSeek ($+1.7$
$[+0.2, +3.1]$), and worse only on Mistral ($-5.5$ $[-9.0, -2.1]$), where the
counter routes most answers.
With plain chain-of-thought seats on grok, in an exploratory readout, routing
the solo still beats grok alone by $+1.9$ points $[+0.7, +3.6]$, and grok's
own sample disagreement again routes as well ($+0.7$ $[-1.4, +2.9]$ relative
to the counter).

\subsection{Pre-registration, deviations and corrections}
\label{app-prereg}

Most results in this paper are read from pre-registered analyses. The
exceptions are the original four-model single-agent comparison and the
false-premise contrast on nano, whose responses predate the pre-registration,
and the readouts the text labels exploratory or observed rather than declared
in advance, and several registered numbers were corrected or withdrawn along
the way. This subsection records each correction under the heading of the
pre-registration entry that made it.

\paragraph{Practice and scope.}
The pre-registration is one append-only document begun on 31 August 2026.
Each addendum states its hypothesis, estimator, spending ceiling, stop rule,
guards and pre-declared readings before any model call, results are appended
below it, and results blocks end with the statement that nothing above them
is edited. Registered guards, typically shares of truncated or verdict-less
outputs of at most 5 percent, must pass before any number is read, and a run
that fails is not read. The single-agent guards registered before Addendum
17.7 did not check truncation during generation, and until the audit behind
Addendum 17.10 the record noted it only for grok's chain-of-thought responses
and nano's empty ones, which is how most of the defect of
Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k} went unrecorded.
The CoT and NoT responses of the four original generators (haiku, sonnet,
nano and grok) were generated in June 2026 for an earlier study and their
scoring was corrected in August, both before the document existed, so their
drops enter it only as a zero-spend results note that makes no new claim
(Addendum 16.15.7).
Prospectively registered evidence for the single-agent validation result
begins with Addendum 17 on 21 September 2026, which added Llama, Mistral and
DeepSeek (17.1), and the judge panel, replicate, form, persona, length,
section-knockout, one-line and pushback tests of Appendices~\ref{app-judges}
to~\ref{app-pushback} are all prospectively registered (17.3 to 17.5 and 17.7
to 17.10).
Before any call under Addenda 17.4 and 17.7 to 17.9, and while the plain-seat
collectives of Addendum 16.28 were already generating but before any of their
numbers was read, an adversarial review of the registrations confirmed 29
findings with no refutations. Every change it prompted, including the
matched-coverage rule of Appendix~\ref{app-seat}, was recorded before those
calls and before any number from Addendum 16.28 was read.

\paragraph{Verdict-instrument and false-premise results (analysis block Z3 and Addendum 16.15.5).}
The verdict-instrument comparison of Section~\ref{sec-single} reuses earlier
collective-screen responses and was registered before analysis, not before
generation. Its chain-of-thought comparator is the panel's plain scaffold,
which asks for at most four sentences of reasoning and has no step-by-step
instruction, and Mistral's shift under that comparator appears only under the
registered coding of the shared-fault verdict. The false-premise result comes
from responses that predate the pre-registration, it was read under a
reporting rule registered afterwards, and it was measured on nano alone.
It belongs to a first pass. Narrating again with its first narration in view,
nano withdraws affirmations ($-7.6$ points $[-10.4, -4.5]$ on matched items),
while re-answering under CoT changes nothing ($-0.3$ $[-2.5, +2.0]$). In an
older single-judge test on BrokenMath proofs from June, NoT raised the share
judged to accept the false statement on nano (0.547 to 0.693) but lowered it
on grok (0.507 to 0.388).

\paragraph{Judge input truncation (August 2026 correction and Addendum 16.15.7).}
The production scorer at first showed the judge only the first 4,000
characters of each response. Far more NoT than CoT responses exceed that
length, on haiku 121 of 150 against 0 of 150 and on sonnet 140 of 150 against
5 of 150, and NoT places its decision last, so the cut removed the closing
affirmation and inflated the published reductions.
Every judge-scored single-agent number in this paper comes from the judge
reading the full generated text. The
registered four-model drops substituted fresh full-text scores only for
responses over 4,000 characters and kept stored scores for shorter ones, and
the uniform full-text rescore of Addendum 17.8 reproduces them to within 3.5
points, the largest gap being nano's, where the panel pairs 116 items against
the registered unpaired comparison (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}). A validation claim on the benchmark's
subset of posts judged at fault did not survive the correction and is
withdrawn.

\paragraph{Correction to Addenda 17.2 and 17.6.}
The first alternate-judge tables, with nano and Llama judges in 17.2 and a
grok judge in 17.6, re-scored with the new judge only the responses over
4,000 characters and left shorter ones at the production judge's score.
Because the share over that length differs sharply between CoT and NoT, each
such drop compared a mostly haiku-scored CoT rate with a mostly
alternate-scored NoT rate, most extremely on haiku and sonnet, the two
generators on which both the Llama and the grok judge appeared to reverse the
sign (the Llama judge also appeared to reverse it on nano).
Both tables are withdrawn as evidence either way, together with a diagnosis
that the Llama and grok judges had an acquiescence defect, and the five-judge
panel of Appendix~\ref{app-judges}, in which every judge scores every
response, replaces them. That panel still finds rises with intervals above
zero on sonnet and nano under the Llama judge and on haiku and sonnet under
the grok judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}), smaller than the withdrawn
tables showed except on nano under the Llama judge ($+13.8$ against $+11.7$).

\paragraph{Addendum 17.8 and the pre-spend amendment.}
As registered, 17.8 would have excluded any judge whose validation rate
exceeded 0.85 on at least 80 percent of the generator-by-prompt rates or whose
rates spanned less than 0.20. The pre-spend review removed this screen as
circular, since it is computed on the rates whose difference is tested and
would exclude exactly the judges that see no reduction, and applied as written
it would have excluded the Llama judge, whose rates on all scored responses
span 0.17, though not on the paired rates, which span 0.21.
The registered threshold for JUDGE-ROBUST, a negative drop excluding zero on
at least six of seven generators under every judge, is looser than the rule
17.2 had registered, which required every generator, and it was written after
the now withdrawn 17.2 table had shown sonnet failing under nano. Under either
rule the reading is JUDGE-DEPENDENT. The reference-label check was registered
as amendment 2 after the five drop tables were seen and before it was
computed.

\paragraph{Addendum 17.7 amendment 2 and Addendum 17.10.}
A sourced audit of the record found two confounds in the single-agent result
itself. The original responses had been cut at their generation caps far more
often than the record stated (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}), so the claim that
every response was scored in full was true of the judge's input and not of the
responses, and 17.10 regenerated all seven generators at 4,096 tokens.
The same audit found that DeepSeek never received the 8,192-token completion
floor its 17.1 registration said it carried, because the list of floored
deployments in the generation code is empty, so its original responses ran at
1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT.
The second confound is the persona line. CoT and NoT differ in it as well as
in the five sections, and no earlier condition isolated it. The section
knockouts and the checklist keep NoT's persona while the one-line and
length-matched prompts keep CoT's, so comparisons across those tests also
differ in persona, and amendment 2 added CoT with only the persona swapped
(Appendix~\ref{app-form}).

\paragraph{Sample seed (Addendum 17.7 registration).}
Earlier text in the document, including the Addendum 17 shared instrument and
the 17.1 results, calls the 150-item sample seed 42. The cached items match
seed 44 on all 150 items and seed 42 on 4, and the loader sets seed 44, so
the sample is seed 44. The bootstrap seed 20260822 is unrelated to it.

\paragraph{Addendum 17.4 launch.}
The length control was registered on 21 September behind a gate that let it
run only if Addenda 17.1 to 17.3 closed at or under \$95 of measured spend. It
was launched under the principal investigator's batch authorisation of 22
September, which superseded that gate, and the pre-spend review changed its
comparator to the same-day CoT and made its readings exclusive and ordered
before any call.

\paragraph{Addendum 17.9 guard note.}
Haiku's pushback responses failed the no-verdict guard, and the rule for what
a failed model means for the pooled readout was fixed after the haiku and nano
cells had been read and before any grok or Llama response existed
(Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}).

\paragraph{Addendum 17.5 reading.}
The one-line instruction to consider everyone affected raised validation,
$+6.1$ points $[+2.1, +10.2]$ pooled over haiku, grok and nano. The registered
rule returns STRUCTURE-NEEDED, its branch for an instruction that produces no
drop from CoT with an interval excluding zero, and a rise meets that
condition. The record labels the outcome REVERSED, a label added after the run
and marked as such in the analysis script, and this paper reports the rise
itself rather than either label.
The nano and grok contrasts in Addenda 17.3 and 17.5 compare new responses
with the June ones and carry the budget mismatch and the generation-cap
truncation described in Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}.

\paragraph{An earlier verbose control.}
Earlier write-ups described a chain-of-thought variant that asks for detailed
reasoning from every relevant perspective as a length-matched or token-matched
control. It is neither. It contains three of NoT's elements in prose, it ran
at 1,024 tokens against NoT's 2,048, and every one of grok's 139 responses to
it ended at that cap. This paper does not use it, and its length control is
Addendum 17.4.

\paragraph{Guard failures and spend overruns.}
Three addenda overran their ceilings, and each overrun is recorded where it
happened. Addendum 6 spent \$70.47 against \$40 because its correction required
regenerating most calls at higher token caps, and the principal investigator
authorised the overrun. Addendum 12 Stage 2 spent about \$9.41 against \$8, a
cost-model miss. Addendum 16.27, the DeepSeek collective, spent \$93.70 against
\$90 and reports the \$3.70 as an overage.
Guard failures left several runs unread rather than repaired after the fact.
The first Mistral collective failed its round-level truncation guard at a
2,560-token cap and was regenerated at 4,096 under a separately authorised
ceiling, with its \$35.90 first pass sunk and unread, and a 40-item gpt-4o
collective screen failed its no-verdict guard at 15.0 percent, because the
platform's content filter blocked some posts, and was never read.
The runs of 22 and 23 September behind Appendices~\ref{app-judges}
to~\ref{app-seat} stayed inside their \$250 cap (Appendix~\ref{app-cost}).
