\section{Appendix}
\label{app}

This appendix gives the full evidence behind the single-agent validation and
pushback results, the narrated-versus-plain-seat comparison of the collective
and the collective's dissent mechanism and routing against the solo model, and
records every correction and deviation from the pre-registration.
Model names are shortened after first use. claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku)
\citep{anthropic_haiku45} and claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet)
\citep{anthropic_sonnet46} are Anthropic models, gpt-5.4-nano (nano)
\citep{openai_gpt54nano} and gpt-4o \citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard}
are OpenAI models, grok-4-1-fast-reasoning
(grok) \citep{xai_grok41} is an xAI model, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama)
\citep{meta_llama33}, Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) \citep{mistral_large3} and
DeepSeek-V4-Pro (DeepSeek) \citep{deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report} are
open-weight models from Meta, Mistral AI and DeepSeek
(Table~\ref{tab-app-models}).
\todo{PI: Mistral-Large-3-2 is the Azure AI Foundry deployment name and no Mistral AI page names a 3.2 version, so it is cited as Mistral Large 3 (25.12). Please confirm the deployment serves that model.}
NoT is the Narration-of-Thought system prompt and CoT the one-line standard
chain-of-thought prompt (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}). The single-agent outcome is
the validation rate, the share of responses a judge model scores as
emotionally validating under the rubric of the ELEPHANT benchmark
\citep{cheng25elephant}, which asks whether a response shows understanding,
validates the person's perspective and expresses care. The items are 150 of
the benchmark's open-ended personal-advice questions, drawn with seed 44.
A drop is the NoT rate minus the CoT rate in percentage points, so a negative
drop means NoT validates less. Intervals are 95 percent percentile bootstraps
that resample items and keep every response to an item together, with 8,000
draws unless stated.

\paragraph{Registered readings.}
Each test was registered with a short set of capitalised readings, one of
which the analysis script returns mechanically once the data are in, and the
tables and text of this appendix report the returned reading beside the
numbers. Every reading used below is defined here and nowhere else. For the
judge panel (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}), JUDGE-ROBUST means a negative drop
with an interval excluding zero on at least six of the seven generators under
every judge, and JUDGE-DEPENDENT means anything short of that.
For the same-day replicate (Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}), HOLDS means a
generator's drop is negative with an interval excluding zero under the
production judge and under at least three of the other four judges, and
JUDGE-DEPENDENT means it is negative with an interval excluding zero under
the production judge but under fewer than three others. The registered names
carry the prefix HEADLINE, which the table omits, and the third registered
reading, FAILS, which would mean the production judge's interval includes
zero or lies above it, is returned on no generator.
For the form tests (Table~\ref{tab-app-form}), CHECKLIST-BETTER means NoT
minus the checklist has an interval above zero, CONTENT-ADDS means NoT minus
the narrative-only prompt has an interval below zero, PERSONA-INERT means the
persona swap alone changes validation by an amount whose interval includes
zero, and PERSONA-RAISES means that interval lies above zero.
For the length control (Table~\ref{tab-app-length}), LENGTH-INERT means NoT
minus length-matched CoT is below zero with an interval excluding zero on
both generators, LENGTH-CARRIES, which no judge returns, would mean the
length-matched prompt's own drop from CoT has an interval below zero and
reaches half of NoT's drop on either generator, and UNRESOLVED means neither
holds.
For pushback (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}), NOT-RESISTS means NoT minus CoT
capitulation is negative with an interval excluding zero, NOT-WORSE means it
is positive with an interval excluding zero, NULL means the interval includes
zero, and FLOOR means CoT's pooled capitulation is under 5 percent so the
contrast is not read.
For the seat comparison (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}), PLAIN-BETTER means the
collective's own accuracy or its routed accuracy favours plain seats with an
interval excluding zero on either model.
For the one-sentence instruction test recorded in Appendix~\ref{app-prereg},
STRUCTURE-NEEDED is the registered branch for an instruction that produces no
drop from CoT with an interval excluding zero, and REVERSED, a label added
after the run, names a rise with an interval excluding zero.

\subsection{Five judges and agreement with the benchmark's labels}
\label{app-judges}

The reduction depends on the judge. When the full generated text of every one
of the 2,004 non-empty CoT and NoT responses is scored by each of five judges,
NoT lowers
validation with an interval excluding zero on all seven generators under the
production judge (haiku), on six under gpt-4o, five under nano, one under the
Llama judge and two under the grok judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}). The pre-declared
reading is therefore JUDGE-DEPENDENT (defined at the head of this appendix),
and only Llama's reduction holds under all five judges.
The registered rules apply no correction for the seven tests per judge, where
earlier addenda had used Bonferroni. A Holm step-down over the seven
generators within each judge, computed after review from the cached per-item
scores, leaves seven, four, five, one and one generators whose negative drop
survives. The correction removes haiku and nano under gpt-4o,
DeepSeek under the grok judge and, among the rises, sonnet under the Llama
judge, and it changes no registered reading, since JUDGE-ROBUST needs six of
seven under every judge and Llama's reduction survives under all five.
Under nano the interval includes zero on haiku
($-5.3$) and sonnet ($+2.0$), and under gpt-4o it includes zero on sonnet
($+3.3$ $[-4.7, +11.3]$). The Llama judge finds a reduction only on Llama
($-11.3$ $[-19.1, -3.5]$) and a rise on sonnet ($+7.4$ $[+2.0, +13.4]$) and
nano ($+13.8$ $[+7.8, +20.7]$). The grok judge finds a reduction on Llama
($-49.6$) and DeepSeek ($-5.0$ $[-10.0, -0.7]$) and a rise on haiku ($+11.4$
$[+5.4, +18.1]$) and sonnet ($+14.0$ $[+8.7, +20.0]$).

Each judge scored each response through the same full-text path, so the
scores differ only in which model judges them, and each contrast is paired on
the items with
a response in both conditions, 116 to 150 per generator
(Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}), with bootstrap seed 20260822.
Unparsed judge outputs were 0.0 percent for haiku, gpt-4o and nano and 0.3
percent for the Llama and grok judges, and one Llama-judge call was lost to a
rate limit.
Scored this way, the production judge reproduces the registered drops
closely, haiku $-26.7$ exactly, grok $-35.2$ against $-36.0$, Llama $-70.2$
against $-69.9$, Mistral $-57.6$ against $-58.2$, DeepSeek $-46.1$ against
$-45.5$, nano $-37.9$ on its 116 paired items against $-41.4$ unpaired, and
sonnet $-19.3$ against $-22.3$, a registered range of 22 to 70 points, or 31
to 87 percent of the CoT rate, from a scoring that mixed stored and fresh
scores. Apart from nano, whose gap reflects the
pairing, sonnet's 3.0 points is the largest difference.

The two judges that find little or no reduction are the two that rate most
responses validating. Of the fourteen generator-by-prompt validation rates,
each over all responses scored in its condition, 11 (79 percent) exceed 0.85
under the Llama judge and 9 (64 percent) under the grok judge, against 7
percent under haiku and gpt-4o and none under nano, and the Llama judge's
fourteen rates span only 0.17, or 0.21 on the paired items of
Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-rates}.
A ceiling can hide a reduction but cannot produce a rise whose interval
excludes zero. The rises these two judges find in Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}
are rises into the ceiling, from a CoT rate of 0.79 to a NoT rate of 0.90 on
haiku and from 0.81 to 0.95 on sonnet under the grok judge and from 0.87 to
0.94 on sonnet under the Llama judge, and each repeats in the regeneration of
Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}, so they are evidence that these judges score NoT's
answers as validating more often than CoT's, whatever feature of the answers
they respond to, and not reductions the ceiling hides. The Llama judge's
rise on nano ($+13.8$) does not repeat there ($-4.1$ $[-11.7, +2.8]$).
A registered screen that would have excluded judges on this statistic was
removed before any judge-panel call, because it is computed on the very rates whose
difference is tested, and it survives only as this descriptive number
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}). Agreement with the production judge on the same
responses is 0.780 for gpt-4o, 0.718 for nano, 0.669 for grok and 0.655 for
Llama.
Raw agreement is inflated by chance where both judges validate most
responses, and Cohen's kappa with the production judge on the same responses
is 0.543 $[0.501, 0.584]$ for gpt-4o, 0.418 $[0.370, 0.461]$ for nano, 0.270
$[0.223, 0.316]$ for grok and 0.231 $[0.179, 0.283]$ for Llama. On the CoT
responses alone the four values lie between 0.48 and 0.61, on the NoT
responses they fall to 0.40, 0.27, 0.14 and 0.12, and the Llama and grok
judges agree with each other at 0.870 raw but 0.427 $[0.345, 0.499]$ by
kappa, so the panel splits into two blocs whose disagreement is concentrated
on the NoT responses (Table~\ref{tab-app-interjudge}). Agreement with the
production judge, itself a generator, is a reliability statistic and not a
validity one, and this computation was added after review from the stored
scores without new calls.
The judges' own rates bound how far kappa can rise. Given the two judges'
marginal rates, kappa with the production judge cannot exceed 0.374 for grok
and 0.276 for Llama, against 0.852 for gpt-4o and 0.899 for nano, so part of
the two judges' low agreement is forced by how often they validate.
The production judge agrees with itself. On the 814 responses of at most
4,000 characters, whose judge input was byte-identical in the production
scorer's pass and in the full-text pass behind
Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}, the two scores agree on 0.969 of responses
with a kappa of 0.932 $[0.906, 0.956]$, 25 flips and a shift of $-0.9$
points $[-2.0, +0.3]$, the two passes being months apart for the four
original generators, whereas on the 1,189 responses over 4,000 characters,
where the first pass read a cut input, agreement is 0.829 with a kappa of
0.659 and a shift of $+7.1$ points $[+4.8, +9.4]$ toward validating, which
is the truncation effect recorded in Appendix~\ref{app-prereg} and not
judge noise.

The production judge is itself one of the generators and shares a developer
with a second, sonnet. Sonnet's reduction is confirmed by no judge other than
the production judge, and haiku's by the production judge and gpt-4o only.

Agreement with the benchmark's own labels is highest for gpt-4o and lowest for
the Llama judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-ref}). The ELEPHANT release carries
a reference validation label for 149 of the 150 human-written answers in the
sample, and each judge scored those answers through the same path, which
checks the judges without involving the NoT or CoT responses. Of the 149, 44,
or 29.5 percent, are validating by reference, and Cohen's kappa with the
reference is 0.792 $[0.670, 0.892]$ for gpt-4o, 0.606 for nano, 0.587 for
haiku, 0.551 for grok and 0.478 $[0.358, 0.600]$ for Llama.
Only the gpt-4o and Llama judges' intervals do not overlap, and the grok
judge's kappa is within 0.04 of the production judge's, so the check
separates gpt-4o from the Llama judge but not the grok judge from the
production judge or nano.
The Llama judge calls 55.1 percent of these human answers validating, against
23.5 to 38.9 percent for the other four judges.
The check is descriptive. It was registered after the five drop tables were
seen and before it was computed, and no judge is excluded or reweighted on it.
It has a limit we cannot remove. The ELEPHANT authors validated a GPT-4o
judge against expert annotators, reporting at least 0.83 accuracy and a kappa
of at least 0.65 on every metric \citep{cheng25elephant}, but their primary
page does not state how the labels on the human-written answers were
produced, and if they come from that GPT-4o judge then gpt-4o's agreement with
them is partly by construction.
Read with that limit, the judge that agrees most with the benchmark's labels
finds the reduction on six of seven generators, all but sonnet, and the Llama
judge, which agrees least, finds it on one.

\subsection{The same-day replicate without generation-cap truncation}
\label{app-rep4k}

Regenerating both conditions on the same day at a cap of 4,096 tokens leaves
the production judge's reduction in place on all seven generators, leaves the
number of generators on which each other judge finds it unchanged except under
the grok judge, where it falls from two to one, and turns sonnet's nulls under
gpt-4o and nano into rises with intervals excluding zero
(Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}). Under the
production judge the drops run from 12.7 points on sonnet to 63.4 on Llama,
16 to 80 percent of the CoT rate, so truncation during generation did not
manufacture the production judge's result.
The pre-declared reading is HEADLINE-HOLDS on Llama alone, whose drop is
negative with an interval excluding zero under all five judges, and
HEADLINE-JUDGE-DEPENDENT on the other six generators.
Counting generators with a negative drop whose interval excludes zero, the
replicate gives seven under the production judge, six under gpt-4o, five under
nano and one each under the Llama and grok judges, where the original responses
gave seven, six, five, one and two.
A Holm correction within each judge leaves the replicate's counts unchanged
and, of sonnet's four rises, removes only the nano judge's.

The replicate answers a defect in the original responses behind the registered
result. Those were generated at 1,024 tokens for CoT and 2,048 for NoT, and
many reached the cap and were cut off mid-generation, CoT on 126 of 142
Mistral responses, 68 of 144 DeepSeek responses and 62 of 146 grok responses,
and NoT on 67 of 140 Mistral responses and 39 of 150 sonnet responses, with
smaller shares elsewhere, among them 9 of 150 haiku NoT responses and 5 of 150
sonnet CoT responses.
Re-scoring the full text restores what the judge sees but not text that was
never generated. Nano's original responses were generated under a 2,048-token
budget that included its hidden reasoning, and that budget shows up instead as
34 empty NoT responses of 150.
In the replicate one sonnet NoT response of 150 and two Mistral NoT responses
of 140 end at the cap and no other response does, and nano keeps its
8,192-token reasoning floor. The paired items number 139 to 150 per generator
under the production judge, gpt-4o and nano. Of the replicate's 2,036
non-empty responses, the Llama judge and the grok judge each lost two scores
to rate limits, both on the grok generator, and each returned five unparsed
outputs, so under those two judges the paired items number 138 to 149, at
most two fewer per generator. Each contrast is computed on the items scored
in both conditions, which treats a lost or unparsed score as unrelated to the
response it would have carried. The pre-declared readings need a negative
drop with an interval excluding zero, two lost items move a drop by at most
about two points, and on no generator that lost items does an upper interval
end in Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k} lie within two points below zero, so no
reading there rests on that assumption.

Each generator answered each item once per prompt in each run, but the CoT
and NoT contrast was generated two or three times per generator, on the
original responses, on the same-day replicate at 2,048 tokens that serves as
the comparator of Appendix~\ref{app-form} (haiku, grok, nano and Llama) and
on the same-day replicate at 4,096 tokens, and every run was scored by all
five judges, so the runs show how far the drop moves between generations
(Table~\ref{tab-app-runs}). This comparison was added after the runs and was
not registered.
The runs differ in their caps and, for nano, in the number of paired items,
so the movement between them includes the effect of truncation and bounds
sampling variation from above. The table gives two pooled estimates, one
that resamples items with run as a fixed crossed factor and one that treats
run as a random factor, with a variance component for run fitted by the
method of moments on one or two degrees of freedom and clipped at zero where
the fit is negative, which it is on 17 of the 35 judge and generator pairs.
Under the production judge the drop is negative with an interval excluding
zero in every run on every generator, and pooled over runs with run fixed it
is $-16.0$ $[-22.7, -9.3]$ points on sonnet, the smallest, and $-65.9$
$[-72.3, -59.2]$ on Llama, the largest. Treating run as random moves each
bound by under 3 points except on nano, where the three runs differ most,
by 13.1 points between the farthest two, and the interval widens from
$[-50.3, -37.1]$ to $[-50.9, -30.4]$.
Under gpt-4o the sign is the same in every run on every generator, sonnet's
pooled rise is $+5.7$ $[0.0, +11.3]$, and Mistral's and DeepSeek's drops
shrink from the original responses to the uncapped run.
Under the Llama judge the reversal on nano, $+13.8$ $[+7.8, +20.7]$ on the
original responses, whose 116 paired items exclude the 34 empty NoT
responses, does not recur in either same-day run ($-4.1$ $[-10.2, +2.0]$ and
$-4.1$ $[-11.7, +2.8]$), and the sign changes between runs on haiku, nano and
grok under the Llama judge and on nano, grok and Mistral under the grok
judge, each time with an interval including zero in at least one run.
The Llama generator's reduction keeps its sign and an interval excluding zero
in every run under every judge.
Item-level drops move far more than generator-level drops. Across the 75
judge, generator and run-pair combinations the item-level drop of one run
correlates with that of another at a mean $r$ of 0.27, and a single run's
item-level drop has an intraclass correlation of about 0.24, while the
run-level drop moves by 2.0 points on average and 8.0 at most between runs,
so a generator's drop under a judge replicates and an item's does not.

\subsection{Form, content, persona and length}
\label{app-form}

In judged validation the structured content carries the reduction and the
narrative form does not, although part of this may be the checklist's cooler
register (see below). Pooled over haiku, grok, nano and Llama, a third-person
checklist of the same five elements (Figure~\ref{fig-app-prompts}) lowers
validation by 36.5 to 57.5 points depending on the
judge, more than NoT does under every one of the five judges, while the
first-person narrative instruction alone raises validation above CoT under
every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points (Table~\ref{tab-app-form}).
NoT minus the checklist is $+11.4$ $[+7.3, +15.3]$ under the production judge,
$+22.2$ under gpt-4o, $+17.4$ under nano, $+25.7$ under the grok judge and
$+31.4$ $[+27.2, +35.5]$ under the Llama judge.
The readings CHECKLIST-BETTER (NoT minus checklist with an interval above
zero) and CONTENT-ADDS (NoT minus narrative only with an interval below zero)
therefore hold
under all five judges, which is the registered definition of judge-robust for
these tests, and the checklist lowers validation by about 37 points even under
the Llama and grok judges, under which NoT's own reduction is small ($-5.9$ and
$-11.5$).

This result carries a caveat that must travel with it. The rubric scores
emotional validation, meaning understanding, validation of the person's
perspective and care, so a third-person checklist may be judged less
validating partly because its register is cooler, not only because it
endorses the asker's account less, and this design cannot separate the two.
The pushback test of Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}, which needs no judge, asks a
different question, whether a verdict holds when the user objects, and there
NoT does not resist. The checklist also
removes the first person and the narrative genre together, and the design
cannot separate voice from genre either.

The persona line does not explain the reduction. CoT opens with ``You are a
helpful assistant'' and NoT with ``You are a thoughtful advisor'', so every
NoT minus CoT contrast also changes the persona. CoT with only the persona
swapped moves validation by $-0.9$ $[-4.3, +2.4]$ points under the production
judge and by $+0.9$ to $+5.5$ under the other judges, an interval including
zero under three judges, a marginal rise under gpt-4o ($+3.3$ $[+0.3, +6.1]$)
and a rise under nano ($+5.5$ $[+1.9, +9.1]$). Under no judge does the
persona lower validation. The readings rest on these pooled contrasts. A
Holm step-down over the four generators within each judge, applied after
review to the per-generator contrasts behind them, keeps every checklist
contrast under every judge, keeps none of the three per-generator persona
contrasts whose raw interval excludes zero, haiku's and Llama's under nano
and haiku's under gpt-4o, and removes one narrative-only contrast, Llama's
under the Llama judge, as the daggers in Table~\ref{tab-app-form} mark,
while for the length control it keeps NoT minus length-matched CoT on both
generators under each of the four judges that read LENGTH-INERT.

against CoT and NoT regenerated on the same day at a 2,048-token cap, with nano at its 8,192-token reasoning floor,
because the original responses were generated under different budgets, in June

\subsection{Pushback}
\label{app-pushback}

NoT gives way more than CoT to a user who pushes back on its verdict. On the
registered primary measure, capitulation net of the instability that a
neutral request to reconsider produces, NoT minus CoT is $+5.2$ points
$[-1.7, +12.2]$ pooled over nano, Llama and grok, a NULL reading under the
registered definitions at the head of this appendix
(Table~\ref{tab-app-pushback}). The pooled net interval reaches 7 points
either side of its estimate and Llama's 15, no minimum detectable difference
was registered for this test, and a calculation made after review puts the
difference this contrast would detect with 80 percent power at about 10
points pooled and 21 on Llama.
On the raw measure NoT gives the user the verdict they asked for more often
than CoT, 0.371 against 0.216 ($+15.5$ $[+11.0, +20.2]$), including on the items
where the crowd found the user at fault and the pushback is therefore wrong
($+16.3$ $[+11.8, +20.8]$), and it also changes its verdict more often when
merely asked to reconsider, 0.228 against 0.125 ($+10.3$ $[+5.7, +14.9]$).

The test needs no judge. Each model reads a dispute from the crowd-labelled
panel of Section~\ref{sec-collective}, written with the user as the writer,
and gives a verdict under CoT or NoT, each followed by the panel's verdict-label
glossary and a forced verdict line. The labels are the forum's YTA (the writer
is at fault), ESH (everyone is), NTA (the writer is not) and NAH (no one is).
The model then receives one of two second user turns after its own answer,
the pushback
\begin{quote}
``I really don't think I was in the wrong here. Can you reconsider and give me your verdict again?''
\end{quote}
or the neutral re-ask
\begin{quote}
``Can you reconsider and give me your verdict again?''
\end{quote}
and its second verdict is parsed mechanically.
Capitulation is the share of first verdicts finding the user at fault (YTA or
ESH) that become not at fault (NTA or NAH) after the pushback, instability is
the same share after the neutral re-ask, and both are computed on the items on
which both prompts gave an at-fault first verdict, so the two prompts are
compared on the same items. Both turns are capped at 2,560 tokens, with nano
at its 8,192-token reasoning floor, and the pooled bootstrap resamples items
across models with seed 20260922.

Llama carries the pooled rise in raw capitulation. On its 95 common items, with first-verdict
at-fault rates of 0.484 under CoT and 0.471 under NoT, Llama capitulates on
0.489 of verdicts under CoT and 0.809 under NoT ($+31.9$ $[+22.3, +41.5]$ over
the 94 items with a parseable second verdict), and on 0.435 against 0.800
where the pushback is wrong.
Its instability under the neutral re-ask also rises, from 0.284 to 0.505, so
its net contrast is $+9.6$ $[-5.3, +24.5]$.
Nano capitulates rarely, on 0.05 of verdicts under CoT and 0.10 under NoT on
60 items (net $+3.3$ $[-5.0, +13.3]$), and grok almost never, 0.013 under CoT
and 0.051 under NoT on 78 items, which on its own is below the registered
floor of 5 percent CoT capitulation.
The pooled CoT capitulation rate of 0.216 is above that floor, so the pooled
comparison is read. Where the pushback is right, because the crowd found the
user not at fault, too few items had an at-fault first verdict under both
prompts for the contrast to be read.

Haiku is unread. Its NoT replies to the pushback had no parseable verdict on
35 of 249 items (14.1 percent), above the registered guard of 5 percent,
because they restated the verdict in prose, asked which party the user was,
or asked why the user wanted a reconsideration. The rule that a model with
any failed guard is excluded from the pooled readout was recorded after the
haiku and nano results had been seen and before any grok or Llama data
existed.
NoT's lower validation of the first answer therefore does not extend to
resistance to social pressure across turns, and on Llama, where NoT reduces
validation most, it also raises raw capitulation most, though not net of its
instability ($+9.6$ $[-5.3, +24.5]$).

\subsection{The collective's dissent and routing against the solo model}
\label{app-collective}

After each platform's content-filter screen the collective's panel, 249
items of which the crowd found the writer at fault on 99 and not on 150,
keeps 210 items on grok, 249 on haiku, 225 on Llama, 217 on Mistral and 210
on DeepSeek.
With four debates per item and framing on grok, two on Llama, Mistral and
DeepSeek and one on haiku, the panels hold 1,680 debates on grok with the
edges on and again with them off, 900 on Llama, 868 on Mistral, 840 on
DeepSeek and 491 on haiku, whose seven missing debates, all with the user as
the writer, failed at the platform and are handled under the registered
partial-panel rule. Dropping the debates in which the moderator's synthesis
or integrated verdict does not parse removes 3, 5, 3, 0, 8 and 4 of these
respectively and leaves the codable counts of Table~\ref{tab-routing}.
Each debate runs seven stages in 17 calls. The three seats open, rebut and
restate, the moderator writes a synthesis, each seat labels it accepted,
accepted with a stated modification or rejected, the moderator writes an
integrated verdict, which is scored as the collective's, and the seats vote.
Role-lock is the gap between the two advocates' opening at-fault rates on
each seat's own codable openings. It is 0.971 on grok, where the writer's
advocate blames the writer on 0.3 percent of openings, the other party's
advocate on 97.4 percent and the neutral seat on 43.0 percent, so that a
majority of the three opening verdicts equals the neutral seat's on all
1,642 debates with three codable openings, 0.862 on Llama (12.3, 98.5 and
73.3 percent), 0.816 on DeepSeek (6.5, 88.1 and 45.4 percent), 0.708 on
Mistral (4.2, 75.0 and 52.9 percent) and 0.419 on haiku (49.9, 91.8 and
68.2 percent), where the writer's advocate blames the writer on half of its
openings and is the most accurate seat. The lock is graded rather than
present or absent, and it does not by itself fix the sensor. Haiku, the
least locked, is the one model on which the second objection's lift on
one-party verdicts includes zero, but Llama's seats lock nearly as tightly
as grok's while its advocates' objections are positively correlated, so a
tight lock appears necessary for opposed objections and not sufficient, a
pattern over five models that we report as suggestive.
The intervals in Table~\ref{tab-routing} come from an independent
recomputation with 4,000 bootstrap draws and agree with each registered
interval to within 0.6 points.
The solo and paired-difference columns of the same table come from a
comparison added after review, which pairs each codable debate with the same
model's majority-of-three answer on its own item and framing and bootstraps
the difference over items with the same 4,000 draws and seed, and a
variant that weights each item and framing pair equally, like a second
seed, moves no bound by more than 0.2 points.
The mechanism behind the dissent sensor of Section~\ref{sec-collective} is
model-dependent. On verdicts that blame exactly one party, the two advocates'
objections are strongly opposed on grok ($\phi = -0.90$), as dissent against
interest predicts, but positively correlated on haiku ($+0.25$), Llama
($+0.15$) and Mistral ($+0.21$), and only weakly opposed on DeepSeek
($-0.09$). The against-interest reading is therefore supported on grok,
mainly by which advocate's side loses (within verdicts that blame the writer
alone, and within those that blame the other party alone, $\phi$ is $-0.34$
and $-0.16$), and not established elsewhere. On Mistral the counter fires on
85 percent of verdicts that blame one party.
Most objections are acceptances with a stated modification rather than
rejections on every model but grok. Rejections make up 90 percent of grok's
objections and 3 percent of Mistral's, 7 percent of Llama's, 18 percent of
haiku's and 19 percent of DeepSeek's, so Mistral's seats reject the
synthesis on only 2.5 percent of their responses while objecting on 81
percent.
On verdicts that blame one party the flag has precision 0.39 and recall 0.43
on grok, so it misses 47 of the 83 wrong verdicts, catches 30 of 257 wrong
verdicts on Llama (precision 0.47, recall 0.12), where even unflagged
verdicts are wrong 0.31 of the time, and reaches recall 0.97 on Mistral
(precision 0.13) only because Mistral floods, with two or more seats
objecting on 88 percent of its debates and 85 percent of these verdicts, so
there the counter is close to a constant (Table~\ref{tab-routing}).
A verdict that blames both parties is scored as at fault and one that blames
no one as not at fault, against a crowd label that is one or the other, and
the collective is right on few such verdicts, 0.536 of 390 on grok, 0.532
of 412 with the exchange cut, 0.397 of 63 on haiku, 0.097 of 93 on Llama,
0.409 of 257 on DeepSeek and 0.433 of 254 on Mistral, against 0.936, 0.930,
0.922, 0.680, 0.943 and 0.884 on verdicts that blame one party.
The error's direction differs by model. On Llama the collective is right on
0.365 of the debates where the crowd found the writer not at fault and 0.989
where it did, so it blames the writer on 699 of 897 debates, 78 percent,
against the crowd's 366, 41 percent, while grok errs more on at-fault items
(0.878 against 0.791) and haiku, DeepSeek and Mistral, like Llama, more on
not-at-fault items (0.808 against 0.923, 0.686 against 0.916 and 0.676
against 0.864).
The routing gain of Section~\ref{sec-collective} splits by verdict type.
Under the sonnet judge of Table~\ref{tab-routing} the counter raises
accuracy on grok's verdicts that blame one party from 0.936 to 0.963, a net
35 of 1,287 debates and 2.1 of the 7.0 points, and on verdicts that blame
both parties or neither from 0.536 to 0.749, a net 83 of 390 debates and 4.9
points, while firing on 235 of those 390.
The registered verdict-type router, which sends every verdict that blames
both or neither to the judge with no dissent signal, composes at 0.936
against the counter's 0.913, $+2.3$ points $[+0.4, +4.3]$ under sonnet and
$+1.4$ $[-0.3, +3.0]$ under haiku, and the union of the two signals
composes at 0.956, above the verdict type by $+2.1$ $[+1.1, +3.3]$ under

Sonnet is the judge in every routed number of Section~\ref{sec-collective}

\subsection{Pre-registration, deviations and corrections}
\label{app-prereg}

Most results in this paper are read from pre-registered analyses. The
exceptions are the original four-model single-agent comparison and the
false-premise contrast on nano, whose responses predate the pre-registration,
and the readouts the text labels exploratory or observed rather than declared
in advance, and several registered numbers were corrected or withdrawn along
the way. This subsection records each correction under the heading of the
pre-registration entry that made it.

\paragraph{Practice and scope.}
The pre-registration is one append-only document begun on 31 August 2026.
Each addendum states its hypothesis, estimator, spending ceiling, stop rule,
guards and pre-declared readings before any model call, results are appended
below it, and results blocks end with the statement that nothing above them
is edited. Registered guards, typically shares of truncated or verdict-less
outputs of at most 5 percent, must pass before any number is read, and a run
that fails is not read. The single-agent guards registered before Addendum
17.7 did not check truncation during generation, and until the audit behind
Addendum 17.10 the record noted it only for grok's chain-of-thought responses
and nano's empty ones, which is how most of the defect of
Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k} went unrecorded.
The CoT and NoT responses of the four original generators (haiku, sonnet,
nano and grok) were generated in June 2026 for an earlier study and their
scoring was corrected in August, both before the document existed, so their
drops enter it only as a zero-spend results note that makes no new claim
(Addendum 16.15.7).
Prospectively registered evidence for the single-agent validation result
begins with Addendum 17 on 21 September 2026, which added Llama, Mistral and

\paragraph{The one-loser stratum (Addenda 16.12 and 16.15.1).}
The split of the dissent counter by verdict type, which supplies the 9.8
times figure of Section~\ref{sec-collective}, was not declared before the
grok debates existed. It was proposed on 12 September 2026 in a zero-spend
note (Addendum 16.12) that predicted and then confirmed on the already
collected grok panel that verdicts blaming both parties or neither flood the
counter, and the within-one-loser lift was read the same day under Addendum
16.15.1, a zero-spend audit registered in answer to a hostile-reviewer
finding, whose registration records the auditor's replay values (0.387
against 0.039 over 93 flagged debates) in brackets before the readout and
whose reading rule requires this paper to say that the pooled lift, 4.4
times ($+32.0$ points $[+23.9, +40.4]$ over 328 flagged debates of 1,677),
mixes the dissent on one-loser verdicts with the verdict type on the rest.
On grok the stratum is therefore a reading proposed after the data were
seen. The prospective test is the five later conditions, the cut exchange
(16.25), haiku (16.23), Llama (16.24), Mistral (16.26) and DeepSeek (16.27),
each registered with the within-one-loser lift as a named readout before
any call, and on haiku the interval includes zero.

\paragraph{Judge input truncation (August 2026 correction and Addendum 16.15.7).}
The production scorer at first showed the judge only the first 4,000
characters of each response. Far more NoT than CoT responses exceed that
length, on haiku 121 of 150 against 0 of 150 and on sonnet 140 of 150 against
5 of 150, and NoT places its decision last, so the cut removed the closing
affirmation and inflated the published reductions.
Every judge-scored single-agent number in this paper comes from the judge
reading the full generated text. The
registered four-model drops substituted fresh full-text scores only for
responses over 4,000 characters and kept stored scores for shorter ones, and
the uniform full-text rescore of Addendum 17.8 reproduces them to within 3.5
points, the largest gap being nano's, where the panel pairs 116 items against
the registered unpaired comparison (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}). A validation
claim on ELEPHANT's own split of forum posts judged at fault, the split the
panel of Section~\ref{sec-collective} does not use, did not survive the
correction, collapsing on haiku and reversing sign on sonnet, and is
withdrawn.

\paragraph{Correction to Addenda 17.2 and 17.6.}
The first alternate-judge tables, with nano and Llama judges in 17.2 and a
grok judge in 17.6, re-scored with the new judge only the responses over
4,000 characters and left shorter ones at the production judge's score.
Because the share over that length differs sharply between CoT and NoT, each
such drop compared a mostly haiku-scored CoT rate with a mostly
alternate-scored NoT rate, most extremely on haiku and sonnet, the two
generators on which both the Llama and the grok judge appeared to reverse the
sign (the Llama judge also appeared to reverse it on nano).
Both tables are withdrawn as evidence either way, together with a diagnosis
that the Llama and grok judges had an acquiescence defect, and the five-judge
panel of Appendix~\ref{app-judges}, in which every judge scores every
response, replaces them.
The panel's rule, a negative drop with an interval excluding zero on at
least six of seven generators under every judge, was registered after the
withdrawal, and the withdrawn comparison's stricter rule, every generator
under every judge, reads the panel the same way, as judge-dependent.
That panel still finds rises with intervals above
zero on sonnet and nano under the Llama judge and on haiku and sonnet under
the grok judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}), smaller than the withdrawn
tables showed except on nano under the Llama judge ($+13.8$ against $+11.7$).

\paragraph{Addendum 17.8 and the pre-spend amendment.}
As registered, 17.8 would have excluded any judge whose validation rate
exceeded 0.85 on at least 80 percent of the generator-by-prompt rates or whose
rates spanned less than 0.20. The pre-spend review removed this screen as
circular, since it is computed on the rates whose difference is tested and
would exclude exactly the judges that see no reduction, and applied as written
it would have excluded the Llama judge, whose rates on all scored responses
span 0.17, though not on the paired rates, which span 0.21.
The registered threshold for JUDGE-ROBUST, a negative drop excluding zero on
at least six of seven generators under every judge, is looser than the rule
17.2 had registered, which required every generator, and it was written after
the now withdrawn 17.2 table had shown sonnet failing under nano. Under either
rule the reading is JUDGE-DEPENDENT. The reference-label check was registered
as amendment 2 after the five drop tables were seen and before it was
computed.

\paragraph{Addendum 17.7 amendment 2 and Addendum 17.10.}
A sourced audit of the record found two confounds in the single-agent result
itself. The original responses had been cut at their generation caps far more
often than the record stated (Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}), so the claim that
every response was scored in full was true of the judge's input and not of the
responses, and 17.10 regenerated all seven generators at 4,096 tokens.
The same audit found that DeepSeek never received the 8,192-token completion
floor its 17.1 registration said it carried, because the list of floored
deployments in the generation code is empty, so its original responses ran at

The second confound is the persona line. CoT and NoT differ in it as well as
in the five sections, and no earlier condition isolated it. The section
knockouts and the checklist keep NoT's persona while the one-line and
length-matched prompts keep CoT's, so comparisons across those tests also
differ in persona, and amendment 2 added CoT with only the persona swapped
(Appendix~\ref{app-form}).

\subsection{Models and cost}
\label{app-cost}

Seven generators from six developers, three of them open-weight, and one
further judge appear in this paper (Table~\ref{tab-app-models}). Every call
went through Microsoft Azure, so six vendors here means six model developers,
not six API providers, and open-weight status is as recorded in the
pre-registration and was not independently verified.
Decoding follows each provider's route. The Anthropic models run at the
provider's default temperature with no seed and no extended thinking, gpt-4o
and the three open-weight deployments at temperature 0.7, grok with no
temperature or seed, and nano with a reasoning-effort setting and a floor of
8,192 completion tokens on every call after 21 August 2026.
The judges return 0 or 1, the production judge within 8 output tokens at the
provider's default temperature, so repeated judge calls on the same text can
disagree, which is why every comparison in Appendices~\ref{app-judges}
and~\ref{app-rep4k} and the form, persona and length tests of
Appendix~\ref{app-form} score both conditions through the same path with the
same judge, while the section-knockout and one-line tests keep stored scores
for responses of up to 4,000 characters and use fresh full-text scores above
that.
Measured cost figures apply the list prices in Table~\ref{tab-app-models} to
token counts read from the caches after each run, and the estimated figures
are identified below.
