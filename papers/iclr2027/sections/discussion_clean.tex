\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec-discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
On judged validation and in the collective the evidence supports the
scaffold's principle (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}) more weakly than
hypothesised and, on validation, depending on the judge. It does not
support our hypothesis, recorded in the name Narration-of-Thought, that a
first-person narrative of the missing perspectives lowers sycophancy.
Pooled over four generators and under every judge, a third-person
checklist of NoT's five sections lowers judged validation further below CoT
than NoT does, and narration without the sections raises it
above CoT (Table~\ref{tab-form-main}), though part of the checklist's effect
may be a cooler register, inseparable here from less endorsement.
In the collective, a verdict blaming one party is wrong more often when a
second seat objects, on four of five models, an analysis proposed after
grok's debates were collected.
Those objections, however, come from assigned opposition,
which on grok costs accuracy, three neutral seats scoring higher, in a
contrast not pre-registered.
CoT seats make the collective more accurate than NoT seats on grok and Llama.
The collective is no better a decider than the solo, worse on four of five
models (Table~\ref{tab-routing}) and indistinguishable on haiku, but a
sensor of its own errors. Routing raises its accuracy on all five but
beats the solo only on Mistral, and 4.9 of its 7.0 points on grok come from
verdicts blaming both parties or neither, where objections carry no
detectable error signal.

\paragraph{Judges and judge-free tests.}
NoT's reduction holds on all seven generators only under the production
judge, haiku, and under every judge only on Llama, while on sonnet, whose
responses haiku could favour as its developer's, the other four reverse it
in the regeneration (Section~\ref{sec-single}).
No judge can separate register from endorsement, but the judge-free
pushback test measures commitment, keeping a verdict when asked to
reconsider, not register.
There NoT gives up its verdict more often than CoT whether the user
disputes it or asks again, carried almost entirely by Llama, and the
registered primary measure, the first rise minus the second, is null
because the rises are alike, not absent.
In a second judge-free test, against the brief unscaffolded prompt, not
CoT, NoT does not remove the verdict's shift toward the user on Llama and
Mistral, and DeepSeek shows it under neither prompt.

\paragraph{Other limitations and future work.}
Each generator answered each item once per prompt per run, and our
intervals resample items, not runs, so only its two to four runs, compared
post hoc (Table~\ref{tab-app-runs}), show how far the reduction moves.
It holds under the production judge in every run but sonnet's second
4,096-token run, and the regeneration shrinks it, for example, on sonnet
under the production judge and on Mistral and DeepSeek under gpt-4o.
Content filters on most platforms refuse some disputes, so collective
and pushback item sets differ by model.
The decisive test of form against content would cross first- and
third-person voice with narrative and checklist form and have blind human
raters score endorsement apart from register.
A second is whether the scaffold's content in either form resists
multi-turn pushback \citep{hong2025sycophancy}.
A third is whether dissent flags errors where
role-lock is weak, as on haiku, and routes them better than the solo's
sample disagreement,
which routed errors at least as well everywhere but Mistral,
in comparisons registered only on grok.
