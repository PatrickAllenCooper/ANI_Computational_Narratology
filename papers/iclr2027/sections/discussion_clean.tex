\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec-discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
On judged validation and in the collective, the two settings in which we
separated the scaffold's content from its form, the evidence supports its
principle, organised criticism across perspectives, only in a weaker form
than we set out to test, and not its narrative packaging.
Pooled over haiku, grok, nano and Llama, a third-person
checklist of the same five elements lowers validation relative to
chain-of-thought by 36.5 to 57.5 points under each of the five judges, more
than NoT does on each generator under every judge, while the first-person
narrative instruction alone raises it by 6.8 to 18.7 points. Because the
rubric scores emotional validation, the checklist may score lower partly for
a cooler register, which this design cannot separate from less endorsement.
In the collective, on grok, the only model whose roles were varied, the
objections routing uses come from assigned opposition, which in a contrast
not declared in advance costs 2.1 points of accuracy against identical
neutral readers. Plain chain-of-thought seats beat narrated ones on both
models tested, and on the five models of Table~\ref{tab-routing} the
collective is, descriptively, at or below the same model answering alone,
which the routed system beats only on haiku and Mistral.

\paragraph{Judges and the construct.}
The single-agent reduction depends on the judge. It holds on seven, six,
five, one and two of the seven generators under the production, gpt-4o,
gpt-5.4-nano, Llama and grok judges, and under all five only on the Llama
generator.
The two judges that see little of it rate most responses as validating
whatever the prompt and agree least, by point estimate, with the benchmark's
labels (Section~\ref{sec-single}).
The production judge is also a generator and shares its developer with
sonnet, and no other judge confirms sonnet's reduction, which all four
reverse in the regenerated responses.
No judge panel can separate register from endorsement, but a judge-free test
on the crowd-labelled disputes of Section~\ref{sec-collective} separates
register from commitment, and there the scaffold does not help. Under
pushback \citep{fanous2025syceval}, NoT, pooled over nano, Llama and grok,
gives way more often than chain-of-thought (0.37 against 0.22), even where the
crowd found the user at fault, but it also changes its verdict more when
merely asked again, and net of that the difference, $+5.2$ points
$[-1.7, +12.2]$, is indistinguishable from zero.
On Llama and Mistral NoT also keeps the verdict's shift toward the user as
writer, and DeepSeek shows none, so the judged reduction does not extend to
resisting social pressure on a verdict.

\paragraph{Other limitations.}
Each generator answered each item once per prompt in each run. Token caps
cut off many original generations, and the two or three runs per generator,
the later ones at caps almost no response reaches, compared after the fact
(Appendix~\ref{app-rep4k}), keep the production judge's reduction with an
interval excluding zero on every generator in every run but shrink it on
sonnet, and under gpt-4o on Mistral and DeepSeek, while the Llama judge's
reversal on nano does not recur in either same-day run.
Content filters removed disputes each platform refuses, so the collective
and pushback panels differ across models.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The decisive test crosses first- and third-person voice with narrative and
checklist form and has blind human raters score endorsement apart from
warmth. Another is whether any form of the content resists pushback over
several turns \citep{hong2025sycophancy}, with repeated samples per item. A
third is whether dissent flags errors where seats lock weakly to their sides,
as it did not detectably on haiku, and routes them better than a model's own
sample disagreement \citep{manakul2023selfcheck}, which matched or beat it
everywhere but Mistral in mostly exploratory readouts
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Until then, on judged validation and in the collective, the evidence supports
the principle of organised criticism across perspectives
\citep{longino1990science} only in this weaker, judge-dependent form, and not
the narrative that first carried it.
