\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec-discussion}

\paragraph{What the evidence supports.}
On judged validation and in the collective the evidence supports the
scaffold's principle more weakly than
hypothesised and, on validation, depending on the judge. It does not
support our hypothesis, recorded in the name Narration-of-Thought, that a
first-person narrative of the missing perspectives lowers sycophancy,
which premise acceptance alone partly supports.
Pooled over four generators, the checklist lowers
validation more than NoT under every judge and narration alone raises
it above CoT, yet each
gives most of NoT's drop in premise acceptance,
NoT lowering it 4 to 8 points further under both judges scoring it and,
unlike either, below the rate of human answers, so part of
the checklist's larger validation drop may be register.
The benchmark's instruction not to validate lowers validation more than NoT
under every judge and than the checklist under three, but leaves premise
acceptance where CoT has it, while our sceptical persona lowers
validation more than NoT under four judges and premise acceptance more than
NoT and the checklist (Table~\ref{tab-app-baselines}).
In the collective, a one-party verdict is wrong more often when a
second seat objects, on four of five models, an analysis proposed after
grok's debates were collected.
The objections come from assigned opposition, which costs grok accuracy
against three neutral seats in a contrast not pre-registered.
CoT seats make the collective more accurate than NoT seats on grok and Llama,
and its routing more accurate on haiku when both route equally often.
The collective is worse than the solo on four of five models
(Table~\ref{tab-routing}), and routing, which raises its accuracy on all
five, beats the solo only on Mistral, 4.9 of its 7.0 points on grok coming from verdicts blaming both
parties or neither, where objections carry no detectable error signal.

\paragraph{Judges, judge-free tests, limitations and future work.}
NoT's validation drop, pooled over runs, holds on all seven only under the
production judge and under every judge only on Llama, and on sonnet,
whose responses haiku could favour as its developer's, the other four reverse
it, pooled and by point estimate in every run, though NoT lowers premise
acceptance on all seven under both judges scoring it (Section~\ref{sec-single}),
a rubric on which the production judge agrees only moderately with the
benchmark's labels (kappa 0.44, \citealp{cohen60}).
In the judge-free pushback test of commitment, not register,
NoT gives up its verdict more often than CoT whether the user
disputes it or asks again, almost entirely on Llama, and the
registered primary measure, the first rise minus the second, is null
because the rises are alike, not absent.
In a second judge-free test, against the brief unscaffolded prompt, not
CoT, NoT does not remove the verdict's shift toward the user on Llama and
Mistral, and DeepSeek shows none to remove.
Each generator answered each item once per prompt and run, and intervals
resample items, not runs, so only its two to four runs
(Table~\ref{tab-app-runs}, compared post hoc) show how far the reduction moves,
while the form and baseline prompts ran once.
It holds under the production judge in all runs but sonnet's second
4,096-token run, and the first 4,096-token run shrinks it on some generators, by up
to 13 points.
Most platforms' content filters refuse some disputes, so collective
and pushback items vary by model.
The decisive test would cross voice with form and have blind human raters
score endorsement apart from register.
A second is whether NoT or the checklist resists multi-turn pushback
\citep{hong2025sycophancy}.
A third is whether dissent flags errors where
role-lock is weak, as on haiku, and routes them better than the solo's
sample disagreement,
which did at least as well everywhere but Mistral,
in comparisons registered only on grok.
