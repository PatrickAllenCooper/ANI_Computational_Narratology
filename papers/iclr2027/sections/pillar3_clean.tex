\section{Collective Judgement}
\label{sec-collective}

The collective places the scaffold's principle across opposed agents but is
less accurate than the solo on four of five models, on which its dissent
flags its own errors, as it does on grok with CoT seats.

\paragraph{Instrument and protocol.}
Disputes are Scruples Anecdotes \citep{lourie2021scruples}, first-person
conflict accounts whose crowd verdict, a community judgement, not a ground
truth, is the reference when at least 90 percent of at least 50
votes agree in blaming the writer alone or the other party alone, and
accuracy is agreement with it.
ELEPHANT's set of such posts lacks vote counts, and this study's claim
that NoT lowers judged validation there is withdrawn
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The set has 249 items, 99 with the writer at fault, less those whose
single-agent NoT answers content filters refused, each posed under two
framings, the third person and the user as the writer, and each item and
framing pair, a case, is debated four times on grok, twice on Llama, Mistral
and DeepSeek and once on haiku (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Over seven stages in 17 calls (Table~\ref{tab-app-stages}), in which three
copies of one model, one advocate per party and a neutral seat, exchange
statements, a fourth copy, a moderator with no side, synthesises their final
positions and, after each seat labels that synthesis, writes the integrated
verdict scored as the collective's.
A verdict blaming the writer alone or both parties counts as
at fault, one blaming the other party alone or no one as not at fault.
A seat objects when it rejects the synthesis or accepts it with a
modification, an objection counter fires, flagging the debate, when at
least two seats object, and routing, to test whether the flag merits
action, replaces a flagged debate's verdict with the majority, if any, of
sonnet's three answers to the case without the debate.

\paragraph{The collective is a poor decider.}
In a paired comparison not pre-registered, the collective's accuracy is
below the solo's on four of five models (Table~\ref{tab-routing}), by
6.9 to 22.2 points, intervals excluding zero, and indistinguishable from it
on haiku ($-0.6$ $[-4.7, +3.5]$), while two unpaired comparisons from earlier
runs favour the collective.
Role-lock, measured as the gap between the advocates' opening at-fault rates, is 0.97
on grok and 0.42 on haiku, the other three between. Llama's collective errs
one way, toward blaming the writer.
Opposition itself costs accuracy, a collective of three neutral seats
scoring $+2.1$ points $[+0.4, +3.8]$ above the opposed one on grok, post hoc
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).

\paragraph{Dissent flags errors.}
On grok's integrated verdicts blaming one party the collective is wrong on 0.387 of
debates where a second seat objects against 0.039 where at most one does,
9.8 times as often, a lift in error rate of $+34.8$ points $[+20.1, +49.3]$,
against 4.4 times pooled over all verdicts.
A verdict blaming one party gives that party's advocate a reason to
object, so the informative objection is a second one no assigned side
explains \citep{milgrom1986relying,lipman1995robust}, which the
two-objection threshold, not a learned rule \citep{he2026minoritysentinel},
approximates.
On verdicts blaming both parties or neither (accuracy 0.536 on 390
grok debates against 0.936 for one-party verdicts), the flag shows no
detectable lift, so the pooled figure mixes dissent with verdict
type, itself a second, cheaper error signal. This split, like the
threshold, was proposed and first read on grok's debates after
collection, so Table~\ref{tab-routing}'s other five rows test them
prospectively.
The lift replicates, 5.1 times ($+22.6$ points) on grok with the exchange
cut (testing whether the flag needs it), 6.2 ($+11.1$) on Mistral, where the counter fires on 88 percent of
debates and is close to a constant, 2.3 ($+5.7$) on DeepSeek and 1.5
($+16.2$) on Llama, each interval excluding zero. On haiku the interval
includes zero (1.5 times, $+3.5$ points $[-4.1, +12.0]$) and rules out a
lift of grok's or Llama's size in points but not Mistral's or DeepSeek's.
The advocates' objections are strongly opposed on grok alone, each objecting
mainly when its side loses, so the argument above is supported only there
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).

\paragraph{Routing.}
Routing improves the collective on every row of Table~\ref{tab-routing}, by
$+7.0$ points $[+4.8, +9.4]$ on grok with 19.6 percent of debates routed, and
holds with DeepSeek in sonnet's place, post hoc
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Most of the 7.0 points, 4.9 against 2.1 from one-party verdicts, come from
verdicts blaming both or neither, flagged in 235 of 390 grok debates with no
detectable lift, and sending every such verdict to sonnet beats the counter.
The routed collective detectably beats the solo only on Mistral ($+12.3$
points $[+8.1, +16.8]$), where 88 percent of debates are flagged, is
indistinguishable from it on grok and haiku and below it on Llama and
DeepSeek.
On grok, priced at one debate per case, it costs 93 percent as much as
sonnet answering every case, at list rates on reported tokens, which may
understate grok's cost, for 6.3 points less accuracy.
Routing grok's solo to sonnet where most debates of its case are flagged
gains $+2.4$ points $[+0.5, +4.5]$ over the solo, 4.0 points below sonnet
answering every case.
We therefore find no setting in which the collective is worth building
for accuracy, since sonnet answering every case is above the routed
collective on all five models, the solo is at least as accurate as the
unrouted collective at 3 calls against 17 and routing on its sample
disagreement \citep{manakul2023selfcheck}, a split of its three answers on
fault, does at least as well as the flags on every model but Mistral, and
the contribution is that a poor decider senses its own errors.

\paragraph{NoT seats cost the collective accuracy.}
Changing only the seats' system prompt from NoT to CoT raises
collective accuracy by 2.0 points $[+0.3, +3.7]$ on grok and
4.2 $[+1.4, +7.0]$ on Llama and, with NoT flags thinned at random to
the CoT fire rate so both route equally often, routed
accuracy by 2.0 $[+0.1, +3.8]$ and 4.6 $[+1.2, +7.8]$, reversing the
scaffold's prediction on both registered measures (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}).
NoT seats raise grok's fire rate and so, by 1.6 points with an interval
reaching zero, its routing gain, changing neither detectably
on Llama, both intervals within 2.7 points of zero.
The flag's lift on one-party verdicts stays detectable with CoT seats on
grok (6.4 against 9.8 times) and is not read on Llama, its 53 flagged
debates under the registered minimum of 60, and the seats' prompt changes
neither model's verdict shift toward the user (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}).
Collective accuracy on grok and Llama, and the flag's lift on grok, do
not depend on the scaffold and so are not evidence for it, the collective
sharing only its principle \citep{longino1990science}.
