\section{Collective Judgement}
\label{sec-collective}

The collective, the scaffold's principle placed across agents with opposed
interests (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), is a poor decider whose dissent can
audit its own verdicts. Its verdict is at or below the model
answering alone on five models, a second objection marks where it is more
often wrong on four, routing the flagged cases to a stronger judge lifts it
above the model alone only on Mistral, and plain chain-of-thought seats
outperform narrating ones on both models tested.

\paragraph{Instrument and protocol.}
Disputes are Scruples Anecdotes \citep{lourie2021scruples}, first-person
conflict accounts whose crowd verdict is gold when at least 90 percent of at
least 50 votes agree.
ELEPHANT's own split of such posts has no vote counts, so it admits no
consensus rule, and our earlier validation claim on it was withdrawn
(Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The panel has 249 items, less those each platform's content filter refuses,
each posed under two framings, the third person and the user as the writer,
and each item and framing pair is debated four times on grok, twice on
Llama, Mistral and DeepSeek and once on haiku
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Three copies of one model take seats, an advocate for each party and a
neutral adjudicator, a fourth copy with no side moderates, and over seven
stages in 17 calls the moderator's integrated verdict, written after each
seat accepts, accepts with a stated modification or rejects its synthesis,
is scored as the collective's. A verdict blaming the writer alone or both
parties counts as at fault, one blaming the other party alone or no one as
not at fault.
A seat objects when it rejects the synthesis or accepts it with a
modification, the counter fires when at least two seats object, and routing
sends the flagged debates to sonnet, a stronger judge that sees only the
dispute and answers by majority of three, ties going to the collective.

\paragraph{The collective is a sensor, not a decider.}
In a pairing computed after review the collective's verdict is at or below
the same model answering alone on the five models of
Table~\ref{tab-routing}, collective minus solo $-6.9$ to $-22.2$
points with intervals excluding zero on all but haiku ($-0.6$
$[-4.7, +3.5]$), while two earlier comparisons outside the table, on nano
and a 100-item haiku panel, point the other way.
The seats lock to their sides, the advocates' opening at-fault rates
differing by 0.97 on grok, 0.86 on Llama, 0.82 on DeepSeek, 0.71 on Mistral
and 0.42 on haiku, and on Llama the error is one-sided, blaming the
writer on 78 percent of debates against the crowd's 41 percent
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Opposition itself costs accuracy, three identical neutral readers scoring
$+2.1$ points $[+0.4, +3.8]$ above the opposed seats on grok in a contrast
not declared in advance.

\paragraph{Dissent flags errors.}
A verdict that blames one party gives that party's advocate a reason to
object, so the informative objection is a second one, which no assigned
side explains \citep{milgrom1986relying,lipman1995robust}, and the counter
is a fixed threshold, not a learned rule \citep{he2026minoritysentinel}.
On grok's verdicts that blame one party the collective is wrong on 0.387 of
the debates where a second seat objects against 0.039 where at most one
does, 9.8 times as often, a lift of $+34.8$ points $[+20.1, +49.3]$,
against 4.4 times pooled over all its verdicts.
On verdicts that blame both parties or neither, right on only 0.536 of 390
grok debates against 0.936 for one-party verdicts, the counter shows no
detectable lift, so the pooled figure mixes the dissent with the verdict
type, itself the cheaper error signal, and this split was proposed and first
read on the grok panel after its debates were collected, so the other five
conditions are its prospective test.
The lift replicates, 5.1 times on grok with the edges (the seats' exchange)
cut, 6.2 on Mistral, where the counter fires on 88 percent of debates and
is close to a constant, 2.3 on DeepSeek and 1.5 on Llama, each interval
excluding zero, while on haiku, whose seats lock least,
the interval includes zero (1.5 times, $+3.5$ points $[-4.1, +12.0]$), and
the advocates' objections are opposed on grok alone, so the against-interest
mechanism is supported only there (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).

\paragraph{Routing.}
Routing the flagged debates to the judge raises accuracy over the collective
on all six conditions of Table~\ref{tab-routing}, by $+7.0$ points
$[+4.8, +9.4]$ on grok with 19.6 percent of debates routed, and holds with
DeepSeek as the judge in an unregistered readout
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Of the 7.0 points, 2.1 come from verdicts that blame one party and 4.9 from
those that blame both or neither, where the dissent shows no detectable
lift and a router on the verdict type alone does better than the counter
under the sonnet judge.
The routed collective beats the solo with an interval excluding zero only
on Mistral ($+12.3$ points $[+8.1, +16.8]$), matches it on grok and haiku,
and stays below it on Llama and DeepSeek.
The routed collective on grok costs 93 percent of sonnet answering every
case (\$0.0107 against \$0.0115 per case at list rates) for 6.3 points less
accuracy.
Routing grok's solo verdicts by the flags lifts them by $+2.4$ points
$[+0.5, +4.5]$, but the solo's sample disagreement
\citep{manakul2023selfcheck} routes as well and sonnet answering every case
is 4.0 points above the routed solo (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).

\paragraph{Narration costs the collective accuracy.}
Replacing each seat's narration with plain chain-of-thought, all else fixed,
gives the collective higher accuracy on both models tested, by 2.0 points
$[+0.3, +3.7]$ on grok and 4.2 $[+1.4, +7.0]$ on Llama, which the registered
rule reads as a reversal (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}).
On grok narrated seats fire the counter more often (0.195 against 0.152
averaged over items) and so gain more from routing, while on Llama
narration changes neither detectably, and at equal coverage the plain-seat
system is more accurate after routing by 2.0 points $[+0.1, +3.8]$ on grok
and 4.6 $[+1.2, +7.8]$ on Llama.
The sensor's survival without narration is shown on grok alone (6.4 against
9.8 times on one-party verdicts), since on Llama that readout had 53 flagged
debates, under the registered minimum of 60, and is unread, and narration
changes neither model's tendency to excuse a writer at fault when the user
is the writer.
The collective's accuracy on both models tested, and its sensor on grok, do
not depend on the scaffold, so the multi-agent result is not evidence for
it, only the external form of the principle it places inside one trace
\citep{longino1990science}.
