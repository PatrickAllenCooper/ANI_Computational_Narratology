\section{Collective Judgement}
\label{sec-collective}

The collective, the scaffold's principle placed across agents with opposed
interests (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), decides worse than the solo on four
of five models, and on those four its
dissent flags its own errors, on grok with or without the scaffold in its
seats.

\paragraph{Instrument and protocol.}
Disputes are Scruples Anecdotes \citep{lourie2021scruples}, first-person
conflict accounts whose crowd verdict, a community judgement of blame rather
than a ground truth, is the reference when at least 90 percent of at least 50
votes agree that the writer alone, or the other party alone, is at fault, and
accuracy is agreement with it.
ELEPHANT's own set of such posts has no vote counts, so it admits no
consensus rule, and a validation claim we made on it in this study was
withdrawn (Appendix~\ref{app-prereg}).
The set has 249 items, less those whose single-agent answers each
platform's content filter refused, each posed under two framings, the third
person and the user as the writer, and each item and framing pair is debated
four times on grok, twice on Llama, Mistral and DeepSeek and once on haiku
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Three copies of one model, each under the NoT system prompt, take seats, an
advocate for each party and a neutral seat, a fourth copy with no side
moderates, and over seven stages in 17 calls (Appendix~\ref{app-prompts})
the moderator's integrated verdict, written after each seat accepts, accepts
with a stated modification or rejects the synthesis, is scored as the
collective's. A verdict blaming the writer alone or both
parties counts as at fault, one blaming the other party alone or no one as
not at fault.
A seat objects when it rejects the synthesis or accepts it with a
modification, the counter fires, flagging the debate, when at least two
seats object, and routing replaces a flagged debate's verdict with the
majority of three answers that sonnet, a stronger model, gave to the framed
account without the debate, unless they tie or none parse.

\paragraph{The collective is a sensor, not a decider.}
In a paired comparison not pre-registered, the collective's accuracy is
below the solo's on four of the five models of Table~\ref{tab-routing}, by
6.9 to 22.2 points with intervals excluding zero, and indistinguishable from
it on haiku ($-0.6$ $[-4.7, +3.5]$), while two earlier comparisons not in
the table point the other way (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
The seats lock to their sides, the two advocates' opening rates
of blaming the writer differing by 0.97 on grok and 0.42 on haiku with the
other three models between, and Llama's error is one-sided, toward blaming
the writer (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Opposition itself costs accuracy, three seats all given the neutral brief
scoring $+2.1$ points $[+0.4, +3.8]$ above the opposed seats on grok in a
contrast not pre-registered.

\paragraph{Dissent flags errors.}
A verdict blaming one party gives that party's advocate a reason to
object, so the informative objection is a second one no assigned side
explains \citep{milgrom1986relying,lipman1995robust}, and the counter
is a fixed threshold, not the learned rule of \citet{he2026minoritysentinel}.
On grok's verdicts that blame one party the collective is wrong on 0.387 of
debates where a second seat objects against 0.039 where at most one does,
9.8 times as often, a lift in error rate of $+34.8$ points $[+20.1, +49.3]$,
against 4.4 times pooled over all verdicts.
On verdicts that blame both parties or neither, right on 0.536 of 390
grok debates against 0.936 for one-party verdicts, the counter shows no
detectable lift, so the pooled figure mixes the dissent with the verdict
type, itself a second, cheaper error signal. This split, like the
two-objection threshold, was proposed and first read on grok's debates after
they were collected, so the other five rows of Table~\ref{tab-routing} are
their prospective test.
The lift replicates, 5.1 times ($+22.6$ points) on grok with the exchange
cut, 6.2 ($+11.1$) on Mistral, where the counter fires on 88 percent of
debates and is close to a constant, 2.3 ($+5.7$) on DeepSeek and 1.5
($+16.2$) on Llama, each interval excluding zero. On haiku the interval
includes zero (1.5 times, $+3.5$ points $[-4.1, +12.0]$) and rules out a
lift of grok's or Llama's size in points but not Mistral's or DeepSeek's.
The advocates' objections are strongly opposed on grok alone, so the
mechanism is supported only there (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).

\paragraph{Routing.}
Routing the flagged debates to sonnet lifts the collective on every row of
Table~\ref{tab-routing}, by $+7.0$ points $[+4.8, +9.4]$ on grok with 19.6
percent of debates routed, and holds with DeepSeek in sonnet's place in a
readout not pre-registered (Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
Of the 7.0 points, 2.1 come from verdicts that blame one party and 4.9 from
those that blame both or neither, where the dissent shows no detectable
lift and sending every such verdict to sonnet does better than the
counter.
The routed collective beats the solo with an interval excluding zero only
on Mistral ($+12.3$ points $[+8.1, +16.8]$), is indistinguishable from it
on grok and haiku, and stays below it on Llama and DeepSeek.
On grok it costs 93 percent as much as sonnet answering every case, at list
rates on reported tokens, which may understate grok's, for 6.3 points less
accuracy.
Routing grok's solo verdict to sonnet where most debates on its item and
framing are flagged lifts it by $+2.4$ points $[+0.5, +4.5]$, but its own
sample disagreement \citep{manakul2023selfcheck} routes at least as well,
and sonnet answering alone is above the routed collective on all five
models and 4.0 points above the routed solo on grok
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
We therefore find no setting in which the collective is worth building
for accuracy, since the solo is at least as accurate at 3 calls against 17
and its own sample disagreement routes its errors at least as well on every
model but Mistral, and the contribution is the mechanism finding stated
above.

\paragraph{Narration costs the collective accuracy.}
Changing only the seats' system prompt from NoT to CoT raises the collective's
accuracy, by 2.0 points $[+0.3, +3.7]$ on grok and 4.2 $[+1.4, +7.0]$
on Llama, a reversal under the registered rule (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}).
On grok narrated seats fire the counter more often and so gain more from
routing, by 1.6 points with an interval reaching zero, on Llama neither rate
nor gain changes detectably, both intervals within 2.7 points of zero, and,
with narrated flags thinned at random to the plain fire rate, plain seats
give higher routed accuracy on both models, by 2.0 and 4.6 points.
The sensor survives without narration on grok (6.4 against 9.8 times on
one-party verdicts) and is not read on Llama, its 53 flagged debates under
the registered minimum of 60, and narration changes neither model's
tendency to excuse a writer at fault who is the user.
The collective's accuracy on grok and Llama, and its sensor on grok, do
not depend on the scaffold and so are not evidence for it, the collective
being only its principle's external form \citep{longino1990science}.
