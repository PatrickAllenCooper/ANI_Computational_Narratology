\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}

Large language models agree with their users more than the evidence
warrants, a trained bias called sycophancy
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}, which in personal advice takes the
form \citet{cheng25elephant} call social sycophancy, validating the person
asking.
% cheng25elephant, abstract page https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13995 fetched 2026-09-23 (title "ELEPHANT:
% Measuring and understanding social sycophancy in LLMs"; social sycophancy is "excessive preservation of
% a user's face", set against agreement with a user's explicitly stated beliefs; ELEPHANT is a benchmark)
The social
account of objectivity locates objectivity in organised criticism across
perspectives, not in one reasoner
\citep{longino1990science,popper2011open}, and a model answering one person's
account hears only that person's side. We introduce
Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a lightweight prompt scaffold asking the model to write the missing perspectives
into its reasoning as a first-person narrative in five sections, the decision-maker, the
stakeholders, the consequences of each action, what remains uncertain and a
decision (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), and compare it with standard chain-of-thought \citep[CoT,][]{wei22,kojima22}.
% five sections: Figure fig:prompt and Section sec-scaffold (scripts/run_phase1_quartet.py PROMPTS["narrative_cot"]);
% the checklist of the next paragraph has the same five items (scripts/scaffold_permutations.py
% _checklist_same_content), so "the same five sections" there has this antecedent

On 150 open-ended advice questions from the ELEPHANT benchmark
\citep{cheng25elephant}, NoT lowers the validation rate, the share
of answers a judge model scores as emotionally validating the asker, on all
seven answering models (generators) under our production judge, the
default scorer claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku), itself one of them, by 13 to 63 percentage points (a relative reduction
of 16 to 80 percent, the fall divided by CoT's rate), but it depends on the judge, holding under all
five judges, from four developers, only on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama)
(Section~\ref{sec-single}).

In a multi-agent setting that we call the collective, three copies
of one model prompted with NoT debate such an account as an advocate for each
party and a neutral seat and accept or object to the verdict a fourth copy
proposes before it writes the final one, scored against the account's
near-unanimous crowd verdict of who is at fault.

The collective is a poor decider, less accurate
than the same model answering alone (the solo) on four
of five models and indistinguishable from it on haiku, but a
sensor of its own errors, since on grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok), among
verdicts blaming one party, which give that party's advocate a reason to
object, it is wrong 9.8 times as often when a second
seat objects as when at most one does, a
finding about mechanism, not a system worth building, as the solo is at
least as accurate at 3 calls against 17.
