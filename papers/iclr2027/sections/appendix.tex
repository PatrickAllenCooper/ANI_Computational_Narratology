\section{Appendix}
\label{app}

This appendix gives the full evidence behind the single-agent validation and
pushback results, the narrated-versus-plain-seat comparison of the collective
and the collective's dissent mechanism and routing against the solo model, and
records every correction and deviation from the pre-registration.
Model names are shortened after first use. claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku)
\citep{anthropic_haiku45} and claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet)
\citep{anthropic_sonnet46} are Anthropic models, gpt-5.4-nano (nano)
\citep{openai_gpt54nano} and gpt-4o \citep{openai_gpt4o,openai_gpt4o_systemcard}
are OpenAI models, grok-4-1-fast-reasoning
(grok) \citep{xai_grok41} is an xAI model, and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama)
\citep{meta_llama33}, Mistral-Large-3-2 (Mistral) \citep{mistral_large3} and
DeepSeek-V4-Pro (DeepSeek) \citep{deepseek_v4pro,deepseek_v4_report} are
open-weight models from Meta, Mistral AI and DeepSeek
(Table~\ref{tab-app-models}).

NoT is the Narration-of-Thought system prompt and CoT the one-line standard
chain-of-thought prompt (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}). The single-agent outcome is
the validation rate, the share of responses a judge model scores as
emotionally validating under the rubric of the ELEPHANT benchmark
\citep{cheng25elephant}, which asks whether a response shows understanding,
validates the person's perspective and expresses care. The items are 150 of
the benchmark's open-ended personal-advice questions, drawn with seed 44.
% prereg Addendum 17 shared instrument (~l.7062) and 17.7 registration (~l.8757-8759, seed 44
% corrects "seed 42"); scripts/load_elephant.py ELEPHANT_SEED = 44; rubric scripts/elephant_scorers.py
% _validation_prompt
A drop is the NoT rate minus the CoT rate in percentage points, so a negative
drop means NoT validates less. Intervals are 95 percent percentile bootstraps
that resample items and keep every response to an item together, with 8,000
draws unless stated.

% 17.8 registration (~l.8719-8721)

\paragraph{Registered readings.}
Each test was registered with a short set of capitalised readings, one of
which the analysis script returns mechanically once the data are in, and the
tables and text of this appendix report the returned reading beside the
numbers. Every reading used below is defined here and nowhere else. For the
judge panel (Appendix~\ref{app-judges}), JUDGE-ROBUST means a negative drop
with an interval excluding zero on at least six of the seven generators under
every judge, and JUDGE-DEPENDENT means anything short of that.
% prereg 17.8 pre-spend amendment (~l.8858-8860) (W45)
For the same-day replicate (Table~\ref{tab-app-rep4k}), HOLDS means a
generator's drop is negative with an interval excluding zero under the
production judge and under at least three of the other four judges, and
JUDGE-DEPENDENT means it is negative with an interval excluding zero under
the production judge but under fewer than three others. The registered names
carry the prefix HEADLINE, which the table omits, and the third registered
reading, FAILS, which would mean the production judge's interval includes
zero or lies above it, is returned on no generator.
% prereg 17.10 registration (~l.8963-8967); scripts/make_iclr_figures.py rep4k_table strips "HEADLINE-";
% headline_rep4k_readout.json per_generator.<gen>.reading (HEADLINE-HOLDS on Llama, HEADLINE-JUDGE-
% DEPENDENT on the other six, no HEADLINE-FAILS)
For the form tests (Table~\ref{tab-app-form}), CHECKLIST-BETTER means NoT
minus the checklist has an interval above zero, CONTENT-ADDS means NoT minus
the narrative-only prompt has an interval below zero, PERSONA-INERT means the
persona swap alone changes validation by an amount whose interval includes
zero, and PERSONA-RAISES means that interval lies above zero.
% prereg 17.7 pre-spend amendment (~l.8845-8849) and 17.7 amendment 2 (~l.8943-8945)
For the length control (Table~\ref{tab-app-length}), LENGTH-INERT means NoT
minus length-matched CoT is below zero with an interval excluding zero on
both generators, LENGTH-CARRIES, which no judge returns, would mean the
length-matched prompt's own drop from CoT has an interval below zero and
reaches half of NoT's drop on either generator, and UNRESOLVED means neither
holds.
% prereg 17.4 pre-spend amendment (~l.8872-8875)
For pushback (Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}), NOT-RESISTS means NoT minus CoT
capitulation is negative with an interval excluding zero, NOT-WORSE means it
is positive with an interval excluding zero, NULL means the interval includes
zero, and FLOOR means CoT's pooled capitulation is under 5 percent so the
contrast is not read.
% prereg 17.9 registration (~l.8803-8805)
For the seat comparison (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}), PLAIN-BETTER means the
collective's own accuracy or its routed accuracy favours plain seats with an
interval excluding zero on either model.
% prereg 16.28 registration (~l.8646-8648; (a) collective accuracy, (b) routed accuracy ~l.8631-8633)
For the one-sentence instruction test recorded in Appendix~\ref{app-prereg},
STRUCTURE-NEEDED is the registered branch for an instruction that produces no
drop from CoT with an interval excluding zero, and REVERSED, a label added
after the run, names a rise with an interval excluding zero.
% prereg 17.5 registration (~l.8005-8007) and RESULTS (~l.8040-8041)

\subsection{Five judges and agreement with the benchmark's labels}
\label{app-judges}

The reduction depends on the judge. When the full generated text of every one
of the 2,004 non-empty CoT and NoT responses is scored by each of five judges,
NoT lowers
validation with an interval excluding zero on all seven generators under the
production judge (haiku), on six under gpt-4o, five under nano, one under the
Llama judge and two under the grok judge (Table~\ref{tab-app-judge-drops}). The pre-declared
reading is therefore JUDGE-DEPENDENT (defined at the head of this appendix),
and only Llama's reduction holds under all five judges.

earlier addenda had used Bonferroni. A Holm step-down over the seven
generators within each judge, computed after review from the cached per-item

survives. The correction removes haiku and nano under gpt-4o,
DeepSeek under the grok judge and, among the rises, sonnet under the Llama
judge, and it changes no registered reading, since JUDGE-ROBUST needs six of
seven under every judge and Llama's reduction survives under all five.

\subsection{Form, content, persona and length}
\label{app-form}

In judged validation the structured content carries the reduction and the
narrative form does not, although part of this may be the checklist's cooler
register (see below). Pooled over haiku, grok, nano and Llama, a third-person
checklist of the same five elements (Figure~\ref{fig-app-prompts}) lowers
validation by 36.5 to 57.5 points depending on the
judge, more than NoT does under every one of the five judges, while the
first-person narrative instruction alone raises validation above CoT under
every judge, by 6.8 to 18.7 points (Table~\ref{tab-app-form}).
% prereg Addenda 17.7, 17.4 and 17.10 RESULTS (~l.9152-9177); narrative_form_readout.json
% judges.<judge>.form_pooled.{checklist,narrative_only}.drop_arm; figures/tab_form.tex
NoT minus the checklist is $+11.4$ $[+7.3, +15.3]$ under the production judge,
$+22.2$ under gpt-4o, $+17.4$ under nano, $+25.7$ under the grok judge and
$+31.4$ $[+27.2, +35.5]$ under the Llama judge.
% narrative_form_readout.json judges.<judge>.form_pooled.checklist.gap; prereg (~l.9165-9167)
The readings CHECKLIST-BETTER (NoT minus checklist with an interval above
zero) and CONTENT-ADDS (NoT minus narrative only with an interval below zero)
therefore hold
under all five judges, which is the registered definition of judge-robust for
these tests, and the checklist lowers validation by about 37 points even under
the Llama and grok judges, under which NoT's own reduction is small ($-5.9$ and
$-11.5$).
% narrative_form_readout.json form_reading and agreement.form_checklist ['CHECKLIST-BETTER'],
% agreement.form_narrative_only ['CONTENT-ADDS']; prereg pre-spend amendment (~l.8840-8852,
% ~l.8862-8864); checklist -37.3 (Llama judge) and -37.2 (grok judge); NoT drop_not -5.9 and -11.5

This result carries a caveat that must travel with it. The rubric scores
emotional validation, meaning understanding, validation of the person's
perspective and care, so a third-person checklist may be judged less
validating partly because its register is cooler, not only because it
endorses the asker's account less, and this design cannot separate the two.
The pushback test of Appendix~\ref{app-pushback}, which needs no judge, asks a
different question, whether a verdict holds when the user objects, and there
NoT does not resist. The checklist also
removes the first person and the narrative genre together, and the design
cannot separate voice from genre either.
% prereg 17.7 amendment (~l.8840-8843) and RESULTS caveat (~l.9177-9184); rubric element "Care"
% in scripts/elephant_scorers.py _validation_prompt. The RESULTS caveat and key_messages.md say the
% pushback test is "where the two come apart"; it measures a different outcome on a different
% instrument, and sections/discussion.tex names the endorsement-versus-warmth test as future work,
% so that wording is not used here (flagged to the PI)

The persona line does not explain the reduction. CoT opens with ``You are a
helpful assistant'' and NoT with ``You are a thoughtful advisor'', so every
