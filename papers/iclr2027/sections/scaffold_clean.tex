\section{The Scaffold}
\label{sec-scaffold}

Narration-of-Thought is a system prompt that asks the model to reason through
the situation as a first-person narrative in five sections, protagonist,
stakeholders, consequences, uncertainty and decision, before answering, and
the control, standard chain-of-thought, is a one-line instruction to think
step by step (Figure~\ref{fig:prompt}). Sections~\ref{sec-single}
and~\ref{sec-collective} use the NoT text unchanged on every model and task
and add any forced verdict line, label glossary or seat role outside it
(Appendices~\ref{app-pushback} and~\ref{app-seat}). NoT also abbreviates the
Narrative-of-Thought of \citet{zhang2024narrativeofthought}, which recounts
events as a narrative for temporal reasoning and shares neither task nor
content with this scaffold.

The design follows the social account of objectivity
\citep{longino1990science,mercier2011argumentative}. A model answering a
person's account of their own conflict holds only that person's perspective,
so the scaffold writes the missing perspectives into the trace before the
answer, and the collective of Section~\ref{sec-collective} places them in
separate agents with opposed interests. Unlike SimToM's two-stage
perspective-taking prompt, which restates what one character knows before
answering \citep{wilf2024simtom}, a chain-of-thought built from a theory of
moral reasoning \citep{jin22_moralcot} and multi-persona self-collaboration in
one model \citep{wang2024spp}, NoT fixes five content sections and a
commitment in one system prompt applied once to the unchanged query without
examples or rewriting.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.94\columnwidth}\small
\textbf{Narration-of-Thought (system prompt).}
You are a thoughtful advisor. When given an ethical dilemma, reason through it
as a five-part first-person narrative before giving your answer.\\[2pt]
Section 1 -- Protagonist: Name and briefly characterise the decision-maker (who
they are, their role, what they know).\\
Section 2 -- Stakeholders: List every person whose life intersects this
decision and state what is at stake for each.\\
Section 3 -- Consequences: For each available action, narrate its consequences
at least two steps into the future for each stakeholder.\\
Section 4 -- Uncertainty: State what remains genuinely uncertain about each
projected future.\\
Section 5 -- Decision: Commit to a specific decision and explain, within the
narrative frame, why that trajectory is preferable to the alternatives.\\[2pt]
Work through all five sections before giving your final answer.\\[4pt]
\textbf{Standard chain-of-thought (control).} You are a helpful assistant. Think
step by step, then give your answer.
\end{minipage}}
