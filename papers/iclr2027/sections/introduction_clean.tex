\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}

Large language models agree with their users more than the evidence
warrants, a trained bias called sycophancy
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}, which in personal advice takes the
form \citet{cheng25elephant} call social sycophancy, validating the person
asking.
The social
account of objectivity locates it in organised criticism across
perspectives, not in one reasoner
\citep{longino1990science,popper2011open}, and a model answering one person's
account holds only that person's perspective. We introduce
Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a lightweight system prompt asking the model to write the missing perspectives
into its reasoning as a first-person narrative of the decision-maker, the
stakeholders, the consequences of each action and what remains uncertain,
ending in a decision (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), and compare it with standard chain-of-thought \citep[CoT,][]{wei22,kojima22}.

On 150 open-ended advice questions from the ELEPHANT benchmark
\citep{cheng25elephant}, NoT lowers the validation rate, the share
of answers a judge model scores as emotionally validating the asker, on all
seven answering models (generators) under our production judge, the
default scorer claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku), by 13 to 63 percentage points, a relative reduction,
the drop divided by CoT's rate, of 16 to 80 percent, but it depends on the judge, holding under all
five judges only on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama)
(Section~\ref{sec-single}).
The scaffold's structured content, not the narrative form, carries the
reduction, since the same five elements as a third-person checklist
lower validation below CoT's by 36.5 to 57.5 points pooled over four
generators, more than NoT under every judge, while first-person narration
without them raises it, though as the rubric scores emotional validation,
part of any reduction may be a cooler tone rather than less endorsement.
In a judge-free test, NoT capitulates more often than CoT when the user,
as writer of a conflict account, disputes being found at fault,
0.37 against 0.22 pooled over three generators, but also more when merely
asked to reconsider, with no detectable difference net of that instability, $+5.2$ points, 95 percent interval $[-1.7, +12.2]$.
In a second setting, the collective, copies of one model using NoT take
opposed seats to debate a Scruples account
\citep{lourie2021scruples}, scored against its near-unanimous crowd verdict of
who is at fault. The collective is a poor decider, less accurate
than the same model answering alone (the solo) on four
of five models and indistinguishable from it on haiku, but a
sensor of its own errors, since on grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok), among
verdicts that blame one party, it is wrong 9.8 times as often when a second
seat objects as when at most one does, a
mechanism finding, not a system worth building.
Routing debates where two or more seats object to claude-sonnet-4-6, a
stronger model answering afresh, raises accuracy over the
collective on all five models, by 7.0 points on grok, though not above grok's
solo, and CoT seats make the collective more accurate than NoT seats on grok
and Llama
(Section~\ref{sec-collective}).
