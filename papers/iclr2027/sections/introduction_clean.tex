\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}

Large language models agree with their users more than the evidence
warrants, a trained bias called sycophancy
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}, which in personal advice takes the
form \citet{cheng25elephant} call social sycophancy, validating the person
asking.
The social
account of objectivity locates it in organised criticism across
perspectives, not in one reasoner
\citep{longino1990science,popper2011open}, and a model answering one person
hears only that person's side. We introduce
Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a lightweight prompt scaffold asking the model to write the missing perspectives
into its reasoning as a first-person narrative in five sections, the decision-maker, the
stakeholders, the consequences of each action, what remains uncertain and a
decision (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), and test whether, against standard chain-of-thought \citep[CoT,][]{wei22,kojima22}, it lowers one agent's sycophancy (Section~\ref{sec-single}) and helps a collective of opposed agents decide who is at fault (Section~\ref{sec-collective}).

On 150 open-ended advice questions from the ELEPHANT benchmark
\citep{cheng25elephant}, NoT lowers the validation rate, the share
of answers a judge model scores as emotionally validating the asker, on all
seven answering models (generators) under our production judge, the
default scorer claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku), by 13 to 63 percentage points (a relative reduction
of 16 to 80 percent, the fall divided by CoT's rate), though in a second run on
claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet) that judge's interval includes zero. Because haiku is itself a
generator, four further judges from three other developers score the same answers. The
reduction depends on the judge, holding under all five only on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Llama)
(Section~\ref{sec-single}).
NoT's structured content, not the narrative form, carries the
reduction in judged validation. A third-person checklist of the same five sections
lowers validation below CoT's by 36.5 to 57.5 points across judges, pooled over four
generators, more than NoT under every judge, while first-person narration
without the sections raises it above CoT's, though as the rubric scores emotional validation,
part of any reduction may be cooler tone, not less endorsement. NoT also lowers premise
acceptance, whether an answer accepts the asker's framing under the benchmark's second rubric,
on all seven generators under the two judges scoring it, narration alone giving most of that drop on four,
while the benchmark's instruction not to validate, which lowers validation more than NoT under
every judge, leaves it where CoT has it.
In a judge-free test on Scruples conflict accounts
\citep{lourie2021scruples}, NoT withdraws
its verdict blaming the user more often than CoT when the user
disputes it, 0.37 against 0.22 pooled over three generators, but also when
merely asked to reconsider, an instability the pre-registered primary measure
nets out, leaving no detectable difference, $+5.2$ points
$[-1.7, +12.2]$, a 95 percent interval from resampling items.

The collective is a poor decider but a sensor of
its own errors. Three copies
of one model, each prompted with NoT, debate such an account as an advocate for each
party and a neutral seat and accept or object to the verdict a fourth copy
proposes before it writes the final one, scored against the account's
near-unanimous crowd verdict of who is at fault.
It is less accurate
than the same model's majority of three brief unscaffolded answers (the solo) on four
of five models and indistinguishable from it on haiku. On grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok),
the model of our primary evaluation, among
verdicts blaming one party, which give that party's advocate a reason to
object, it is wrong 9.8 times as often when a second
seat objects as when at most one does (a post hoc
analysis), a finding about mechanism, not a system worth building, as the solo is at
least as accurate at 3 calls against 17.
Routing each flagged debate, one where two or more seats object, to sonnet,
a stronger judge whose answer without the debate replaces the
collective's verdict, raises its accuracy on all five models,
by 7.0 points on grok, though not above the
solo there, and 4.9 of those points come from verdicts blaming both parties or
neither, where objections carry no detectable error signal.
CoT seats make the collective more accurate than NoT seats on
grok and Llama, and on grok its dissent flags errors with either, so
these results are not evidence for the scaffold (Section~\ref{sec-collective}).
