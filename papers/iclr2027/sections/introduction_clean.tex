\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}

Large language models give advice with apparent empathy and understanding,
yet they agree with their users more than the evidence warrants
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}. In personal advice this takes a social
form, validating the person asking \citep{cheng25elephant}.

The social account of objectivity locates objectivity in organised criticism
across perspectives rather than in any single reasoner
\citep{longino1990science,popper2011open}, and a model answering one person's
account holds only that person's perspective. Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a
lightweight system prompt, asks the model to write the missing perspectives
into its reasoning as a first-person narrative of the decision-maker, the
stakeholders, the consequences of each available action and what remains
uncertain, ending in a decision (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), and we compare
it with standard chain-of-thought \citep[CoT,][]{wei22,kojima22}. A collective
places the perspectives in separate agents with opposed interests and sends
the cases on which they dissent to a stronger judge
(Section~\ref{sec-collective}).

This paper makes four claims. Table~\ref{tab-claims} states each, the
collective's in three rows, with where it holds, where it fails and the
reading fixed before the data were seen. On 150 open-ended advice questions
from ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant}, NoT lowers the rate at which our
production judge, claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku), scores an answer as emotionally
validating the asker on all seven answering models (generators), by 13 to 63
points, a relative reduction of 16 to 80 percent, but the reduction depends
on the judge and holds under all five of ours only on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct
(Llama) (Section~\ref{sec-single}).
The reduction comes with the scaffold's structured content and not its
narrative form, since the same content as a third-person checklist lowers
judged validation more than NoT under every judge, by 36.5 to 57.5 points
pooled over four generators, while the narrative instruction alone raises it,
and because the rubric scores emotional validation part of the reduction may
be a cooler register.
NoT gives no protection against pushback on a verdict, capitulating more
often than CoT on the raw measure, 0.37 against 0.22 pooled over three
models, and no differently net of its own instability, $+5.2$ points
$[-1.7, +12.2]$.
In the collective, judged against near-unanimous crowd verdicts on
first-person conflict accounts, opposed agents make a poor decider,
descriptively at or below the same model answering alone on five models, but
a usable sensor of their own errors, since on grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok)
the collective is wrong 9.8 times as often when a second agent objects as
when at most one does.
Sending the flagged debates to a stronger judge raises accuracy over the
collective on all five models, by 7.0 points on grok, though not above grok
answering alone, and plain chain-of-thought seats make the collective more
accurate than narrating ones on both models tested
(Section~\ref{sec-collective}).
