\section{Related Work}
\label{sec-related}

\paragraph{Sycophancy is a trained bias, and it is steerable.}
Preference-tuned models agree with the user more often than the evidence
warrants, more so with scale and with the user's confidence
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}, validate the asker's own account of a
conflict \citep{cheng25elephant} and revise correct answers under pushback
\citep{fanous2025syceval,malmqvist24}. Sycophancy is a linear direction in
activation space \citep{rimsky2024caa} and behaves as a persona-level trait,
so an off-the-shelf sceptical persona recovers most of what targeted steering
achieves \citep{chen2025persona,kelkar2026devil}, and synthetic fine-tuning
data reduce it \citep{wei2023synthetic}. If sycophancy is a stance the model
adopts, assigning another stance inside the model's trace is the natural
control.

\paragraph{Role assignment controls what a model says, at a cost.}
Assigning a character or an interest reliably moves a model's output
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay,li2023camel}, although persona prompts explain
little variance in simulated survey judgement \citep{hu2024persona}, can
degrade zero-shot reasoning \citep{kim2025persona}, carry implicit biases
\citep{gupta2024bias,deshpande2023toxicity} and amplify sycophancy when the
persona is agreeable \citep{shah2026toonice}, while multi-persona prompting
within one model improves some tasks \citep{wang2024spp}. Closest to our
collective, \citet{dietrich2026roles} assign opposed advocates, a balanced
seat and a supervisor and measure how far each advocate's output is fixed by
its role. We find this role-lock near total on one model and below one half
on another, and a seat whose verdict its role fixes carries no information
about the case.

\paragraph{Debate places truth at the judge, not in the debaters.}
Scalable oversight puts opposed agents before a judge \citep{irving18}, and
judges become more accurate watching a debate than consulting one advocate
\citep{michael2023debate,khan2024debating}, but the gain over a judge answering
alone appears only under information asymmetry \citep{kenton2024scalable}.
Debate among copies of one model improves some benchmarks
\citep{du23,liang2024divergent,chan2023chateval} and fails to beat
self-consistency on others \citep{smit2024mad,wang2024rethinking}, and formal
accounts explain why. Same-model debate converges to the majority already in
the room \citep{estornell2024multillm}, homogeneous agents with uniform updates
cannot reliably improve expected correctness \citep{zhu2026demystifying}, and
exchange preserves expectations where voting does not
\citep{choi2025debateorvote}.
Disagreement has also served as a signal, in cross-examination that detects
factual errors \citep{cohen2023lmvslm} and in prover and verifier games
\citep{kirchner2024prover}. We assign opposed interests rather than opposed
answers, so no advocate is right by construction, and ask what their
disagreement carries.

\paragraph{Communication topology and aggregation.}
Work on multi-agent topology scales and optimises agent graphs
\citep{qian2025macnet,zhuge2024gptswarm,liu2024dylan}, finds sparse ones
competitive at lower cost \citep{li2024sparse}, and finds that topology
governs how errors propagate
\citep{shen2025propagation,han2026conformity}, that agents conform to
majorities \citep{zhang2024socialpsych,weng2025conformity}, that correlated
errors limit aggregation \citep{kim2025correlated} and that heterogeneous
panels beat homogeneous ones \citep{wang2025moa}, with recent work on
effective team size, unguided homogeneous debate and when a minority should
overturn a majority
\citep{bertalanic2026ringelmann,bertalanic2026costconsensus,he2026minoritysentinel}.
Aggregation theory states when pooling judgements helps, under correlated
votes \citep{ladha1992condorcet,boland1989majority}, unequal competence
\citep{shapley1984optimizing,nitzan1982optimal,bahrami2010optimally}, social
influence in networks
\citep{degroot1974reaching,golub2010naive,lorenz2011social,becker2017network},
diversity \citep{zollman2010epistemic,hong2004groups,thompson2014diversity},
small deliberating groups \citep{navajas2018aggregated} and shared biases
\citep{prelec2017solution}, and where it cannot \citep{list2002aggregating}.
Where this literature measures accuracy or cost, we measure the structure of
dissent and whether it transfers across generators.

\paragraph{The argumentative theory and the social account of objectivity.}
Human reasoning may have evolved for argument, with individuals producing
biased advocacy and truth emerging on the evaluation side of an exchange
\citep{mercier2011argumentative,mercier2017enigma}, since arguments rather
than confidence move groups \citep{trouche2014arguments} while individuals are
lazy about their own arguments \citep{trouche2016selective}, though the
evidence for the collective half is the weaker \citep{mercier2016predictions}.
Assigned devil's advocacy helps less than authentic dissent
\citep{nemeth2001devil}, though structured conflict beats consensus
\citep{schweiger1986group,schwenk1990effects}. Objectivity is a
property of a community's structure of criticism rather than of any member
\citep{longino1990science,longino2002fate,popper2011open}, and the scaffold
places that structure inside a single trace while the collective places it
across agents. Mediation and mechanism design study agents with divergent
interests
\citep{dafoe2020cooperative,akata2025repeated,piatti2024cooperate,leon2026personas,tewolde2026coopeval},
where a correlating third party can reach outcomes no equilibrium of the
players alone reaches \citep{aumann1974subjectivity}, the position our
collective's neutral seat and external judge occupy.
