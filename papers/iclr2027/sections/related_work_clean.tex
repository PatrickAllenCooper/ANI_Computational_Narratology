\section{Related Work}
\label{sec-related}

\paragraph{Sycophancy is a trained bias, and it is steerable.}
Preference-tuned models agree with the user more than the evidence warrants,
more so with scale and user confidence
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}, validate the asker's own account of a
conflict \citep{cheng25elephant} and revise correct answers under pushback
\citep{fanous2025syceval,malmqvist24}. Sycophancy is a linear direction in
activation space \citep{rimsky2024caa} and a persona-level trait, so an
off-the-shelf sceptical persona vector recovers most of what steering along
that direction achieves \citep{chen2025persona,kelkar2026devil}, and synthetic fine-tuning
data reduce it \citep{wei2023synthetic}. If sycophancy is a stance the model
adopts, NoT has the model write other perspectives into its reasoning trace,
and we compare it with chain-of-thought.
Language-model judges favour their own generations, among other biases
\citep{panickssery2024selfrecognition,wataoka2024selfpreference,zheng2023judging},
so we state every claim per judge and check each judge against ELEPHANT's
labels on human-written answers (Section~\ref{sec-single}).

\paragraph{Role assignment controls what a model says, at a cost.}
Assigning a character or an interest reliably moves a model's output
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay,li2023camel}, although persona prompts explain
little variance in simulated surveys \citep{hu2024persona}, can degrade
zero-shot reasoning \citep{kim2025persona}, carry implicit biases
\citep{gupta2024bias,deshpande2023toxicity} and amplify sycophancy when
agreeable \citep{shah2026toonice}, while multi-persona prompting in one model
helps some tasks \citep{wang2024spp}. Closest to our
collective, \citet{dietrich2026roles} assigns opposed advocates, a balanced
seat and a supervisor and measures how far each advocate's output is fixed by
its role, a role-lock we find near total on grok and below one half on
haiku (Section~\ref{sec-collective}).

\paragraph{Debate places truth at the judge, not in the debaters.}
Scalable oversight puts opposed agents before a judge \citep{irving18}, who gains more from a debate than from one advocate
\citep{michael2023debate,khan2024debating}, and over answering alone only
when it lacks the debaters' information \citep{kenton2024scalable}.
Debate among copies of one model helps some benchmarks
\citep{du23,liang2024divergent,chan2023chateval} and fails to beat
self-consistency \citep{wang2023selfconsistency}, our single-model baseline,
on others
\citep{smit2024mad,wang2024rethinking}, since it converges to the existing
majority \citep{estornell2024multillm}, homogeneous
agents with uniform updates cannot reliably improve expected correctness
\citep{zhu2026demystifying}, and debate alone leaves it unchanged where voting
raises it \citep{choi2025debateorvote}.
Disagreement has also been a signal, in cross-examination
\citep{cohen2023lmvslm}, in prover-verifier games
\citep{kirchner2024prover} and, as sample disagreement or stated confidence
\citep{manakul2023selfcheck,farquhar2024semantic,xiong2024confidence}, as the
rule by which cascades escalate a cheap model's uncertain cases
\citep{chen2023frugal,jitkrittum2023deferral,gupta2024cascades,yue2024cascades,aggarwal2024automix,jung2025escalate,wang2026defer}.
We assign opposed interests, not opposed answers, so no advocate is right
by construction, and ask whether their disagreement routes errors to a
stronger judge better than sample disagreement.

\paragraph{Topology and aggregation ask when pooling helps.}
Work on multi-agent topology scales and optimises agent graphs
\citep{qian2025macnet,zhuge2024gptswarm,liu2024dylan}, finds sparse graphs
competitive at lower cost \citep{li2024sparse}, topology to govern how
errors propagate \citep{shen2025propagation,han2026conformity}, agents to
conform to majorities \citep{zhang2024socialpsych,weng2025conformity},
correlated errors to limit aggregation \citep{kim2025correlated} and
heterogeneous panels to beat homogeneous ones \citep{wang2025moa}, and studies
team size, unguided homogeneous debate and minority overrides
\citep{bertalanic2026ringelmann,bertalanic2026costconsensus,he2026minoritysentinel}.
Aggregation theory states when pooling judgements helps, under correlated
votes \citep{ladha1992condorcet,boland1989majority}, unequal competence
\citep{shapley1984optimizing,nitzan1982optimal,bahrami2010optimally}, social
influence in networks
\citep{degroot1974reaching,golub2010naive,lorenz2011social,becker2017network},
diversity \citep{zollman2010epistemic,hong2004groups,thompson2014diversity},
small deliberating groups \citep{navajas2018aggregated} and shared biases
\citep{prelec2017solution}, and where it cannot \citep{list2002aggregating}.
We ask instead whether a second objection flags the collective's own errors,
on each of five models.

\paragraph{Two accounts of reasoning as social motivate the design.}
Human reasoning may have evolved for argument, with biased advocacy from
individuals and truth emerging when others evaluate it
\citep{mercier2011argumentative,mercier2017enigma}, since arguments rather
than confidence move groups \citep{trouche2014arguments} while individuals are
lazy about their own arguments \citep{trouche2016selective}, though the group
claim has the weaker evidence \citep{mercier2016predictions}.
Assigned devil's advocacy helps less than authentic dissent
\citep{nemeth2001devil}, though structured conflict beats consensus
\citep{schweiger1986group,schwenk1990effects}. Objectivity is a
property of a community's structure of criticism rather than of any member
\citep{longino1990science,longino2002fate,popper2011open}, a structure the
scaffold places inside one trace and the collective across agents. Mediation and
mechanism design study agents with divergent
interests
\citep{dafoe2020cooperative,akata2025repeated,piatti2024cooperate,leon2026personas,tewolde2026coopeval},
where a mediator's correlated recommendations can reach outcomes no
equilibrium of the players alone reaches \citep{aumann1974subjectivity}, the
position of our neutral seat and stronger judge.
