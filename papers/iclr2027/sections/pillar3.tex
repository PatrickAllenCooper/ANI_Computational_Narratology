\section{Collective Judgement}
\label{sec-collective}

The collective places the principle behind the scaffold outside a single
trace, as separate agents with opposed interests
(Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}). It is a poor decider whose dissent can audit its
own verdicts. On the five models tested its verdict is at or below the same
model answering alone, on four a second objection marks where it is more
often wrong, and sending the flagged cases to a stronger judge raises
accuracy over the collective's own verdict but not above grok, Llama or
DeepSeek answering alone. On the two models tested narration in the seats
does not help, and plain chain-of-thought seats make the collective more
accurate.

\paragraph{Instrument and protocol.}
Disputes are Scruples Anecdotes, first-person conflict accounts whose crowd
verdict is gold when at least 90 percent of at least 50 votes agree.

% scripts/run_crowdgold_aita.py (--min-votes 50, --min-consensus 0.90), prereg E4 ~l.294-312
The panel has 249 items, 99 on which the crowd found the writer at fault
(YTA) and 150 on which it did not (NTA), each posed in the third person and
with the user as the writer.
% prereg E4 ~l.294-312 and 16.23 registration ~l.6737 (--n-yta 99 --n-nta 150 --seed 44)
A per-model content-filter screen drops the items a platform refuses,
leaving 210 on grok, 249 on haiku, 225 on Llama, 217 on Mistral and 210 on
DeepSeek.
% prereg E5 RESULTS ~l.528-537 (grok 210), 16.23 RESULTS (haiku 249), 16.24 RESULTS ~l.7640
% (Llama 225), 16.26 RESULTS ~l.8465 (Mistral 217), 16.27 RESULTS ~l.8277 (DeepSeek 210)
The models are grok-4-1-fast-reasoning (grok), claude-haiku-4-5 (haiku),
gpt-5.4-nano (nano), Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, Mistral-Large-3-2 and
DeepSeek-V4-Pro, and the judge is claude-sonnet-4-6 (sonnet).
% identifiers as in prereg E5, 16.4 table ~l.3143, 16.24, 16.26 and 16.27 RESULTS headers, and
% routing_certification.json judges.sonnet
Three copies of one model take seats, an advocate for the writer and an
advocate for the other party with opposed interests and a neutral
adjudicator with none, and a further copy with no side moderates. A debate
runs seven stages in 17 calls. The seats open, rebut and restate, the
moderator writes a synthesis that each seat accepts, accepts with a stated
modification or rejects, the moderator then writes an integrated verdict,
scored as the collective's with YTA and ESH as at fault and NTA and NAH as
not, and the seats vote.
% scripts/run_crowdgold_deliberation.py docstring (17 = 3+3+3+1+3+1+3), prereg E4 ~l.296,
% 16.27 RESULTS ~l.8349 (14,280 calls = 840 debates x 17), scripts/verify_pillar3_headline.py scores S2
The counter fires when at least two of the three seats object to the
synthesis, and routing sends the flagged debates to sonnet, a stronger judge
with no side that sees only the dispute, not the debate, and answers by
majority of three samples, ties going to the collective.
% prereg 16.1 ~l.3042-3084 (counter = n_objectors >= 2, objection = ACCEPT_WITH_MODIFICATION or
% REJECT), 16.15.1 and 16.22 (sonnet standard majority-of-3 judge), scripts/verify_pillar3_headline.py

\paragraph{The collective is a sensor, not a decider.}
On the five models in Table~\ref{tab-routing} the collective's verdict is at
or below the same model answering alone by majority of three, 0.843 against
0.912 on grok, 0.854 against 0.862 on haiku, 0.620 against 0.842 on Llama,
0.752 against 0.843 on Mistral and 0.778 against 0.959 on DeepSeek.
% collective from verify_pillar3_headline_{grok,haiku_249,llama,mistral,deepseek}.json, solo from
% routing_certification{,_haiku,_llama,_mistral,_deepseek}.json acc_base (sonnet-judge slot),
% prereg 16.22 RESULTS ~l.6806, 16.23 ~l.6925, 16.24 diagnostic ~l.7907-7912
These comparisons are descriptive, debates against cells with no paired
interval, and two earlier comparisons outside the table, on nano and on a
100-item haiku panel, point the other way.
% prereg 16.4 ~l.3145-3146, nano 0.829 (828 debates, 16.4) vs solo 0.820 (routing_certification_nano.json
% acc_base, 406 cells, unregistered), haiku_fixed 0.885 (192 debates, 16.4) vs solo 0.851 (prereg 16.17
% cell 2 ~l.6485, n 630) to 0.862 (routing_certification_haiku.json acc_base)
On grok, whose seats lock to their sides, a majority of the three opening
verdicts adds nothing to the neutral seat's. The writer's advocate finds the
writer at fault on 0.3 percent of opening statements and the other party's
advocate on 97.4 percent, so the three-seat majority equals the neutral
seat's verdict on all 1,642 debates with three codable openings.
% prereg 16.15.4 RESULTS ~l.4496, effective_voter.json (majority of the r0 opening verdicts,
% majority_equals_neutral 1642 of 1642, grok panel), topology_2x2_analysis.json (P(at-fault) 0.003
% and 0.974, role-lock 0.971)
The opposition also costs accuracy. With the exchange kept, three identical
neutral readers score $+2.1$ points $[+0.4, +3.8]$ above the opposed seats on
grok, a contrast observed rather than declared in advance, with narrated
seats in both arms.

\paragraph{Dissent flags errors.}
A verdict that blames one party gives that party's advocate a reason to
object, so the informative objection is a second one, which no assigned
side explains \citep{milgrom1986relying,lipman1995robust}. On grok's
one-loser verdicts (YTA or NTA) the collective is wrong on 0.387 of the
debates where a second seat objects against 0.039 where at most one does, 9.8
times as often, a lift of $+34.8$ points $[+20.1, +49.3]$ over 93 flagged
debates of 1,287.
% prereg 16.15.1 RESULTS ~l.4273-4283 (full 210-item frame), router_decomposition.json
% counter_lift_by_stratum.one_loser, verify_pillar3_headline_grok.json (0.3871 vs 0.0394, 9.834x;
% unfired = fewer than two objectors, 1,194 debates, flooding_analysis.json one-loser 8 with none)
On grok's verdicts that blame both parties or neither, the counter fires on
60 percent of them and shows no detectable lift, and the verdict type is
itself the cheaper error signal.
% prereg 16.12 and flooding_analysis.json (P(2+ object) both-party 0.603), prereg 16.15.1 RESULTS
% (both-party 0.426 vs 0.523, -0.097 [-0.233, +0.043]) and its Rule 2 (verdict type as second signal)
The one-loser lift replicates at varying strength, 5.1 times with the
exchange between seats cut on grok, 6.2 on Mistral (where the counter fires
on 85 percent of one-loser debates), 2.3 on DeepSeek and 1.5 on Llama, each
interval excluding zero, while on haiku (1.5 times, $+3.5$ points
$[-4.1, +12.0]$) the interval includes zero.
% verify_pillar3_headline_{noedge,mistral,deepseek,llama,haiku_249}.json (ol_ratio 5.06, 6.16,
% 2.27, 1.53, 1.49), intervals from prereg 16.25 RESULTS ~l.7580-7636, 16.26 ~l.8493,
% 16.27 ~l.8306, 16.24 ~l.7662, 16.23 ~l.6937, verify_pillar3_headline_mistral.json ol_fired 521 of
% ol_n 614 (0.849)
The mechanism behind it is model-dependent. The advocates' objections on
one-loser verdicts are strongly opposed on grok ($\phi = -0.90$), as dissent
against interest predicts, but positively correlated on haiku ($+0.25$),
Llama ($+0.15$) and Mistral ($+0.21$), and only weakly opposed on DeepSeek
($-0.09$), so the against-interest reading is supported on grok, mainly by
which advocate's side loses (within YTA or NTA alone $\phi$ is $-0.34$ and
$-0.16$), and not established elsewhere.
% flooding_analysis_{,haiku_249,llama,mistral,deepseek}.json one-loser advocate phi, and
% phi_predictors_diagnostic.json (grok -0.901 [-0.929, -0.868], haiku +0.246 [+0.130, +0.360],
% Llama +0.148 [+0.065, +0.227], Mistral +0.208 [+0.119, +0.299], DeepSeek -0.093 [-0.182, -0.007])
% within-type residual: flooding_analysis.json s2_codable_16_12 YTA -0.340 [-0.663, +0.011],
% NTA -0.162 [-0.317, -0.022], prereg 16.12 RESULTS note ~l.4999-5006
Mistral floods, with two or more seats objecting on 88 percent of its
debates, so there the counter is close to a constant.

\paragraph{Routing.}
Routing the flagged debates to the judge raises accuracy over the
collective on all six verified conditions (Table~\ref{tab-routing}), by
$+7.0$ points $[+4.8, +9.4]$ on grok with 19.6 percent of debates routed.
% verify_pillar3_headline_grok.json (delta 0.0704 [0.0482, 0.0943], 328 of 1,677),
% prereg 16.15.1 RESULTS ~l.4314-4325 and the verification block ~l.7038-7058
It repairs the collective more than it improves on the model. The routed
system only matches grok answering alone (0.913 against 0.912), stays below
the solo on Llama (0.661 against 0.842) and DeepSeek (0.850 against 0.959),
and beats it only on haiku (0.891 against 0.862) and on Mistral (0.967
against 0.843), whose gain of $+21.4$ points comes from consulting the judge
on 88 percent of debates, near-blanket consultation rather than selective
routing.
% figures/tab_routing.tex routed column (verify_pillar3_headline_{grok,llama,deepseek,haiku_249,
% mistral}.json), solo acc_base from routing_certification{,_llama,_deepseek,_haiku,_mistral}.json
% sonnet-judge slot, descriptive, debates against cells, no paired interval,
% verify_pillar3_headline_mistral.json (delta 0.2143, fire 0.876), prereg 16.26 RESULTS ~l.8500-8545
Against the model's own solo answer the gain is smaller. Routing the grok
solo verdicts of the cells (an item in one framing) on which the counter
fires in more than half of the four debates, 12.1 percent of cells, lifts
grok alone from 0.912 to 0.936, $+2.4$ points $[+0.5, +4.5]$, but the
solo's own disagreement among its three samples \citep{manakul2023selfcheck}
routes as well, $+0.2$ points $[-1.9, +2.4]$ relative to the counter, at a
seventh of the cost of one 17-call debate and a twenty-fifth of the four
debates behind the flags, and sonnet answering every case is still 4.0
points higher.
% prereg 16.22 RESULTS ~l.6806-6863, routing_certification.json (R1 +0.0238 [+0.0048, +0.0452],
% 51 of 420 cells, R3 +0.0024 [-0.0190, +0.0238], cost $0.0014 vs $0.0098 per decision (counter
% priced as a k = 1 sensor, 17 calls; the k = 4 flag as run costs $0.0333 plus the judge, $0.0352 per
% decision in R5 k4_sensor_over_base/counter/sonnet; 16.22's 'a tenth' at ~l.6860 does not match its
% own prices), R2 -0.0405 [-0.0690, -0.0143], sonnet everywhere 0.976), cells and firing rule from
% scripts/analyze_router_decomposition.py build_cells (counter_fired = k_counter*2 > k)
In exploratory readouts the same comparison holds on Mistral, again at
high coverage ($+12.2$ points $[+8.1, +16.6]$ with 77 percent of cells
routed), and on haiku's weaker solo ($+3.1$ $[+1.2, +5.1]$), and it fails
on Llama ($+0.4$ $[0.0, +1.1]$) and on DeepSeek ($+0.2$ $[-0.5, +1.2]$).
% routing_certification_{mistral,haiku,llama,deepseek}.json sonnet-judge R1 (in the haiku file the
% slot labelled haiku holds sonnet), all status exploratory, prereg 16.26 ~l.8506, 16.23 ~l.6961,
% 16.24 ~l.7667, 16.27 ~l.8312. Not reported: routing_certification_noedge_k4.json (edges off) is
% UNREAD, its replay gate cannot run and 16.25 RESULTS never reads it (+0.0071 [-0.0071, +0.0214],
% 19 of 420 cells)
The collective's value is as an audit of its own verdicts, not as a cheap
detector of a solo model's errors.

\paragraph{Narration costs the collective accuracy.}
In a registered comparison we replaced each seat's narration with plain
chain-of-thought and held everything else fixed. Plain seats give the
collective higher accuracy on both models tested, by 2.0 points
$[+0.3, +3.7]$ on grok (0.863 against 0.843) and 4.2 points $[+1.4, +7.0]$
on Llama (0.662 against 0.620), which the registered rule reads as a
reversal.
% prereg Addendum 16.28 RESULTS ~l.8977-9002 (reading PLAIN-BETTER ~l.8994, registered as a reversal
% ~l.8646-8648), seat_scaffold_comparison.json (acc, narrated minus plain -0.020 [-0.037, -0.003]
% grok, 210 items, -0.042 [-0.070, -0.014] Llama, 225 items)
Narrated seats fire the counter more often on grok (0.195 against 0.152,
$+4.3$ points $[+1.8, +6.9]$) and so gain more from routing (7.0 against
5.4 points, a difference of $+1.6$ $[0.0, +3.3]$), which brings their routed
accuracy to within 0.4 points of the plain seats' (0.913 against 0.917).
% seat_scaffold_comparison.json grok (fire +0.043 [+0.018, +0.069], gain 0.070 vs 0.054, +0.016
% [+0.000, +0.033], routed -0.004 [-0.023, +0.015]), prereg 16.28 RESULTS ~l.8990 rows (c) and (d);
% fire rates here are item-averaged (narrated 0.195), the per-debate rate in Table tab-routing is

With the narrated flags thinned at random to the plain seats' fire rate, the
plain-seat system is more accurate after routing by 2.0 points
$[+0.1, +3.8]$ on grok and 4.6 points $[+1.2, +7.8]$ on Llama, about its
margin before routing, so at equal coverage routing adds about as much to
either kind of seat.

On Llama narration has no detectable effect on the fire rate ($+0.2$ points
$[-2.2, +2.7]$, narrated minus plain) or the routing gain ($-0.2$
$[-1.9, +1.4]$), and on neither model do we detect a change in how much more
often the verdict excuses a writer at fault when the user is the writer.

On grok each part of the sensor survives without narration, at a lower fire
rate and with a smaller lift. The plain seats lock to their sides (opening

$[+11.8, +41.2]$), routing gains $+5.4$ points $[+3.5, +7.4]$ over the

disagreement again routes as well ($+0.7$ $[-1.4, +2.9]$ relative to the

On the two models tested the multi-agent result therefore does not depend on
the scaffold, and on grok it traces to the assigned opposition and the
routing rule, so it is not evidence for the scaffold itself.
% seat_scaffold_comparison.json (grok, Llama, reading PLAIN-BETTER), prereg 16.28 RESULTS

We present the collective as the external form of the same principle of
social objectivity that the scaffold places inside one trace
\citep{longino1990science}, and confine the evidence for the scaffold to the
single-agent results.
