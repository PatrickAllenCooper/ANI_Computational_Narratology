\section{Collective Judgement}
\label{sec-collective}

The collective, which places the scaffold's principle across agents with
opposed interests (Section~\ref{sec-scaffold}), is a poor decider whose
dissent can audit its own verdicts. On five models its verdict is at or below
the same model answering alone, on four a second objection marks where it is
more often wrong, and sending the flagged cases to a stronger judge raises
accuracy over the collective but not above grok, Llama or DeepSeek answering
alone. On the two models tested plain chain-of-thought seats make the
collective more accurate than narrating ones.

\paragraph{Instrument and protocol.}
Disputes are Scruples Anecdotes, first-person conflict accounts whose crowd
verdict is gold when at least 90 percent of at least 50 votes agree.
\todo{PI: Scruples Anecdotes has no key in references.bib, so the dataset is uncited.}
% scripts/run_crowdgold_aita.py (--min-votes 50, --min-consensus 0.90), prereg E4 ~l.294-312
The panel has 249 items, 99 on which the crowd found the writer at fault and
150 on which it did not, each posed in the third person and with the user as
the writer, less the items each platform's content filter refuses
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
% prereg E4 ~l.294-312 and 16.23 registration ~l.6737 (--n-yta 99 --n-nta 150 --seed 44); per-model
% counts in Appendix app-collective
Three copies of one model take seats, an advocate for the writer, an advocate
for the other party and a neutral adjudicator, and a fourth copy with no side
moderates. A debate runs seven stages in 17 calls. The seats open, rebut and
restate, the moderator writes a synthesis that each seat accepts, accepts with
a stated modification or rejects, the moderator writes an integrated verdict,
scored as the collective's, and the seats vote. A verdict that blames the
writer alone or both parties counts as at fault, and one that blames the other
party alone or no one as not at fault.
% scripts/run_crowdgold_deliberation.py docstring (17 = 3+3+3+1+3+1+3), prereg E4 ~l.296,
% 16.27 RESULTS ~l.8349 (14,280 calls = 840 debates x 17), scripts/verify_pillar3_headline.py scores S2
% (YTA and ESH at fault, NTA and NAH not); models as in prereg E5, 16.4 table ~l.3143, 16.24, 16.26
% and 16.27 RESULTS headers
The counter fires when at least two seats object to the synthesis, and
routing sends the flagged debates to sonnet, a stronger judge that sees only
the dispute and answers by majority of three samples, ties going to the
collective.

\paragraph{The collective is a sensor, not a decider.}
On the five models in Table~\ref{tab-routing} the collective's verdict is at
or below the same model answering alone by majority of three, 0.843 against
0.912 on grok, 0.854 against 0.862 on haiku, 0.620 against 0.842 on Llama,
0.752 against 0.843 on Mistral and 0.778 against 0.959 on DeepSeek.
% collective from verify_pillar3_headline_{grok,haiku_249,llama,mistral,deepseek}.json, solo from
% routing_certification{,_haiku,_llama,_mistral,_deepseek}.json acc_base (sonnet-judge slot),
% prereg 16.22 RESULTS ~l.6806, 16.23 ~l.6925, 16.24 diagnostic ~l.7907-7912
These comparisons are descriptive, with no paired interval, and two earlier
comparisons outside the table, on nano and on a 100-item haiku panel, point
the other way.

On grok the seats lock to their sides (the writer's advocate finds the writer

percent), so a majority of the opening verdicts equals the neutral seat's on
all 1,642 debates with three codable openings.

\paragraph{Dissent flags errors.}
A verdict that blames one party gives that party's advocate a reason to
object, so the informative objection is a second one, which no assigned
side explains \citep{milgrom1986relying,lipman1995robust}. On grok's
verdicts that blame exactly one party the collective is wrong on 0.387 of the
debates where a second seat objects against 0.039 where at most one does, 9.8
times as often, a lift of $+34.8$ points $[+20.1, +49.3]$ over 93 flagged
debates of 1,287.
% prereg 16.15.1 RESULTS ~l.4273-4283 (full 210-item frame), router_decomposition.json
% counter_lift_by_stratum.one_loser, verify_pillar3_headline_grok.json (0.3871 vs 0.0394, 9.834x;
% unfired = fewer than two objectors, 1,194 debates, flooding_analysis.json one-loser 8 with none)
On verdicts that blame both parties or neither, the counter fires on 60
percent of debates and shows no detectable lift, and the verdict type is
itself the cheaper error signal.
% prereg 16.12 and flooding_analysis.json (P(2+ object) both-party 0.603), prereg 16.15.1 RESULTS
% (both-party 0.426 vs 0.523, -0.097 [-0.233, +0.043]) and its Rule 2 (verdict type as second signal)
The lift replicates at varying strength, 5.1 times with the exchange between
seats cut on grok, 6.2 on Mistral, 2.3 on DeepSeek and 1.5 on Llama, each
interval excluding zero, while on haiku (1.5 times, $+3.5$ points
$[-4.1, +12.0]$) the interval includes zero. Mistral floods, with two or more
seats objecting on 88 percent of its debates, so there the counter is close to
a constant. The advocates' objections are strongly opposed on grok, as dissent
against interest predicts, but correlated or only weakly opposed on the other
four models, so that mechanism is supported on grok alone
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).

\paragraph{Routing.}
Routing the flagged debates to the judge raises accuracy over the collective
on all six conditions of Table~\ref{tab-routing}, by $+7.0$ points
$[+4.8, +9.4]$ on grok with 19.6 percent of debates routed.
% verify_pillar3_headline_grok.json (delta 0.0704 [0.0482, 0.0943], 328 of 1,677),
% prereg 16.15.1 RESULTS ~l.4314-4325 and the verification block ~l.7038-7058
It repairs the collective more than it improves on the model. The routed
system only matches grok answering alone (0.913 against 0.912), stays below
the solo on Llama (0.661 against 0.842) and DeepSeek (0.850 against 0.959),
and beats it only on haiku (0.891 against 0.862) and on Mistral (0.967
against 0.843), whose gain of $+21.4$ points comes from consulting the judge
on 88 percent of debates.
% figures/tab_routing.tex routed column (verify_pillar3_headline_{grok,llama,deepseek,haiku_249,
% mistral}.json), solo acc_base from routing_certification{,_llama,_deepseek,_haiku,_mistral}.json
% sonnet-judge slot, descriptive, debates against the solo's answers, no paired interval,
% verify_pillar3_headline_mistral.json (delta 0.2143, fire 0.876), prereg 16.26 RESULTS ~l.8500-8545
Using the flags to route grok's own solo verdicts lifts grok alone by $+2.4$
points $[+0.5, +4.5]$, but the disagreement among the solo's
own three samples \citep{manakul2023selfcheck} routes as well, $+0.2$ points
$[-1.9, +2.4]$ relative to the counter, at a seventh of the cost of one debate,
and sonnet answering every case is still 4.0 points higher
(Appendix~\ref{app-collective}).
% prereg 16.22 RESULTS ~l.6806-6863, routing_certification.json (R1 +0.0238 [+0.0048, +0.0452],
% 51 of 420 item-framing units, R3 +0.0024 [-0.0190, +0.0238], cost $0.0014 vs $0.0098 per decision,
% R2 -0.0405 [-0.0690, -0.0143], sonnet everywhere 0.976); the exploratory readouts on the other four
% models are in Appendix app-collective
The collective's value is as an audit of its own verdicts, not as a cheap
detector of a solo model's errors.

\paragraph{Narration costs the collective accuracy.}
In a registered comparison we replaced each seat's narration with plain
chain-of-thought and held everything else fixed (Appendix~\ref{app-seat}).
Plain seats give the collective higher accuracy on both models tested, by 2.0
points $[+0.3, +3.7]$ on grok (0.863 against 0.843) and 4.2 points
$[+1.4, +7.0]$ on Llama (0.662 against 0.620), which the registered rule reads
as a reversal.
% prereg Addendum 16.28 RESULTS ~l.8977-9002 (reading PLAIN-BETTER ~l.8994, registered as a reversal
% ~l.8646-8648), seat_scaffold_comparison.json (acc, narrated minus plain -0.020 [-0.037, -0.003]
% grok, 210 items, -0.042 [-0.070, -0.014] Llama, 225 items)
On grok narrated seats fire the counter more often (0.195 against 0.152) and
so gain more from routing (7.0 against 5.4 points, a difference of $+1.6$
$[0.0, +3.3]$), which brings their routed accuracy to within 0.4 points of
the plain seats' (0.913 against 0.917), while on Llama narration changes
neither detectably. At equal coverage the plain-seat system is more accurate
after routing by 2.0 points $[+0.1, +3.8]$ on grok and 4.6 points
$[+1.2, +7.8]$ on Llama.

against 9.8 times on verdicts that blame one party), and on neither model do
we detect a change in how much more often the verdict excuses a writer at

the scaffold and is not evidence for it, and we present the collective only as
the external form of the principle that the scaffold places inside one trace
\citep{longino1990science}.
