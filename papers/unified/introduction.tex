\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}

Language models are adept at conversation. They give advice and suggestions
with apparent empathy and an apparent understanding of the person asking.
Whatever that understanding is and however it arose, it is widely accepted
to break down under more complex social conditions, when the person asking
has a stake in the answer, when the account they give is one side of a
dispute, or when several parties want different things. The sharpest case
is sycophancy. Ask a model whether you were in the wrong and it answers more
gently than it would a stranger with the same account
\citep{sharma23,cheng25elephant}, and preference training builds this in as
a bias prescribed by the user. The general problem is a conclusion pulled
away from the evidence by something else in the room, the asker's feelings,
an accepted premise, an assigned side, and a fix for sycophancy alone leaves
the others.

The social account of objectivity says where a remedy lies. On the
argumentative theory of reasoning individuals argue for a side, and on the
social account of objectivity truth is a property of the organised
criticism among them rather than of any one mind
\citep{mercier2011argumentative,longino1990science}. A model answering a
single user has no critic in the room. Our proposal is to put one there, in
two ways. Narration-of-Thought (NoT) is a lightweight prompt scaffold that
guides the model through a first-person narrative that identifies the
decision-maker and what they know, names every affected party and their
interests, traces the consequences of each available action for each of
them, acknowledges what remains uncertain, and only then commits. The
critic is represented inside the trace. The collective goes further and
assigns the interests to separate copies of the model, one speaking for
the writer of an account, one for the other party, one for no one, so that
the criticism is real and its dissent can be read by a simple rule, that a
statement against the speaker's assigned interest is informative and a
statement with it is not \citep{milgrom1986relying,lipman1995robust}.

evidence supports. Used alone, the scaffold reduces validation of the
asker's own account on open-ended personal dilemmas by 22 to 41 points

The paper's claim is one sentence long. Objectivity is obtained by placing
an opposed interest into the process, represented inside one trace or
assigned to a separate seat, and it is a property of that arrangement and
not of any copy. Our contributions are the scaffold and its single-agent
evaluation on social sycophancy with the controls that scope it, the
collective and its dissent sensor with the ablations that locate the
signal in the assigned roles, and a map of where each effect holds and
where it does not. Every experiment was pre-registered with a spending
ceiling and a stop rule before any model call, every interval is an
item-clustered bootstrap, and every citation was fetched at a primary
page.
