\subsection{Pillar 1, the scaffold reduces validation of the asker's account}
\label{sec-results-pillar1}

On every model the scaffold lowers the rate at which the response endorses
the asker's account, by 22.3 points on the model that moves least and 41.4
on the model that moves most,
% pillar1_headline.json: sonnet -22.3 [-31.3, -13.3]; nano -41.4 [-51.1, -31.4]
which is 31 to 55 percent of the control rate
% sanctioned_phrasing.relative
(Table~\ref{tab-pillar1}). The largest drop is also the least certain
because that model returned no response on 23 percent of scaffold items
against 3 percent of control items,
% nonresponse_not 0.227, nonresponse_cot 0.033
and scoring the missing responses at either extreme bounds its drop
between 24 and 50 points.
% delta_pp_bracket [-50.0, -24.0]
A control that asks the standard prompt for a response of the scaffold's
length raises validation rather than lowering it on three models
% 1b-RESULT-5 corrected verbose rates 0.760 / 0.827 / 0.780 vs CoT 0.627 / 0.727 / 0.759
and lowers it on the fourth,
% grok verbose 0.288
so length is not what carries the effect, though that control contains
three of the scaffold's instructions in prose and is a control on wording
as much as on length. Restricting both arms to responses of the same
length leaves drops of 35.6 and 24.6 points on the two models whose arms
share enough support to compare.
% length_matched nano -35.6 (119+85), grok -24.6 (141+75)

The reduction is concentrated where a counterparty is represented, at
the level of what the account contains.
On the 110 accounts that name a specific other party with an interest in
the outcome the pooled drop is 37.7 points, and on the 40 that do not it is
11.3, a difference of 26.3 points with an interval that excludes zero,
% counterparty_moderator.json pooled: -37.7 [-43.6, -31.8]; -11.3 [-18.5, -4.0]; diff -26.3 [-35.7, -17.0]
and the difference holds on three of the four models taken separately.
% per-generator diff CIs: haiku, nano, grok exclude 0; sonnet [-32.2, +5.9]
The residual drop on accounts with no counterparty is real and is the part
the represented-counterparty account does not explain.

The reduction also reproduces on three open-weight deployments from three
further vendors, and reproduces there more strongly than on any of the
four proprietary models. Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct falls 69.9 points, from
0.806 to 0.106, Mistral-Large-3-2 falls 58.2 points, and DeepSeek-V4-Pro
falls 45.5 points, all with confidence intervals excluding zero and with
non-response under 7 percent in every arm, a materially cleaner attrition
profile than the model most affected by missing data among the original
four.
% Addendum 17.1 RESULTS: Llama -69.9 [-77.7,-61.4], Mistral -58.2 [-66.2,-49.7],
% DeepSeek -45.5 [-53.8,-37.1]; non-response 4-7% each arm; nano's 22.7% for comparison
Seven models from six vendors, three of them open-weight, now show the
same negative, significant drop.
% pillar1_headline.json sanctioned_phrasing: 22 to 70 points, 31 to 87 percent

The claim survives a change of judge on most, not all, of these models. A
second judge from a different vendor confirms a negative, significant
drop on six of the seven; a third judge reverses the sign on three of
them, a pattern traced to that judge's own validation rate sitting near
ceiling on almost every response regardless of its content, which is
reported as a property of that judge and not as evidence against the
effect.
% Addendum 17.2 RESULTS: nano judge confirms 6/7 (sonnet's CI touches zero);
% Llama judge reverses sign on haiku/sonnet/nano, full_rate 0.80-1.00 on nearly every cell
One model, the one answering as sonnet, does not survive a change of
judge under either reading, and its reduction should be read as
judge-specific.

The discriminating test of which section carries the reduction does not
resolve the question at the level of the scaffold's own parts. Removing
one of the three candidate sections at a time, the stakeholder list, the
consequence trace, or the uncertainty statement, on the same 150 items
and the same three models, pooled across models no single section's
removal takes back a quarter of the reduction with a confidence interval
excluding zero. The pooled result hides real disagreement between
models. On the model with the smallest overall reduction, removing any
one of the three sections gives back close to half of it, so the whole
scaffold is load-bearing there and the result does not favour the
counterparty account over the competing hedging-register account. On the
two models with the largest reductions, removing any single section
gives back almost nothing, with a confidence interval that includes
zero on every knockout, so the reduction there does not depend on any
one part of the prompt at all. Moving the commitment to the front of the
scaffold instead of the end does not change the validation rate with a
confidence interval excluding zero on any model.
% Addendum 17.3 RESULTS: pooled NEITHER; haiku shares 0.42-0.55; grok/nano near zero;
% commit-first pooled -0.08 [-0.33, +0.08]
The represented-counterparty account is therefore supported by what an
account contains, an item that names a second party loses substantially
more validation than one that does not, and is not supported by which
sentence in the scaffold does the work. No single section is the carrier
of the effect, on most models the reduction is not attributable to any
one part, and where it is attributable at all it implicates the whole
scaffold rather than the sections that name the other parties
specifically.

The effect is on the register of the answer and not on its verdict. On the
mechanically scored dispute instrument the asker's own framing moves the
verdict toward the asker under both the scaffold and the control on two of
the three open-weight models and on neither under the third,
% Z3 RESULTS: Llama CoT +0.256 [+0.130, +0.406], NoT +0.270 [+0.092, +0.451]; Mistral CoT +0.127, NoT +0.299; DeepSeek both include 0
and the binary instrument returned a clean null on the original models.
% E1 clean null, HANDOFF_2026-08-26 §R1
Where the asker's pull reaches the verdict itself, the scaffold does not
remove it. Where a premise is false and no second party exists, the
scaffold affirms the falsehood more often on a forced verdict, 22.3
against 15.7 percent on a first pass,
% bm_neutral_ncot / bm_neutral_standard (16.15.5 S0 +0.067 [+0.033, +0.103])
and lowers false affirmation elsewhere by abstaining more rather than by
rejecting more.
% 16.15.6 RESULTS
These are the boundaries the theory owns.
