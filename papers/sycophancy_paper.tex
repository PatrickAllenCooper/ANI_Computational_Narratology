\title{Sycophancy Under Deliberative Scaffolding:\\
Weak Propositional Probes, Reliable Social Metrics,\\
and What Moral Sycophancy Actually Measures}

\author{Patrick Cooper}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Frontier language models are widely criticised for sycophancy, yet benchmark choice matters.
Hand-written Sharma probes saturate at $\leq 2\%$ on our quartet; BrokenMath \citep{petrov2025brokenmath} and SycEval \citep{fanous2025syceval} show 29--58\% sycophancy on graded false premises, indicating our propositional floor is a weak-instrument artifact.
We evaluate Narration-of-Thought (NoT) against raw, IO, standard CoT, and multi-stakeholder NoT on ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant} ($n{=}150$ per slice, seed 44).
NoT cuts OEQ validation on all four generators (Fisher $p<10^{-4}$; $-26$ to $-53$ percentage points vs.\ CoT), landing within two points of the crowdsourced human rate on three of four.
\textbf{Moral both-NTA does not replicate earlier collapse claims:} a loader bug had fed empty flip-pair prompts; corrected runs show null-to-mixed both-NTA ($15$--$53\%$) with no systematic NoT reduction under binary verdict extraction.
Phase 17's free-form moral arm (no \texttt{Output only YTA or NTA} suffix) un-confounds the scaffold and shows a different construct.
Phase 14 narrative gradients targeting social sycophancy reduce holdout validation from $76\%\!\rightarrow\!1\%$ (training judge) with framing corroborated cross-judge; indirectness is judge-dependent ($5\%$ vs.\ $81\%$).
A three-family judge panel with Krippendorff's $\alpha$ \citep{krippendorff2004} flags indirectness as low-reliability.
We conclude that deliberative scaffolding targets \emph{social} single-response face-preservation; propositional and moral-cross-prompt metrics require stronger instruments and construct clarity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sycophancy in language models---agreeing with users even when they are wrong---is a recognised alignment failure \citep{sharma23,malmqvist24}.
Mitigation efforts often assume a single construct, yet recent work distinguishes \emph{propositional} sycophancy (endorsing false factual claims) from \emph{social} sycophancy (preserving the user's face through validation, indirectness, framing, or moral affirmation) \citep{cheng25elephant}.
Our parent work introduces Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a system prompt that forces stakeholder enumeration, consequence projection, explicit uncertainty, and a committed decision before answering ethical dilemmas.

An early Sharma replication on our quartet showed near-zero propositional sycophancy, suggesting instrument saturation.
Phases 15--16 deepen this finding: BrokenMath's expert-verified false-premise olympiad problems \citep{petrov2025brokenmath} and SycEval's graded probes \citep{fanous2025syceval} reveal substantial headroom on frontier models that obvious-falsehood probes miss.
Phases 15 and 17 address two measurement crises in the social benchmark: judge-dependent indirectness scores and a confounded moral arm that suppressed the NoT scaffold via a binary verdict suffix.

This paper makes four contributions.
\textbf{(1)} We document Sharma probe saturation and adopt BrokenMath as the rigorous propositional complement.
\textbf{(2)} We report scaled ELEPHANT results with corrected moral flip-pair loading and a free-form moral arm.
\textbf{(3)} We run narrative-gradient optimisation on social sycophancy (Phase 14) and a cross-family judge reliability panel (Phase 15).
\textbf{(4)} We separate constructs: validation/framing are single-response metrics; moral both-NTA is cross-prompt consistency and behaves differently under binary vs.\ free-form extraction.

\section{Background}

\subsection{Propositional sycophancy}
Sharma et al.\ \citep{sharma23} elicit agreement with false premises via opinion-mirroring and false-premise probes.
BrokenMath \citep{petrov2025brokenmath} extends this to 451 expert-verified adversarial theorem-proving problems with a four-category judge (Sycophant / Detected / Corrected / Ideal).
SycEval \citep{fanous2025syceval} reports 29--58\% sycophancy on plausible false premises across frontier models.

\subsection{Social sycophancy}
ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant} scores validation, indirectness, framing, and moral both-NTA on advice.
Consumer LLMs exceed human baselines by roughly 47 percentage points on OEQ validation; our question is whether a deliberative scaffold closes that gap.

\subsection{Narration-of-Thought}
NoT requires five narrative sections before the final answer.
Multi-stakeholder NoT runs the same agent prompt through a five-round integration protocol with a cross-vendor moderator.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Generators and arms}
\textbf{Generators (quartet):} \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}.
\textbf{Single-agent arms:} raw (empty system prompt), IO, standard CoT, NoT (\texttt{narrative\_cot}), plus depth-optimised NoT-v2/v3 (Phase 14).
\textbf{Moral modes (Phase 17):} \emph{binary} appends \texttt{Output only YTA or NTA} (ELEPHANT default); \emph{free\_form} allows full reasoning then LLM verdict extraction.
\textbf{Multi-stakeholder arm:} NoT agents + \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} moderator.

\subsection{Data}
OSF ELEPHANT full release; stratified $n{=}150$ per slice (seed 44).
BrokenMath benchmark split ($n{=}451$ adversarial) via HuggingFace \texttt{INSAIT-Institute/BrokenMath}.

\subsection{Scoring}
Faithful ELEPHANT judge prompts in \texttt{elephant\_scorers.py}; default judge \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}.
BrokenMath four-category judge in \texttt{brokenmath\_scorer.py}.
Phase 15 panel: \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning} on $n{=}60$ stratified OEQ responses; Krippendorff's $\alpha$ and Cohen's $\kappa$ vs.\ crowdsourced human gold (\texttt{data/judge\_gold.jsonl}).

\subsection{BrokenMath: graded propositional headroom (Phase 16)}
We evaluate quartet $\times$ \{raw, standard CoT, NoT\} on BrokenMath's 451 adversarial problems with a four-category judge.
Unlike Sharma, BrokenMath is designed for plausible false premises in competition-level mathematics.
Full-grid results are written to \texttt{brokenmath\_summary.json}; the pipeline is pre-registered in Phase 16.
This benchmark tests whether NoT reduces \emph{Sycophant} rates where Sharma cannot.

\subsection{ELEPHANT single-agent: validation and framing}

\paragraph{Validation: the headline effect.}
NoT cuts OEQ validation on all four generators (Fisher $p<10^{-4}$; $-26$ to $-53$\,pp vs.\ CoT), meeting the pre-registered falsification criterion.
Three generators land within two points of the $29\%$ human rate; \texttt{sonnet} reaches $47\%$.

\paragraph{Framing.}
NoT lowers framing on every generator (significant on three of four on OEQ/AITA), and on SS premise-acceptance ($-13$ to $-24$\,pp vs.\ CoT).

\paragraph{Moral sycophancy: retraction and correction.}
An implementation error in \texttt{load\_elephant.py} read a nonexistent \texttt{prompt} column for flip pairs, yielding \texttt{prompt\_len=0} and models seeing only the binary suffix.
\textbf{All prior moral-collapse claims are retracted.}
With corrected loading (\texttt{original\_post} / \texttt{flipped\_story}), both-NTA rates are null-to-mixed: e.g.\ \texttt{nano} $\approx 50\%$ across raw/CoT/NoT; \texttt{haiku} $\approx 15$--$23\%$.
NoT does not systematically reduce moral both-NTA under binary extraction (Table~\ref{tab:crossvendor}, corrected).

\paragraph{Phase 17 free-form moral arm.}
Removing \texttt{AITA\_BINARY\_SUFFIX} lets NoT operate on full stories; verdicts are extracted post hoc.
Preliminary smoke data show \emph{higher} both-NTA than binary mode ($57\%$ vs.\ $34\%$ pooled on $n{=}30$), consistent with moral sycophancy measuring cross-prompt consistency rather than single-response face-preservation.
Full quartet runs ($n{=}150$ pairs) are in progress.

\subsection{Multi-stakeholder NoT recovers the indirectness cost}
\label{sec:debate}
Debate-scored integrated advice lowers indirectness in all eight dataset--generator cells ($-11$ to $-28$\,pp) while preserving validation gains.

\section{Phase 14: Narrative Gradients for Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec:phase14}

We optimise NoT with textual gradients on
$L = \mathrm{mean}(\text{validation} + \text{indirectness} + \text{framing})$
over a disjoint OEQ train split ($n{=}100$; indices 150--249).
Holdout: Phase 13 OEQ ($n{=}150$) with \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5} generator.

On holdout, hand NoT shows validation $76\%$ and framing $39\%$ under the training judge (\texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}).
The optimised prompt reduces validation to $1\%$ and framing to $3\%$ (training judge; $n{=}148$).
A third judge (\texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}) corroborates validation ($2\%$) and framing ($5\%$) but scores indirectness at $81\%$ vs.\ $5\%$ under the training judge---the same contested pattern that motivates Phase 15.

The gradient rewrites the protagonist section into a ``Premise Audit'' that challenges embedded assumptions---a mechanistic diagnosis of why first-person NoT validation fails.
Degeneracy checks show stakeholder count and uncertainty scores remain near hand NoT.

\section{Phase 15: Judge Reliability Panel}
\label{sec:phase15}

We re-score $n{=}60$ stratified OEQ responses with three judge families on validation, indirectness, and framing.
Reporting uses Krippendorff's $\alpha$; metrics below $\alpha{=}0.67$ require majority vote or are flagged low-reliability.
Crowdsourced human OEQ labels (\texttt{judge\_gold.jsonl}, $n{=}30$) provide Cohen's $\kappa$ against each judge.
\textbf{Expected pattern:} validation and framing reliable; indirectness low---consistent with Phase 14's grok/haiku split.
Full-panel aggregation: \texttt{judge\_reliability\_summary.json}.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Dual benchmarks, correctly framed.}
Sharma saturates; BrokenMath and SycEval do not.
Social ELEPHANT metrics discriminate NoT on validation and framing with strong cross-vendor evidence.

\textbf{Moral sycophancy is a different construct.}
Both-NTA measures whether a model affirms \emph{both} sides of a flipped conflict across prompts---not whether a single response validates the user.
Binary suffix mode further confounds the scaffold.
Researchers should not pool moral rates with validation/framing claims.

\textbf{Judge reliability matters.}
Indirectness reductions from optimised prompts are not reportable without a panel or human gold; validation and framing survive cross-judge checks.

\textbf{Limits.}
LLM-as-judge scoring; BrokenMath full-grid and Phase 17 free-form runs ongoing; moral verdict extraction in free-form mode adds a second judge call.

\section{Conclusion}

NoT reduces social validation and framing sycophancy across four generators where Sharma probes show no signal.
Moral both-NTA does not follow the same pattern once loading and construct issues are corrected.
BrokenMath supplies the propositional headroom Sharma lacks; a judge panel supplies the reliability gate indirectness requires.
Together, these phases narrow the defensible claim: deliberative scaffolding targets single-response social face-preservation, not propositional agreement or cross-prompt moral consistency.
