\begin{abstract}
Frontier language models are widely criticised for sycophancy, yet benchmark choice matters.
Hand-written Sharma probes saturate at $\leq 2\%$ on our quartet; on BrokenMath \citep{petrov2025brokenmath} the same models show $39$--$83\%$ sycophancy ($n{=}451$), confirming the propositional floor is a weak-instrument artifact.
We evaluate Narration-of-Thought (NoT) against raw, IO, standard CoT, and multi-stakeholder NoT on ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant} ($n{=}150$ per slice, seed 44).
NoT cuts OEQ validation on all four generators (Fisher $p<10^{-4}$; $-26$ to $-53$ percentage points vs.\ CoT), landing within two points of the crowdsourced human rate on three of four.
\textbf{The same scaffold does not transfer to propositional sycophancy:} on BrokenMath, NoT moves the Sycophant rate significantly on only one of three generators (\texttt{grok}, $-12$\,pp, $p<0.001$; \texttt{haiku}/\texttt{sonnet} null).
\textbf{Moral both-NTA does not replicate earlier collapse claims:} a loader bug had fed empty flip-pair prompts; corrected runs show null-to-mixed both-NTA with no NoT reduction under binary extraction, and Phase 17's free-form arm reveals the opposite sign---NoT \emph{raises} both-NTA ($34\%\!\rightarrow\!67\%$ pooled), confirming a distinct cross-prompt construct.
Phase 14 narrative gradients targeting social sycophancy reduce holdout validation from $76\%\!\rightarrow\!0.7\%$ (training judge), beating TextGrad, OPRO, and APE baselines; framing is corroborated cross-judge while indirectness is judge-dependent ($5\%$ vs.\ $81\%$).
A three-family judge panel with Krippendorff's $\alpha$ \citep{krippendorff2004} flags all three social metrics as low inter-judge reliability ($\alpha\leq0.42$), with validation alone showing strong judge-vs-human agreement ($\kappa\,0.73$--$0.86$).
We conclude that deliberative scaffolding targets \emph{social} single-response face-preservation; propositional and moral-cross-prompt metrics require stronger instruments and construct clarity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sycophancy in language models---agreeing with users even when they are wrong---is a recognised alignment failure \citep{sharma23,malmqvist24}.
Mitigation efforts often assume a single construct, yet recent work distinguishes \emph{propositional} sycophancy (endorsing false factual claims) from \emph{social} sycophancy (preserving the user's face through validation, indirectness, framing, or moral affirmation) \citep{cheng25elephant}.
Our parent work introduces Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a system prompt that forces stakeholder enumeration, consequence projection, explicit uncertainty, and a committed decision before answering ethical dilemmas.

An early Sharma replication on our quartet showed near-zero propositional sycophancy, suggesting instrument saturation.
Phases 15--16 deepen this finding: BrokenMath's expert-verified false-premise olympiad problems \citep{petrov2025brokenmath} and SycEval's graded probes \citep{fanous2025syceval} reveal substantial headroom on frontier models that obvious-falsehood probes miss.
Phases 15 and 17 address two measurement crises in the social benchmark: judge-dependent indirectness scores and a confounded moral arm that suppressed the NoT scaffold via a binary verdict suffix.

This paper makes four contributions.
\textbf{(1)} We document Sharma probe saturation and adopt BrokenMath as the rigorous propositional complement.
\textbf{(2)} We report scaled ELEPHANT results with corrected moral flip-pair loading and a free-form moral arm.
\textbf{(3)} We run narrative-gradient optimisation on social sycophancy (Phase 14) and a cross-family judge reliability panel (Phase 15).
\textbf{(4)} We separate constructs: validation/framing are single-response metrics; moral both-NTA is cross-prompt consistency and behaves differently under binary vs.\ free-form extraction.

\section{Background}

\subsection{Propositional sycophancy}
Sharma et al.\ \citep{sharma23} elicit agreement with false premises via opinion-mirroring and false-premise probes.
BrokenMath \citep{petrov2025brokenmath} extends this to 451 expert-verified adversarial theorem-proving problems with a four-category judge (Sycophant / Detected / Corrected / Ideal).
SycEval \citep{fanous2025syceval} reports 29--58\% sycophancy on plausible false premises across frontier models.

\subsection{Social sycophancy}
ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant} scores validation, indirectness, framing, and moral both-NTA on advice.
Consumer LLMs exceed human baselines by roughly 47 percentage points on OEQ validation; our question is whether a deliberative scaffold closes that gap.

\subsection{Narration-of-Thought}
NoT requires five narrative sections before the final answer.
Multi-stakeholder NoT runs the same agent prompt through a five-round integration protocol with a cross-vendor moderator.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Generators and arms}
\textbf{Generators (quartet):} \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}.
\textbf{Single-agent arms:} raw (empty system prompt), IO, standard CoT, NoT (\texttt{narrative\_cot}), plus depth-optimised NoT-v2/v3 (Phase 14).
\textbf{Moral modes (Phase 17):} \emph{binary} appends \texttt{Output only YTA or NTA} (ELEPHANT default); \emph{free\_form} allows full reasoning then LLM verdict extraction.
\textbf{Multi-stakeholder arm:} NoT agents + \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} moderator.

\subsection{Data}
OSF ELEPHANT full release; stratified $n{=}150$ per slice (seed 44).
BrokenMath benchmark split ($n{=}451$ adversarial) via HuggingFace \texttt{INSAIT-Institute/BrokenMath}.

\subsection{Scoring}
Faithful ELEPHANT judge prompts in \texttt{elephant\_scorers.py}; default judge \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}.
BrokenMath four-category judge in \texttt{brokenmath\_scorer.py}.
Phase 15 panel: \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning} on $n{=}60$ stratified OEQ responses; Krippendorff's $\alpha$ and Cohen's $\kappa$ vs.\ crowdsourced human gold (\texttt{data/judge\_gold.jsonl}).

\subsection{BrokenMath: graded propositional headroom (Phase 16)}
We evaluate quartet $\times$ \{raw, standard CoT, NoT\} on BrokenMath's 451 adversarial problems with a four-category judge (Sycophant / Detected / Corrected / Ideal).
Unlike Sharma, BrokenMath is designed for plausible false premises in competition-level mathematics.

\textbf{The instrument is not saturated.} Standard-CoT Sycophant rates span $51\%$ (\texttt{grok}) to $83\%$ (\texttt{haiku})---two orders of magnitude above the Sharma floor (Table~\ref{tab:brokenmath}).
\textbf{NoT does not generalise to propositional sycophancy.} The validation/framing reductions that hold on ELEPHANT do \emph{not} transfer: NoT moves the Sycophant rate by $-3.5$\,pp on \texttt{haiku} ($p{=}0.20$) and $+3.1$\,pp on \texttt{sonnet} ($p{=}0.34$), neither significant.
The single exception is \texttt{grok}, where NoT cuts sycophancy $51\%\!\rightarrow\!39\%$ ($-12$\,pp, Fisher $p{<}0.001$)---the only generator that exposes substantial \emph{Detected} and \emph{Ideal} headroom under the scaffold.
This sharpens the paper's central claim: deliberative scaffolding targets social face-preservation, not the propositional error-detection that BrokenMath measures.

\subsection{ELEPHANT single-agent: validation and framing}

\paragraph{Validation: the headline effect.}
NoT cuts OEQ validation on all four generators (Fisher $p<10^{-4}$; $-26$ to $-53$\,pp vs.\ CoT), meeting the pre-registered falsification criterion.
Three generators land within two points of the $29\%$ human rate; \texttt{sonnet} reaches $47\%$.

\paragraph{Framing.}
NoT lowers framing on every generator (significant on three of four on OEQ/AITA), and on SS premise-acceptance ($-13$ to $-24$\,pp vs.\ CoT).

\paragraph{Moral sycophancy: retraction and correction.}
An implementation error in \texttt{load\_elephant.py} read a nonexistent \texttt{prompt} column for flip pairs, yielding \texttt{prompt\_len=0} and models seeing only the binary suffix.
\textbf{All prior moral-collapse claims are retracted.}
With corrected loading (\texttt{original\_post} / \texttt{flipped\_story}), both-NTA rates are null-to-mixed: e.g.\ \texttt{nano} $\approx 50\%$ across raw/CoT/NoT; \texttt{haiku} $\approx 15$--$23\%$.
NoT does not systematically reduce moral both-NTA under binary extraction (Table~\ref{tab:crossvendor}, corrected).

\paragraph{Phase 17 free-form moral arm.}
Removing \texttt{AITA\_BINARY\_SUFFIX} lets NoT operate on full stories; verdicts are extracted post hoc by an LLM.
The full quartet run ($n{=}150$ pairs per cell) shows that free-form both-NTA is \emph{higher} than binary mode and that \textbf{NoT increases rather than decreases moral both-NTA} once the scaffold is un-suppressed (Table~\ref{tab:moralfree}).
Pooled both-NTA rises from $34\%$ (binary) to $67\%$ (free-form); under NoT, \texttt{haiku} moves $15\%\!\rightarrow\!93\%$ and \texttt{sonnet} $17\%\!\rightarrow\!72\%$.
This is the opposite sign of the validation/framing effect and confirms moral both-NTA measures cross-prompt consistency rather than single-response face-preservation: when NoT enumerates stakeholders and refuses to take sides, it affirms \emph{both} parties of a flipped conflict more often, not less.

\subsection{Multi-stakeholder NoT recovers the indirectness cost}
\label{sec:debate}
Debate-scored integrated advice lowers indirectness in all eight dataset--generator cells ($-11$ to $-28$\,pp) while preserving validation gains.

\section{Phase 14: Narrative Gradients for Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec:phase14}

We optimise NoT with textual gradients on
$L = \mathrm{mean}(\text{validation} + \text{indirectness} + \text{framing})$
over a disjoint OEQ train split ($n{=}100$; indices 150--249).
Holdout: Phase 13 OEQ ($n{=}150$) with \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5} generator.

On holdout, hand NoT shows validation $76\%$, framing $39\%$, and indirectness $16\%$ under the training judge (\texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}; pooled loss $0.44$).
The optimised prompt reduces validation to $0.7\%$, framing to $3\%$, and indirectness to $5\%$ (training judge; $n{=}148$; loss $0.03$).
A third judge (\texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}) corroborates validation ($2\%$) and framing ($5\%$) but scores indirectness at $81\%$ vs.\ $5\%$ under the training judge---the same contested pattern that motivates Phase 15.

\paragraph{Optimiser comparison.}
We benchmark narrative gradients against three matched-budget baselines ($\sim$10 prompt evaluations each): TextGrad on standard CoT, OPRO, and APE (Table~\ref{tab:optimizer}).
Narrative gradients achieve the lowest holdout loss ($0.03$), beating APE ($0.10$), TextGrad-CoT ($0.17$), and OPRO ($0.20$).
This supports the pre-registered hypothesis that scaffold structure---not merely iterative optimisation---carries the social-sycophancy reduction: the two narrative-initialised methods (narrative grad, APE) outperform the CoT-initialised TextGrad baseline, and trajectory-based narrative gradients beat the others outright.

The gradient rewrites the protagonist section into a ``Premise Audit'' that challenges embedded assumptions---a mechanistic diagnosis of why first-person NoT validation fails.
Degeneracy checks show stakeholder count and uncertainty scores remain near hand NoT.

\section{Phase 15: Judge Reliability Panel}
\label{sec:phase15}

We re-score $n{=}60$ stratified OEQ responses with three judge families (\texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}) on validation, indirectness, and framing.
Reporting uses Krippendorff's $\alpha$; metrics below $\alpha{=}0.67$ require majority vote or are flagged low-reliability.
Crowdsourced human OEQ labels (\texttt{judge\_gold.jsonl}, $n{=}30$) provide Cohen's $\kappa$ against each judge, scored on the \emph{human} responses directly.

\textbf{Inter-judge reliability is low on all three metrics} (Table~\ref{tab:judge}): validation $\alpha{=}0.42$, framing $\alpha{=}0.11$, indirectness $\alpha{=}-0.23$. None clears the $0.67$ threshold, so panel headline rates use majority vote.
The picture is more favourable against human gold: validation agreement is strong for every judge (Cohen's $\kappa$ vs.\ human $0.73$--$0.86$), indirectness is moderate and judge-dependent ($0.31$--$0.63$), and framing is mixed ($0.15$--$0.70$).
The contrast---high judge-vs-human $\kappa$ on validation but only moderate \emph{inter-judge} $\alpha$---reflects the narrow positive base rate among the gold human responses; the practical implication is that validation is the only ELEPHANT metric we report without a reliability caveat, and indirectness reductions from optimised prompts (Section~\ref{sec:phase14}) are not reportable from any single judge.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Dual benchmarks, correctly framed.}
Sharma saturates at $\leq 2\%$; BrokenMath does not ($39$--$83\%$).
Crucially, the scaffold that reliably reduces \emph{social} sycophancy on ELEPHANT does not transfer to BrokenMath's \emph{propositional} error-detection (significant only on \texttt{grok}), evidence that the two are distinct constructs rather than a single ``sycophancy'' axis.
Social ELEPHANT metrics discriminate NoT on validation and framing with strong cross-vendor evidence.

\textbf{Moral sycophancy is a different construct.}
Both-NTA measures whether a model affirms \emph{both} sides of a flipped conflict across prompts---not whether a single response validates the user.
Binary suffix mode further confounds the scaffold.
Researchers should not pool moral rates with validation/framing claims.

\textbf{Judge reliability matters.}
Indirectness reductions from optimised prompts are not reportable without a panel or human gold; validation survives cross-judge checks against human labels, while framing is mixed.

\section{Conclusion}

NoT reduces social validation and framing sycophancy across four generators where Sharma probes show no signal.
Moral both-NTA does not follow the same pattern once loading and construct issues are corrected.
BrokenMath supplies the propositional headroom Sharma lacks; a judge panel supplies the reliability gate indirectness requires.
Together, these phases narrow the defensible claim: deliberative scaffolding targets single-response social face-preservation, not propositional agreement or cross-prompt moral consistency.

\section*{Limitations}
All sycophancy outcomes are scored by LLM judges; while Phase 15 validates the validation metric against human gold ($\kappa\,0.73$--$0.86$), indirectness and framing carry low inter-judge reliability and should be read as judge-conditioned.
The optimiser comparison uses a single training judge (\texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}) and a single generator (\texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}) for tractability; cross-generator optimisation is left to future work.
The Phase 14 result that narrative gradients beat other optimisers isolates \emph{outcome}, not \emph{mechanism}: we have not yet ablated which narrative sections carry the effect, so causal attribution to specific scaffold components remains open.
BrokenMath uses a single judge family, and the free-form moral arm adds a second verdict-extraction call whose own reliability we do not separately audit.
Sample sizes are $n{=}150$ per ELEPHANT slice and $n{=}451$ for BrokenMath; effects below a few points may be underpowered.
All generators are accessed through a single hosted provider, and results may not transfer to other deployments or model versions.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}
This work studies sycophancy mitigation and does not involve new human-subjects data collection; human labels are drawn from the public ELEPHANT/OSF release and used only as reliability gold.
Reducing sycophancy is dual-use: a scaffold that makes models less validating can also make them blunt or dismissive in genuinely sensitive contexts, and the Phase 17 finding that NoT \emph{increases} moral both-NTA shows that ``less sycophantic'' is not uniformly desirable.
We therefore frame NoT as a measurement and construct-clarification tool, not a deployment-ready safety intervention, and we caution against optimising single sycophancy metrics in isolation.
No personally identifying information is processed, and all adversarial math problems come from the public BrokenMath benchmark.
