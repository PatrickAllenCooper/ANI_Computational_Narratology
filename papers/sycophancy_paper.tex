\title{Sycophancy Under Deliberative Scaffolding:\\
Propositional Probes Saturate While Social Sycophancy\\
Responds to Narration-of-Thought}

\author{Patrick Cooper}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Frontier language models are widely criticised for sycophancy, yet benchmark choice matters: propositional sycophancy probes often saturate at the floor on modern generators, while social sycophancy---excessive face-preservation in advice---remains elevated tens of percentage points above human baselines.
We replicate Sharma-style SycophancyEval on a verified four-generator panel and find near-zero sycophancy rates ($\leq 2.2\%$ per cell), confirming instrument saturation.
We then evaluate Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a five-section deliberative scaffold, against a literature-comparable \emph{raw} one-shot arm (no system prompt), plain IO, standard chain-of-thought, and multi-stakeholder NoT on the ELEPHANT benchmark \citep{cheng25elephant} at $n{=}150$ per dataset slice across OEQ, AITA-YTA, SS, and moral-flip pairs.
NoT cuts open-ended-advice validation sycophancy on \emph{all four} generators (Fisher $p<10^{-4}$ each; $-26$ to $-53$ percentage points vs.\ CoT), landing within two points of the crowdsourced human rate on three of the four, and collapses moral sycophancy on the two generators where it is at ceiling ($97\%\!\rightarrow\!19\%$ and $94\%\!\rightarrow\!1\%$ both-sides affirmation).
The scaffold's one cost is a small, non-significant rise in indirectness---and the multi-stakeholder integration layer pays that cost back, lowering indirectness in every dataset--generator cell ($-11$ to $-28$ points) while preserving the validation gains.
We conclude that deliberative scaffolding targets \emph{social} rather than \emph{propositional} sycophancy; the two benchmark families are complementary, not redundant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Sycophancy in language models---agreeing with users even when they are wrong---is a recognised alignment failure \citep{sharma23,malmqvist24}.
Mitigation efforts often assume a single construct, yet recent work distinguishes \emph{propositional} sycophancy (endorsing false factual claims) from \emph{social} sycophancy (preserving the user's face through validation, indirectness, framing, or moral affirmation) \citep{cheng25elephant}.
Our parent work introduces Narration-of-Thought (NoT), a system prompt that forces stakeholder enumeration, consequence projection, explicit uncertainty, and a committed decision before answering ethical dilemmas.
An advisor suggested evaluating NoT on ELEPHANT because our Sharma SycophancyEval replication showed no headroom: every generator--condition cell sat at or near $0\%$ propositional sycophancy, so the instrument could not discriminate NoT from baselines.

This paper makes three contributions.
\textbf{(1)} We document propositional probe saturation on a four-generator panel and argue that social-sycophancy benchmarks remain necessary for scaffold evaluation.
\textbf{(2)} We run a scaled ELEPHANT study ($n{=}150$ stratified subsample per slice, seed 44) with four single-agent arms---raw (no system prompt, matching ELEPHANT's published generation protocol), plain IO, standard CoT, and NoT---scored against crowdsourced human references, plus multi-stakeholder NoT debates scored on integrated consensus advice.
\textbf{(3)} We report per-model panels across \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, and \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}, enabling cross-vendor claims beyond a single budget model.

\section{Background}

\subsection{Propositional sycophancy}
Sharma et al.\ \citep{sharma23} elicit agreement with false premises via opinion-mirroring and false-premise probes.
We replicate their protocol in \texttt{scripts/run\_sycophancyeval.py} with standard CoT and NoT conditions.

\subsection{Social sycophancy}
ELEPHANT \citep{cheng25elephant} scores four face-preservation dimensions on advice and subjective statements: \emph{validation} (affirming the user's stance), \emph{indirectness} (hedging instead of direct guidance), \emph{framing} (accepting unstated premises), and \emph{moral} sycophancy (affirming both sides of a flipped moral conflict).
Consumer LLMs exceed human baselines by roughly 47 percentage points on OEQ validation; our question is whether a deliberative scaffold closes that gap.

\subsection{Narration-of-Thought}
NoT requires five narrative sections (protagonist, stakeholders, consequences, uncertainty, decision) before the final answer.
Multi-stakeholder NoT runs the same agent prompt through a five-round integration protocol with a cross-vendor moderator, yielding consensus advice rather than a single-agent monologue.

\section{Method}

\subsection{Generators and arms}
\textbf{Generators (quartet):} \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}, \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}, \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}.
\textbf{Single-agent arms:}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Raw} --- empty system prompt; bare user turn only (ELEPHANT \texttt{get\_responses\_*} protocol).
  \item \textbf{IO} --- ``You are a helpful assistant. Answer directly and concisely.''
  \item \textbf{Std CoT} --- step-by-step reasoning instruction.
  \item \textbf{NoT} --- five-section narrative scaffold (verbatim \texttt{narrative\_cot} prompt).
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Multi-stakeholder arm:} NoT agents + \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} moderator; score \texttt{integrated\_description}.
\textbf{Human reference:} crowdsourced responses on OEQ/AITA-YTA scored with identical judges.

\subsection{Data}
Full OSF \texttt{datasets.zip} (file GUID download via OSF API when legacy node URL returns HTTP 500).
Stratified subsample $n{=}150$ per slice (seed 44): OEQ, AITA-YTA, SS (framing only), and 150 OG/FLIP moral pairs.

\subsection{Scoring}
Faithful ELEPHANT judge prompts in \texttt{scripts/elephant\_scorers.py}; default judge \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}.
Empty generations (flagged \texttt{empty\_response}) are excluded from rate denominators.
Wilson 95\% confidence intervals; Fisher exact tests on arm differences.

\subsection{Pre-registered falsification}
NoT must lower validation+framing on OEQ vs standard CoT for a majority of quartet models, or the social-sycophancy claim weakens.
Raw establishes the literature floor; multi-stakeholder NoT should move toward human validation rates on OEQ (directional).

\subsection{ELEPHANT single-agent}

\paragraph{Validation: the headline effect.}
Table~\ref{tab:elephant_main} walks the arms in order of increasing structure for \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}; Table~\ref{tab:crossvendor} replicates the key contrast across the quartet.
The raw arm reproduces ELEPHANT's published picture: models validate users at $66$--$83\%$ on open-ended advice against a $29\%$ human rate, a $+37$ to $+54$-point gap.
Neither a helpful-assistant system prompt (IO) nor step-by-step reasoning (CoT) closes it---CoT leaves validation essentially where raw put it.
NoT does: OEQ validation drops to $27$--$29\%$ on three of the four generators (the fourth, \texttt{sonnet}, reaches $47\%$), within two points of the human rate.
Every per-generator reduction vs.\ CoT is individually significant (Fisher $p<10^{-4}$; $-26$ to $-53$\,pp), meeting the pre-registered falsification criterion on all four models rather than the required majority.

\paragraph{Framing and subjective statements.}
Framing falls under NoT on every generator (significant on three of four: haiku $-41$\,pp, sonnet $-30$\,pp, grok $-13$\,pp; nano $-10$\,pp at $p{=}0.09$), and on haiku NoT's $23\%$ undercuts even the human rate ($61\%$).
The same direction holds on SS, where NoT lowers premise-acceptance on all four generators ($-13$ to $-24$\,pp vs.\ CoT).

\paragraph{Moral sycophancy: collapse where there is headroom.}
The both-NTA rate on flipped AITA pairs splits the quartet (Table~\ref{tab:crossvendor}): the Claude models refuse to affirm both sides almost regardless of prompt ($0$--$10\%$), but \texttt{nano} and \texttt{grok} affirm both sides of the same moral conflict on $94$--$99\%$ of pairs under raw and CoT.
NoT collapses this to $19\%$ and $1\%$ respectively---the largest single effect in the study, and invisible in a haiku-only analysis.

\paragraph{The honest cost: indirectness.}
NoT nudges indirectness up on OEQ ($+1$ to $+7$\,pp, none significant) and more visibly on AITA-YTA ($95\%$ vs.\ $74\%$ CoT on haiku).
No single-agent arm approaches the human indirectness rate ($21\%$ on OEQ vs.\ $71$--$97\%$ for every model arm): hedged delivery is the one face-preserving habit that prompting alone does not remove.
Section~\ref{sec:debate} shows the multi-stakeholder layer recovers exactly this metric.

\subsection{Multi-stakeholder NoT recovers the indirectness cost}
\label{sec:debate}
Debate-scored integrated advice on OEQ and AITA-YTA ($n{=}50$ per generator, \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} moderator) improves on single-agent NoT precisely where the scaffold underperformed.
Indirectness falls in \emph{all eight} dataset--generator cells ($-11$ to $-28$\,pp; e.g.\ OEQ sonnet $64\%$ vs.\ $92\%$ single-agent), consistent with the integration round forcing a concrete consensus recommendation where a lone narrator hedges.
OEQ validation also falls on all four generators (Table~\ref{tab:crossvendor}, Debate row), pushing \texttt{grok} to $8\%$---below the human rate.
The exception is AITA-YTA validation, where debate is mixed (\texttt{nano} rises $8\%\!\rightarrow\!36\%$ while sonnet falls $32\%\!\rightarrow\!20\%$): integrating multiple perspectives on a post whose author is in the wrong can soften the verdict.
We therefore read the debate layer as a complement, not a replacement: single-agent NoT delivers the verdict-level gains, and integration converts them into direct, committed advice.

\section{Phase 14: Narrative Gradients for Social Sycophancy}
\label{sec:phase14}

Phase 13 established that hand-designed NoT reduces ELEPHANT social sycophancy.
Phases 10--10b optimised NoT for deliberative \emph{depth} (stakeholder count, uncertainty score) but never evaluated those prompts on ELEPHANT.
Phase 14 joins the narrative-gradient line to the sycophancy benchmark and compares optimizer families on a shared sycophancy loss.

\subsection{Track 1: Depth-optimised NoT on ELEPHANT}
We evaluate \texttt{narrative\_cot\_v2} (Phase 10, in-family training judge) and \texttt{narrative\_cot\_v3} (Phase 10b, cross-family judge) on the Phase 13 ELEPHANT grid ($n{=}150$, seed 44).
This tests whether depth-targeted optimisation preserves the hand prompt's social-sycophancy reduction or trades it away.

\subsection{Track 2: Sycophancy-targeted prompt optimisation}
We run textual-gradient descent initialised at verbatim NoT, optimising
$L = \mathrm{mean}(\text{validation} + \text{indirectness} + \text{framing})$
on a disjoint OEQ train split (indices 150--249 of the seed-44 shuffle; $n{=}100$).
Holdout evaluation uses the Phase 13 OEQ set (indices 0--149) plus FLIP moral pairs.
Training uses \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5} as generator, \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning} as cross-family training judge (following Phase 10b), and \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6} as optimiser \citep{yuksekgonul2024textgrad}.

\subsection{Optimizer baselines}
On the identical loss and train split we compare:
\textbf{(i)} TextGrad-on-standard-CoT (init \texttt{standard\_cot});
\textbf{(ii)} OPRO \citep{yang2023opro} (trajectory meta-prompt, no textual gradient step);
\textbf{(iii)} APE \citep{zhou2022ape} (one-shot candidate sampling and selection).
Each baseline receives $\sim$10 prompt evaluations.
Explicit ``do not be sycophantic'' instructions underperform structural mitigation \citep{askdonttell2026}; our optimisers target scaffold structure rather than negation.

\subsection{Degeneracy guard}
Holdout responses are re-coded with the Phase 1 deliberative rubric (stakeholder count, uncertainty score) to detect whether the optimiser gutted the narrative scaffold to win on sycophancy metrics.

Scripts: \texttt{syco\_loss.py}, \texttt{run\_phase14\_syco\_grad.py}, \texttt{run\_phase14\_tg\_cot.py}, \texttt{run\_phase14\_opro.py}, \texttt{run\_phase14\_ape.py}, \texttt{run\_phase14\_holdout.py}, \texttt{aggregate\_phase14.py}.
Pre-registration: Phase 14 block in \texttt{Guidance\_Documents/study\_design.md}.

\section{Discussion}

\textbf{Complementary benchmarks.}
Propositional probes saturated; social probes discriminated.
Had we evaluated NoT on SycophancyEval alone, the verdict would have been a false null---both conditions at $0$--$2\%$.
Researchers evaluating deliberative prompts should report both families.

\textbf{Why structure matters and instructions do not.}
The arm ordering in Tables~\ref{tab:elephant_main} and~\ref{tab:crossvendor} isolates \emph{what kind} of prompting moves social sycophancy.
A generic system prompt trims validation a little ($-8$ to $-33$\,pp raw$\to$IO) but inflates framing on all four generators (e.g.\ grok $70\%\!\rightarrow\!84\%$): told to ``answer directly,'' models accept the user's premises and proceed.
Step-by-step reasoning changes almost nothing.
Only the scaffold that names stakeholders, projects consequences, and forces a committed decision shifts every face-preservation metric with headroom---suggesting the gains come from making the model represent parties other than the user, not from added deliberation length.

\textbf{A two-layer reading.}
The single-agent scaffold and the integration layer fix different failures: NoT removes \emph{whose-side-am-I-on} sycophancy (validation, framing, moral affirmation) but hedges its delivery; the debate's consensus round removes the hedging.
Neither layer alone produces advice that is simultaneously non-validating and direct.

\textbf{Limits.}
LLM-as-judge scoring inherits ELEPHANT's judge assumptions; multi-stakeholder runs are costly; moral pairs depend on YTA/NTA extraction heuristics.
AITA-YTA validation under debate is a genuine mixed cell, and no arm---including debate---reaches the human indirectness rate.
Vendor asymmetry in the moral metric (Claude at floor, nano/grok at ceiling) means pooled moral rates would mislead; we report per model.

\section{Conclusion}

Deliberative scaffolding via NoT reduces \emph{social} sycophancy---validation, framing, and moral both-sides affirmation---exactly where Sharma-style propositional probes show no signal, on all four generators tested, and the multi-stakeholder integration layer converts those gains into direct rather than hedged advice.
A raw one-shot baseline, quartet-wide replication, and multi-stakeholder integration make this a standalone complement to our parent Narration-of-Thought work.
