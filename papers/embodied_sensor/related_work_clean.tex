\section{Related Work}
\label{sec-related}

\paragraph{Sycophancy is a trained, steerable bias, one of a family.}
Preference-tuned models repeat the answer the user prefers
\citep{perez2023discovering,sharma23}, validate the asker's account of a
conflict \citep{cheng25elephant,cheng2026prosocial}, abandon a correct answer
under pushback
\citep{fanous2025syceval,malmqvist24,hong2025sycophancy,nikeghbal2026flips},
and it has surfaced in deployment \citep{openai_sycophancy_2025}. The pull has
other doors, a false premise surviving into a task though the model registers
the error \citep{chen2026knowing,petrov2025brokenmath}, the answer folding
under pressure while the reasoning holds \citep{li2026chain}, and a persona
shifting the answer, which a benchmark may record as deference when it is
personalization \citep{batzner2025claims}. The bias is an activation direction
one can add or remove
\citep{rimsky2024caa,chen2025persona,vennemeyer2025separation}, a sceptical
persona recovers most of what targeted steering achieves
\citep{kelkar2026devil}, and remedies exist in training
\citep{wei2023synthetic,chen2024yesmen} and in a third-person perspective at
inference \citep{hong2025sycophancy}. We read the three as one family, a
conclusion fixed by the prompt, and place the pull on purpose, a copy told its
side taking it on 99.7 and 97.4 percent of opening statements, while
narration, first person with no side, cuts validation of the asker by 22 to 41
points yet leaves one model affirming a false premise more often, so
assignment is a lever, not a cure.

\paragraph{Role assignment controls what a copy says, at a price.}
A persona prompt moves output in the direction it implies
\citep{shanahan2023roleplay,salewski2023impersonation,li2023camel}, within
asymmetric limits
\citep{miehling2025steerability,liu2024persona,santurkar2023opinions}, and
moves accuracy either way
\citep{zheng2024helpful,kim2025persona,kong2024roleplay}. Assigned personas
carry bias \citep{gupta2024bias,deshpande2023toxicity,dash2026motivated},
agreeable ones amplify sycophancy \citep{shah2026toonice}, and simulated
debaters drift toward the model's own view \citep{taubenfeld2024systematic}.
\citet{dietrich2026roles} comes closest, with opposed advocates, a balanced
seat and a supervisor, and finds 39 to 67 percent role fidelity collapsing on
unambiguous facts. We measure the same quantity as role-lock, near total on
one model (0.97) and soft on another (0.44), and unchanged with the seats'
reads cut or narration removed. A like-biased second advocate copies its
principal yet pulls the moderator's verdict twelve points its way and costs
seven points of accuracy, and a third party's advocate breaks the
one-dimensional stake the flag needs. We add the control's price, that a
locked seat's verdict carries nothing about the case and that seat count is a
persuasion channel a naive moderator does not resist, so assignment is a lever
paid for at the verdict.

\paragraph{Debate puts truth at a judge whose competence sets the verdict, and disagreement is a signal.}
Scalable oversight puts two opposed agents before a judge \citep{irving18},
who gains from debate
\citep{michael2023debate,khan2024debating,rahman2025controversial,arnesen2024selfplay,lang2025debate}
only under information asymmetry \citep{kenton2024scalable}, only by
verifying, not summarising \citep{elasky2026weak}, in step with its capability
\citep{engels2025scaling}, and not when it shares the speakers' errors
\citep{goel2025alike}. Debate among copies of one model helps some benchmarks
\citep{du23,liang2024divergent,chan2023chateval} and not others
\citep{smit2024mad,wang2024rethinking,zhang2025overvaluing}, since copies
converge to the majority in the room
\citep{estornell2024multillm,zhu2026demystifying,choi2025debateorvote}, mixed
panels yield to a peer's flawed reasoning even when stronger models are the
majority \citep{wynn2025talk}, opposed positions invite cheap talk
\citep{chen2026fail}, and instructed dissent, like instructed confidence, is
not evidence \citep{qian2026layered,prasad2025win}. A second lineage treats
disagreement itself, resolving it \citep{jiang2026disagreement} or reading it
through cross-examination \citep{cohen2023lmvslm}, a verifier's check
\citep{kirchner2024prover,browncohen2024doubly,browncohen2025prover,hammond2025neural}
or variation across one model's samples
\citep{wang2023selfconsistency,manakul2023selfcheck,farquhar2024semantic,xiong2024confidence},
and such signals decide when to overturn a majority
\citep{he2026minoritysentinel} or escalate to a stronger model or human
reviewer
\citep{chen2023frugal,aggarwal2024automix,gupta2024cascades,yue2024cascades,jung2025escalate,wang2026defer,jitkrittum2023deferral}.
Our seats hold opposed interests, not opposed answers, so neither holds the
truth by construction, and where a verdict names one loser exactly one seat
has reason to object, so no brief explains a second objector. We add that
flag, needing no labels, logits or second sample, which marks cases wrong 9.8
times as often on one-loser verdicts, survives cutting every read between
seats and cutting narration, firing a third less often, and routes the flagged
fifth to a stronger uninterested judge for a gain over the collective, though
the sonnet judge everywhere does better and the solo's own sample disagreement
does as much.

\paragraph{Topology decides what a collective aggregates, and same-model seats aggregate to one.}
Debate graphs can be sparse \citep{li2024sparse}, scaled and optimised
\citep{qian2025macnet,zhuge2024gptswarm,liu2024dylan}, topology governs error
propagation \citep{shen2025propagation}, centralised aggregation is hostage to
its hub \citep{han2026conformity}, agents conform to a majority
\citep{zhang2024socialpsych,weng2025conformity,bertalanic2026costconsensus},
and effective team size is fittable \citep{bertalanic2026ringelmann}.
Correlated error bounds aggregation and rises as models improve
\citep{kim2025correlated,goel2025alike}. Mixed panels beat same-model panels
only sometimes \citep{wang2025moa,li2025rethinking}, a majority beats its best
member in a tenth of three-model panels \citep{kim2026diversity}, and
cross-model gains need learned weights, not a vote \citep{douven2026wisdom}.
Our three same-model seats never beat their best seat, locked or not, and a
three-vendor panel with less correlated error (0.25 against 0.65) does not
beat its best member at the opening statement and sits below the best model
alone. Over byte-identical locked seats a stronger moderator moves the verdict
up four points, a weaker one down twelve. We add the structure of dissent as
the outcome and the reading that the moderator's competence sets the accuracy
while the seats supply the dissent, so the moderator should be one strong
model.

\paragraph{Jury theorems, network learning and advocacy theory supplied the design and predict the measurements.}
Correlated votes can help or hurt a majority
\citep{ladha1992condorcet,boland1989majority,dietrich2013independent}, the
optimal rule over unequal voters weights by log-odds
\citep{nitzan1982optimal,shapley1984optimizing}, a shared bias needs a
non-majoritarian rule \citep{prelec2017solution}, and a network of averagers
reaches the truth only when no node dominates
\citep{degroot1974reaching,golub2010naive}. Advocacy theory supplies the
seats, as a naive decider can rely on competition among opposed parties
\citep{milgrom1986relying,froeb1996naive}, advocates beat a nonpartisan
investigator \citep{dewatripont1999advocates}, conflict guarantees a mistaken
inference is corrected \citep{lipman1995robust}, an adversarial procedure
allocates the burden of proof \citep{shin1998adversarial}, two experts help
only with opposed biases \citep{krishna2001model}, adding senders need not add
information \citep{gentzkow2017competition}, a large bias babbles
\citep{crawford1982strategic}, senders must span the case's dimensions
\citep{battaglini2002multiple}, and an objection is read relative to what it
counters \citep{glazer2001debates,glazer2004optimal}. Across fields a
statement against interest is credible since the speaker had reason not to
make it \citep{fre2024statement,jefferson1944declarations}, given what the
receiver infers of the speaker's motives \citep{sobel1985credibility} and
costly fabrication \citep{sanchirico2001relying}. We supply an instance where
these predictions split by layer, the opposed-expert gain arriving as dissent,
not votes, the optimal rule over the opening verdicts reducing under lock to
the uninterested seat's own, the like-biased second expert copying its
principal yet moving the verdict, and the flag working only where opposed
interests, an allocable burden and a spanned dimension coincide, on one-loser
verdicts, flooding or falling silent elsewhere.

\paragraph{Argumentative reasoning and social objectivity locate truth in the structure of criticism.}
On the argumentative account reasoning evolved for argument, individuals
produce biased advocacy and truth emerges in evaluation
\citep{mercier2011argumentative,mercier2017enigma,trouche2014arguments,trouche2016selective},
and more conflict between opinions should improve outcomes as evaluation
prevails over production \citep{mercier2016predictions}. Objectivity belongs
to a community's criticism and its uptake, not to any member
\citep{longino1990science,longino2002fate,popper2011open}. Assigned devil's
advocacy beats consensus \citep{schweiger1986group,schwenk1990effects}, yet a
role-played devil's advocate mainly provokes bolstering, unlike authentic
dissent \citep{nemeth2001devil}. We build the structure from copies of one
model and find the production half reproduced and the uptake half all but
absent, since the seats never converge and a collective fed its own dissent
does not move, so truth, where the collective was wrong, entered from outside
the graph, and our objectivity is a rule for reading dissent, the objection no
role explains carrying the information.

\paragraph{Cooperative AI asks what a third node can do, and correlated equilibrium sets the limit.}
Cooperative AI asks how agents with divergent interests reach joint welfare
\citep{dafoe2020cooperative}, and in repeated dilemmas language models play
with model-specific dispositions, several leaning cooperative
\citep{akata2025repeated,fontana2025nicer,willis2025cooperate,zheng2025bounded},
fail a commons without foresight \citep{piatti2024cooperate}, and shift with
an assigned persona \citep{leon2026personas,jia2025strategic}. Mediation,
contracting and one-word talk sustain cooperation
\citep{tewolde2026coopeval,wyse2026contracts,madmoun2026communication},
mediator design moves consensus \citep{liu2026promediate}, and negotiation
among role-assigned stakeholders is scorable \citep{abdelnabi2024negotiation}.
The formal basis is Aumann's mediator, whose correlated recommendation reaches
outcomes outside the Nash set \citep{aumann1974subjectivity}, though a direct
mechanism with equal commitment achieves the same
\citep{myerson1986multistage}. Our advocates lock at the opening statement
wherever interests diverge and yield at the vote on every payoff table,
leaving the flag nothing to read. Our games found what that theory predicts
and no more, since without enforcement every node and the solo recommend the
equilibrium in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma, whose correlated-equilibrium set
is its Nash set, and under a binding plan the model needs no help, so no
seating made cooperation self-enforcing, while with two equilibria the
collective coordinates where the solo declines, the mediator adding nothing
over a naive reader, and where no plan is unique it adds, given the mediator,
an unreplicated restraint.
