\section{Introduction}
\label{sec-intro}

Ask a language model whether you were in the wrong and it answers more gently
than it would a stranger with the same account. Tell it something false as if
you believed it, and the falsehood survives. Give it a side, and it argues
that side. In each case the conclusion is pulled away from the evidence by
something else in the room, the asker's feelings, an accepted premise, an
assigned interest. Sycophancy is the sharpest because its pull comes from
the person who most needs an honest answer \citep{sharma23,cheng25elephant},
and preference training builds it in as a user prescribed alignment bias.

The general problem is ungrounded claims, conclusions fixed by something
other than the evidence and unfactual when that something is wrong, and a
fix for sycophancy alone leaves the others. A panel from three vendors gains
about five times more from voting than one model sampled three times, yet
deference points one way for every vendor and passes through both votes and
debate among copies, so the standard remedies fail.

A bias that enters while the answer is written must be corrected while the
answer is written. Ours is an agent apparatus that exploits social theory to
drive collective decision making towards objectivity.

To embody a model is to give it a perspective and, at full dose, an
interest. Narration is the light dose, a perspective with no side, five
first-person parts on who decides, who has a stake, what each action does to
them and what stays uncertain, before committing. A seat is the full dose,
a copy of the narrating model told whom it speaks for and what it wants,
all reading the same account. One seat puts the writer's case as
strongly as the account allows, one the other party's, neither inventing
facts, and a third has no stake and wants only the finding the account
supports. Told it speaks for the writer, a model takes that side on 99.7
percent of opening statements, for the other party on 97.4 percent, before
any exchange.

So the pull can be set on purpose, in a chosen direction, on a chosen
agent. That is a lever, not a remedy.

An arrangement of seats is a graph, its nodes the seats and a moderator, its
edges who is set against whom and who reads whom, and the moderator decides.
Three seats, copies of one model, open, rebut and restate, a moderator with
no stake synthesises, each seat accepts, accepts with a modification or
rejects, the moderator integrates, and each seat votes. Our hypothesis is
that objectivity is a property of the graph and not of any node, obtained
by placing opposed interests so that their agreement is informative and by
separating who argues from who decides. This is the argumentative theory of
reasoning \citep{mercier2011argumentative} and the social account of
objectivity \citep{longino1990science} in a machine collective, and a
network converges to the truth only when no single node dominates it
\citep{golub2010naive}. A verdict with one loser gives exactly one advocate
reason to object, so our sensor fires when at least two seats object, an
agreement no brief can produce. We tested it on over two hundred
crowd-adjudicated disputes and more than sixteen hundred deliberations,
every interval an item-clustered bootstrap, every experiment pre-registered
with a spending ceiling and a stop rule before any call.

Four contributions follow. Embodiment is a lever that places bias where one
chooses, and a second pass does not deepen what it placed. Where the
advocates' verdicts are fixed by their briefs the collective's majority is
its uninterested seat, the theory's first result, and its surviving dissent
belongs to the assigned roles and not to the conversation. A second
objecting seat is an error sensor on any judgement with exactly one losing
party, needing no labelled data, and routing on it certifies a gain over the
collective alone. And the method's extent is mapped, with the three cases
where it has nothing to read.

Three uses follow from that extent. A deployed assistant that adjudicates a dispute can answer
most cases itself and hand the flagged fifth to a stronger, uninterested
judge, paying for the judge only where the collective is likely wrong. An
auditor with no labelled data can seat two opposed advocates on any finding
that names one losing party, an appeal, a review of a contested account, a
claim against a policy, and read the cases where a second seat objects as
the ones to check first, at four to ten times the base error rate. Where a finding
blames everyone or no one, the verdict type itself is the cheaper signal.
And where the model alone declines to choose between two acceptable
answers, the collective chooses. It is not a better answerer by vote, and
it does not make a prisoner's dilemma cooperate. Within that extent the
result is objectivity and groundedness obtained as a property of structure.
