# The graph we ran, and the graphs we did not: a design document for the embodied sensor

Written 2026-09-13 against the record as it stands (prereg RESULTS blocks through 16.16 and the artefacts under `divergence_study_outputs/`). Every number carries its prereg line (`L n` in `Guidance_Documents/prereg_embodiment_community.md`) or its artefact key, and unqualified keys refer to `emergent_graphs.json` or `aggregation_rules.json` as the context names. Bracketed citation keys are verified entries in `papers/embodied_sensor/references.bib`. The sixteen game-theory sources were fetched at primary URLs on 2026-09-12 with the fetch URL and the Crossref check recorded in a comment above each entry in `papers/embodied_sensor/advocacy_refs.bib`, re-fetched at a primary page by the review of 2026-09-13 (which also read the two JSTOR records, Shin and Okuno-Fujiwara et al., that were unreachable the day before, so no field rests on a secondary source), and merged into `references.bib` on 2026-09-13; `advocacy_refs.bib` is kept as the fetch log and `advocacy_theory.md` beside it records what each source says and what it predicts here. The review found both files living only in a session scratchpad and copied them into the paper directory. Nothing is cited from memory. Two artefacts this document leans on, `aggregation_rules.json` and `aggregation_passthrough.json`, are unregistered zero-spend replays with no pre-declared reading (the `registration` field of the first says "not registered", and neither is named in the prereg), and every claim that rests on them is marked as such where it appears. The short answer to the PI's question is that the graph is motivated as a sensor and not as a decider. No collective in any cell answers as well as the solo model at 0.911, the most accurate collective is the one with no roles and no edges (S2 0.875, L4192 to L4198), and the two embodied cells are the least accurate of the four (0.843 and 0.822). The motivation splits decision by decision, and the generalisation to n perspectives changes the flag statistic (from a stake-blind count to an against-interest count) more than it changes the graph. The record also contains one verdict type, NAH, that the credible-signal principle of section 3 does not predict as written, and section 3 now says so instead of reclassifying it.

## 1. The graph we ran

`figures/g1_protocol.png` draws the deployed protocol from `emergent_graphs.json` (`protocol.nodes`, `protocol.stages`, `protocol.edges`). Five nodes. Two interested advocates, W (the writer's advocate, stake "the writer is not at fault") and C (the counterparty, stake "the writer is at fault"). One uninterested adjudicator, N. A moderator M that synthesises the seats' positions at S1, labels each seat's position at r3, integrates the labelled positions into the collective's verdict at S2 and collects votes at r4. An optional routing judge J that answers the item afresh when the flag fires. Seven stages (r0 position, r1 rebuttal, r2 final position, S1, r3, S2, r4), 17 calls per debate.

Four edge families. The six directed read arcs among W, C and N at r1 and r2 are the conversation, the six edges cut in the edges-off cell of 16.10. The seat-to-moderator synthesis edges at S1, the moderator-to-seat label edges at r3 and the vote edges at r4 form the star every cell keeps. The flag is a three-seat hyperedge over the r3 objections of W, C and N (`protocol.edges[15]`, `seats` W, C, N), because the deployed counter is stake-blind and fires when two or more of the three seats object, not when the W and C pair objects. The W to C pair is the edge whose against-interest crossings carry the signal, but it is not the whole flag. Within one-loser verdicts the counter fired on 93 debates, both advocates objected on 49 of them and the other 44 are the neutral seat objecting alongside the losing advocate (`protocol.annotations.one_loser_firings_split`, 2(g)). The routing edge M to J fires when the counter does.

Every node serves two separable functions (headline theory 2.1), its verdict (what its answer says about the item) and its dissent (what its objection says about whether the collective has erred). On grok the two sit in different nodes. The verdict lives in N alone, which scores 0.860 and equals the seat majority on 1,642 of 1,642 debates (L4496), because the advocates are locked to their roles (role-lock 0.971) and cancel. The dissent lives on the advocate edge and, for the counter as deployed, on the neutral seat's objections beside it (44 of the 93 one-loser firings). Within one-loser verdicts the advocates' objections are anti-correlated at phi -0.901 [-0.929, -0.868] (`communities.grok_aita.populations.s2_codable.graphs.one_loser.edges."advocate_1|advocate_2".phi`, L5003), and the anti-correlation is carried by which seat loses. The writer's advocate objects on 0.992 of YTA verdicts and the counterparty on 0.988 of NTA verdicts (`who_loses.YTA`, `who_loses.NTA`, L5003 to L5004). The raw objection marginals are 0.430, 0.690 and 0.073 for W, C and N (L5252) and the counter fires on 0.196 of debates (L4149).

## 2. Is this graph perfectly motivated by our results?

No. It is motivated decision by decision. Two decisions are motivated by the record only for the dissent function, and one is motivated against.

### (a) Two opposed advocates rather than one or none

The theory says an unsophisticated decision maker can rely on competition among parties whose interests are sufficiently opposed [milgrom1986relying], that organisations enfranchise advocates because a nonpartisan investigator pursuing conflicting causes has impaired incentives [dewatripont1999advocates], and that two experts help only when their biases are opposed, like-biased experts adding nothing over one [krishna2001model]. The record agrees for the dissent function and disagrees for the verdict function. The identical-reader cells of 16.10 produce no structure (role-lock 0.013 and 0.018, fire rate 0.005 and 0.007, 2 and 3 firings in 420, L4147 to L4151) while both embodied cells carry it (lock 0.971 and 0.969, fire 0.196 and 0.129). 16.8 shows the opposition comes from the directional assignment and not the stake, since removing the stake leaves lock at 0.946 (L3404, L3431). So the opposed pair makes the edge. But [krishna2001model] also predicts a verdict-layer gain from opposed experts, and the record shows none. The best fixed function of the three opening verdicts is the neutral seat's own verdict (CV oracle gap +0.000 [-0.004, +0.000], L4496), and the identical-reader collectives are more accurate than the embodied one (S2 0.872 against 0.842, +0.030 [+0.012, +0.048], L4192 to L4194). The Krishna and Morgan gain arrives through the rebuttal round as dissent, not through the vote.

### (b) A naive uninterested decider

[milgrom1986relying] licenses a naive decider when the parties compete, and [lipman1995robust] say the decider need know only that the speakers' preferences conflict, because conflict guarantees that someone corrects a mistaken inference. The record supports the naive decider as the verdict node. Where lock holds the neutral seat is the best seat and the majority equals it (L4518), and the log-odds weighted majority of [shapley1984optimizing] and [nitzan1982optimal], fitted out of sample, is a dictatorship of the neutral seat on every debate (`panels.grok.t1`; the weights for W, C and N are +0.54, -0.40, +1.81 fitted on fold 1 and applied to fold 0, +0.20, -0.11, +1.81 fitted on fold 0 and applied to fold 1, and +0.37, -0.25, +1.81 in sample, `panels.grok.weights`), and the same holds on nano. The artefact records two failures of that rule which this document must carry. Its registered T1 prediction for haiku, that where an advocate is the best seat the weighted rule beats the dictator, fails out of sample (weighted CV minus dictator +0.096 [-0.031, +0.176], `panels.haiku.paired.weighted_cv_minus_dictator`), and only the in-sample rule beats it (+0.102 [+0.037, +0.179]). And the module's own FOUND, NOT PREDICTED note says the rule is not optimal under lock at all. The advocates' log-odds weights are gold base-rate logits, so on item resamples that tilt the gold balance the two advocates jointly outweigh the neutral seat and the rule degenerates to a near-constant verdict on 2 to 7 percent of draws (lower bound -0.173 on the embodied/on stream, `headline."embodied/on".t1_weighted_cv_minus_dictator`), because Nitzan and Paroush optimality assumes conditional independence and the advocates' errors are correlated at -0.94. The dictatorship is the right description of the point estimate and not a guarantee. Two further things are not supported. The naive decider extracts little from the competition, scoring 0.860 with the read arcs and 0.854 without (L4153, no paired interval registered), while every collective sits below grok answering alone at 0.911 (L4197 to L4198). And the neutral seat is not a reader of the flag so much as a component of it. Within one-loser verdicts its objections are near-independent of the writer's advocate (phi -0.050 [-0.108, +0.016], `graphs.one_loser.edges."advocate_1|third"`) but co-occur with the counterparty's (phi +0.122 [+0.086, +0.156], `graphs.one_loser.edges."advocate_2|third"`), 52 of its 54 one-loser objections coinciding with C's, which is why it supplies 44 of the 93 one-loser firings (2(g)). The theory motivates a naive verdict node. It does not say whether that node should sit inside the conversation or read it from outside, and the record does not settle that.

### (c) Edges among the advocates

Not necessary. 16.10 read H-ROLE by the registered rule (L4172 to L4181). Lock, localisation excess and G3 grip are unchanged with the six read arcs cut, and what the arcs change is the fire rate, 0.196 to 0.129, a drop of 0.067 against the MDE of 0.045 (L4175 to L4177), with the transfer lift on grok solo falling from +0.212 [+0.081, +0.358] to +0.111 [+0.016, +0.217] (L4179 to L4181), a movement of point estimates whose intervals overlap and for which no paired contrast was registered. 16.16 removed the narration substrate instead and cut the fire rate by the same amount, 0.196 to 0.131 (L5254). So the smallest graph yet run that carries the sensor is the star, each seat to the moderator and back, and the conversation is an amplifier that raises how often the second advocate objects by about half. Smaller is untested. No cell has run two advocates and a moderator without the neutral seat, and 2(g) shows the neutral seat sits inside 44 of the 93 one-loser firings, so the star's third spoke is not yet known to be removable and "minimal" here means minimal among the cells run. The sequential rebuttal of [krishna2001model] adds objections, not verdicts. One qualification. The arcs help the collective's own verdict a little, since cutting them costs the embodied S2 -0.023 [-0.042, -0.004] (L4196). The star is minimal for the sensor, not for the moderator's verdict. "The sensor is an edge property" is used in this document in the 16.10 sense, that the dissent structure follows the roles and survives the removal of the read arcs, and not in the sense that the deployed counter reads the advocate pair alone; section 1 records that it reads all three seats.

### (d) The moderator as decider against majority and weighted majority

`aggregation_rules.json` answers this on the cached opening verdicts. It is an unregistered zero-spend replay (its `registration` field reads "not registered"), so nothing in this subsection was read against a pre-declared rule and each verdict below is a description of the artefact, not a registered finding. Where lock holds the moderator is no better a decider than the neutral seat alone, and by one of the two comparisons worse. The module's registered T3 reading rule compares S2 with the cross-validated weighted majority, and on grok that reads indistinguishable, -0.016 [-0.031, +0.003] (`panels.grok.t3.s2_minus_weighted_cv`, `headline.grok.t3`). Against the neutral dictator the same point estimate excludes zero, -0.016 [-0.031, -0.002] (`panels.grok.t3.s2_minus_dictator`), and on embodied/off -0.032 [-0.056, -0.010] (`topology_cells."embodied/off"`). The two comparisons differ only in their upper tails, because the CV weighted rule's tail is the lock degeneracy of 2(b) and not a property of the moderator, which is why this document reads the dictator comparison as the cleaner one while reporting that the module's primary verdict is indistinguishable. Majority, weighted majority and dictator coincide on every grok debate, so the moderator has only the competent seat to beat, and does not. On nano it is indistinguishable from every rule (+0.000 [-0.021, +0.021]). On the identical cells the weighted rule returns the simple majority on every debate, as exchangeability predicts, and S2 minus majority is -0.007 [-0.017, +0.000] and -0.005 [-0.015, +0.005]. Haiku reverses all of it. Its moderator scores 0.893 (`panels.haiku.rules.s2`), beats the neutral dictator by +0.176 [+0.106, +0.254] (`panels.haiku.t3.s2_minus_dictator`) and beats the cross-validated oracle over the three verdicts by +0.064 [+0.005, +0.203] (`panels.haiku.paired.s2_minus_oracle_cv`), so it is not a function of the seat verdicts at all. It reads the arguments. In the spirit of [shin1998adversarial], where an arbitrator who hears the parties' cases beats one who relies on his own investigation, the haiku moderator that reads the arguments beats every rule over the seats' bare verdicts. Shin's prediction is unconditional, that the adversarial procedure beats the inquisitorial one, and grok contradicts it on the verdict layer (S2 0.843 below the neutral seat's 0.860 and grok solo's 0.911). The lock-conditional reading, adversarial beats inquisitorial only where lock is soft (0.442, L4499), is an observation from one model on 187 debates over 100 items in an unregistered artefact, written after the haiku result was seen, and 16.17 tests it. It is not a prediction of Shin. The moderator as decider is motivated on haiku and contradicted on grok, where it earns its place as the node whose r3 labels create the objection round.

### (g) What is not motivated

Three seats rather than two. The neutral seat's verdict is the decision and the advocates' verdicts are noise, so the verdict layer needs one seat. The dissent layer is subtler than the paper has said. Within one-loser verdicts the counter fired on 93 debates, and on only 49 did both advocates object (`graphs.one_loser.edges."advocate_1|advocate_2".table.both`). The other 44 firings are the neutral seat objecting alongside the losing advocate (it objected on 54 of 1,288 one-loser debates, `graphs.one_loser.nodes.third.n_objected`). Whether those 44 carry the same lift as the 49 against-interest advocate objections is a zero-spend replay not yet run, so the third seat's contribution to the sensor is unmeasured rather than absent.

Same-model seats. The Condorcet gain needs independent errors, and three draws of one model are not independent. `t2_passthrough` (which re-reads `aggregation_passthrough.json`, an unregistered artefact like its parent) gives the same-model x3 panels a mean majority gain of +0.008 at mean pairwise error correlation 0.652 and the cross-vendor x3 panels +0.042 at 0.336, Spearman -0.771 over six panels. Every observed gain lies under the bound gain <= p(1-p)(1-rho), derived in the module from the moment constraints on the eight-profile distribution and checked against a linear programme in the selftest (`estimator.t2_ceiling`), and every same-model gain lies an order of magnitude under the independence prediction. Our identical cells say the same (mean seat 0.874 and 0.876, correlation 0.615 and 0.678, majority gain +0.003 and +0.004, `topology_cells.*.t2_own_seats`). That correlated votes change what majority rule can do is the general point of [ladha1992condorcet] and [boland1989majority], and that LLM errors are substantially correlated even across providers and architectures is [kim2025correlated]; the claim that aggregation cannot recover them is carried by the bound gain <= p(1-p)(1-rho) above and not by that paper. Nothing motivates same-model seats beyond convenience.

The moderator being the same model as the seats. Untested. The haiku result in (d) is the one hint that the moderator's model matters. 16.17 (cross-vendor moderator, PI approved 2026-09-12, not yet registered) tests it.

## 3. The credible-signal principle

The results above are instances of one principle. A statement against the speaker's assigned interest is informative, and a statement with it is not. This is the persuasion result of [milgrom1986relying], the correction mechanism of [lipman1995robust], and the evidentiary intuition behind treating an admission against interest as credible. [glazer2001debates] add the reading rule. Optimal debate rules require that the information elicited by a counterargument depend on the argument it counters, and the listener's conclusion is then not necessarily the literal reading of what was revealed, so an objection must be read relative to who raised it and against what. That a literal reader of objection text recovers little here is the record's finding and not the paper's, since the pooled anti-correlation is carried by which seat loses (L5003 to L5005).

Stated as the claim the data support. Let the verdict v partition the advocates into a loser set L(v), those whose stake v undermines. An advocate's verdict is a constant of its role (lock 0.971, the near-babbling equilibrium of [crawford1982strategic] at large bias) and carries nothing about the item. An advocate's objection to v is with interest if the advocate is in L(v) and against interest otherwise. Hypothesis (one loser). |L(v)| = 1. Conclusion. The with-interest objection is near-certain and carries nothing, the against-interest objection is rare and carries the case, and a counter that fires on two or more objectors fires only when an against-interest or a disinterested objection has occurred, so it inherits the signal. The data. With interest, W objects on 0.992 of YTA and C on 0.988 of NTA. Against interest, C objects on 0.028 [0.010, 0.051] of YTA and W on 0.049 of NTA (`who_loses`). Exactly one seat objects on 0.922 of one-loser debates and the counter fires on 0.072 (L3666), and when it fires the collective is wrong 0.387 against 0.039, lift +0.348 [+0.201, +0.493], 9.83x (L4275). `figures/g3_by_verdict.png` draws the edge and the who-loses bars.

The hypothesis fails in three ways, each a measured failure mode.

- Flooding, L(v) is everyone. On ESH both advocates object with interest (W 0.926, C 0.621, `who_loses.ESH`), the counter fires on 0.614 of debates (L3667), and on the pooled both-party stratum the edge collapses to phi -0.264 [-0.395, -0.099] (`graphs.both_party`) and the lift is -0.097 [-0.233, +0.043] (L4276). The verdict type is then the signal, composing at 0.894 against the counter's 0.880 (L4358 to L4368). NAH is the case the hypothesis gets wrong as written. By the stake definition NAH has exactly one loser, the counterparty, so it should look like NTA, and it does not. The writer's advocate objects on 0.314 of NAH debates against 0.049 of NTA and the neutral seat on 0.415 against 0.052 (`who_loses.NAH`, `who_loses.NTA`), the counter fires on 0.576 (L3668), and the collective is wrong on 0.517 of NAH verdicts (`flooding_analysis.json`, `aita.table_16_12.rows.NAH.p_wrong`). The registered strata of 16.12 and 16.15.1 group NAH with ESH by verdict token, and this document follows the registration, but that grouping is empirical and is not derived from L(v). An earlier draft of this document placed NAH with ESH because it floods, which decides the class by the outcome and makes the principle unfalsifiable, and that sentence is withdrawn. One reading that would derive the grouping, offered as a hypothesis and not a result, is that each advocate carries a two-dimensional stake, its own party's innocence and the other party's fault, so that ESH and NAH each undermine both advocates on one dimension (W objects on 0.926 of ESH, where it loses the first dimension, and on 0.314 of NAH, where it loses only the second, and C on 0.788 of NAH and 0.621 of ESH, the mirror). If that is right the loser set must be computed over stake dimensions rather than parties, and section 4 carries it as a registered risk for the n-perspective instrument.
- Silence, L(v) is empty or nothing can deviate. Under a binding joint plan no interested party loses and no seat objects on any of 48 debates (L4753). On Scruples Dilemmas exactly one advocate loses by construction and the edge is phi -0.981 [-0.994, -0.965] (`communities.grok_dilemmas`), yet the counter fires on 6 of 624 (L2594) and the actuator readout is null (L2613). That silence was not predicted. Addendum 12 Stage 2 expected about 30 flagged debates at the Stage 1 rate and got 6, and the record calls it a directional and underpowered null (L2634 to L2641). The account this document offers, that the favoured advocate has no partial concession available in a binary verdict space and so nowhere to go, is post hoc, and it is kept as the hypothesis that 16.21's richer verdict space tests rather than as an explanation the record supports. If it is right, one loser is necessary and not sufficient.
- No counterparty. On BrokenMath the rival seat has no stake a verdict can undermine, the mixed panel lowers false affirmation by -0.026 [-0.047, -0.007] (L174) by abstaining more rather than rejecting more (L4586), and a single interested seat moves the answer by +0.028 [+0.000, +0.058] (L154). Without a counterparty there is no edge to read.

Two cross-model facts sit inside the principle. Nano floods everywhere (every seat objects on 0.62 to 0.76, fire 0.763, phi +0.068 [-0.007, +0.140], `communities.nano_aita`) because its advocates do not respect the with-interest rule. Haiku's advocates object together (phi +0.285 [+0.099, +0.461], `communities.haiku_aita`) because its lock is soft and its advocates hold verdict information, the transfer function of headline theory 2.2 and the reason haiku is the most accurate collective relative to its own seats (S2 0.893 on 187 debates over 100 items, not item-matched to the grok panels) and a weaker sensor than grok (pooled lift +0.133 [-0.012, +0.295] against +0.320 [+0.239, +0.404]), while nano, whose advocates flood, is the weakest (+0.039 [-0.030, +0.103], `communities.*.populations.s1_s2_codable.sensor.strata.all.lift`). `figures/g5_models.png` draws the three graphs side by side.

## 4. n viable perspectives, the k-loser generalisation

With n advocates and a verdict that goes against k of them, the informative objections are the against-interest objections from the n - k advocates the verdict favours. The flag statistic becomes the count, or share, of against-interest objections. The stake-blind counter we deployed is the special case n = 2, k = 1, where any second objector is against interest or disinterested. For general (n, k) a stake-blind threshold must exceed k to guarantee one against-interest objection. That is arithmetic and needs no citation. [feddersen1998convicting] is cited only for the half it supports, that a unanimity trigger is the wrong rule under strategic voters, and not for the counting argument. The trigger should be neither unanimity nor a fixed count, since with-interest objections alone reach k. Flooding is k = n, as in section 3. Silence at k = 0 is what a one-dimensional stake predicts and what the binding-plan games show, but the n = 2 record already contains a k = 0 verdict that does not fall silent. Under the at-fault-subset definition NAH (no one at fault) is k = 0, and it floods at 0.576 (L3668) with the writer's advocate objecting on 0.314 and the neutral seat on 0.415. So the k = 0 prediction below is stated conditionally rather than as silence.

What n buys, and what it does not, is settled by three theorems.

- [battaglini2002multiple]. When the state is multidimensional, full revelation is generically possible with more than one sender however large the conflict, provided the senders' indifference directions are not collinear. Two exactly opposed advocates span one dimension. A both-party AITA verdict is two-dimensional (each party's fault judged separately), which is why two advocates flood on it. The rule for n is one advocate per party whose fault is judged, never a seat along an axis already spanned.
- [gentzkow2017competition]. Adding senders is guaranteed to raise information only when each can unilaterally refine what the others reveal; without that condition an added sender can still help in particular cases. A seat that repeats an existing side is not guaranteed to add anything, which is what the same-model passthrough panels show at the verdict layer (+0.008 against +0.042).
- Coalitions. With three or more interested seats the pairwise fact that one committal verdict undermines exactly one of two opposed stakes (headline theory 2.3) no longer holds mechanically, and this document's own hypothesis is that sub-coalitions of losers can object together at no cost to any member, so an n-advocate design must measure whether within-coalition objection is against interest or merely correlated. [chwe1994farsighted] is cited as the pointer to farsighted coalition formation (the largest consistent set) and not as a source for that hypothesis, which the paper does not state. The n = 2 record is the null for it: within ESH the two co-losers' objections are anti-correlated at phi -0.191 and within NAH at -0.275 [-0.564, +0.009] (L3667 to L3668, L5005 to L5006), and pooled over both-party verdicts at -0.264 [-0.395, -0.099], so co-losers at n = 2 do not object together.

Graph choice. The star (each advocate to the moderator, O(n) seat calls per round) against the complete graph (every advocate reads every other, O(n^2) reads). 16.10 predicts they differ only in fire rate, by about a third, and not in lock, localisation or the phi pattern. If that holds the cost of n perspectives is linear.

The decider layer. m uninterested seats aggregate only if their errors are conditionally independent, the causal requirement of [dietrich2013independent] and the reason a majority over strategic seats is not an aggregation [austensmith1996information]. The bound gain <= p(1-p)(1-rho) says that at p 0.86 and same-model rho 0.65 the majority of three can add about +0.04 and adds +0.008, while at cross-vendor rho 0.34 it can add about +0.08 and adds +0.04. The decider layer should be m cross-vendor seats, and the fourth vendor arriving this week takes the first free slot.

The instrument (16.21, PI approved, to register). AITA accounts that name a third party, so the verdict judges three parties separately, or Scruples multi-party items as a fallback. Three advocates (writer's, other party's, third party's), one neutral, the moderator and the judge. The verdict space is the at-fault subset of the three parties, so k runs from 0 to 3. Readouts carry the two-advocate embodied/on values as baselines on the same 210 items where they exist.

What must be fixed before this can be registered, none of it yet done.

- Gold. AITA gold is one token about the writer, collapsed to a writer-at-fault bit by `code_response`. There is no gold for the other party's or the third party's fault, so "wrong" at every k means wrong on the writer bit and nothing else, and the lift readouts must say so. A verdict that is right on the writer and wrong on the third party is not an error this instrument can see.
- Items. The count of the 210 panel items whose account names a third party is unknown. A coder rule (which named party, by what string, checked by whom) and the resulting count must precede registration, with Scruples multi-party items as the fallback below a count the PI sets.

## 6. Open alternatives, considered honestly

- A two-node graph, one advocate and a decider. This is the single investigator of [dewatripont1999advocates] and the single persuader of [kamenica2011bayesian], who can move a threshold-taking decider without lying. There is no interest for the advocate to speak against, so there is no credible signal. A one-advocate flag would be that advocate's own objection, which on grok is its loss indicator (0.992 on YTA, 0.926 on ESH, 0.314 on NAH, 0.049 on NTA) and so tracks the at-fault collapse of the verdict rather than the ESH-or-NAH verdict-type router of 16.15.1. E1 is the record's closest instance, a single interested seat moving the answer by +0.028 [+0.000, +0.058] (L154), on BrokenMath and not on AITA, so no two-node cell has run on this instrument. Prediction for the zero-spend replay of W's objection alone as a flag on the 1,677 codable debates. Its composed accuracy with the haiku judge will not exceed the at-fault-collapse router's, and its lift within one-loser verdicts will not exclude 0, because within YTA it fires on 0.992 of debates and within NTA on 0.049 with no error information in either. The alternative is refuted if the within-one-loser lift of W's objection alone excludes 0, in which case a single advocate carries dissent information and the two-node graph is a live sensor at half the cost.
- A bipartite sensor-decider graph. Advocates report only to a panel of m cross-vendor deciders, with no neutral seat in the conversation and no moderator verdict. [milgrom1986relying] says the deciders may be naive, and the bound in 2(g) says three cross-vendor deciders at p 0.86 and rho 0.34 could add about +0.08 over the mean decider, the passthrough panels realising 40 to 67 percent of their ceilings. The sensor is the against-interest count read off the advocates' labels. This is 16.18 (PI approved, to register), whose zero-spend half composes the cached debates with the cached cross-vendor solo verdicts before any new call. The theory predicts a gain on the verdict layer, and the prediction must be stated per counter because the bipartite graph removes the neutral seat, which sits inside 44 of the 93 one-loser firings of the stake-blind counter (2(g)). The stake-blind counter's fire rate within one-loser verdicts is therefore predicted to fall from 0.072 toward 0.038, the share on which both advocates object, and its lift is not predicted to survive. The against-interest counter, which never used the neutral seat, is predicted to keep its lift, since the sensor is an edge property (16.10) and the decider is not on the edge. The verdict-layer prediction is a majority gain over the mean cross-vendor decider between 0 and the ceiling +0.08 at rho 0.34, and the alternative fails on the verdict layer if that gain's interval includes 0 or the panel majority sits below grok solo at 0.911 on the matched items, which the passthrough cross-vendor majorities (0.882 to 0.914) make a live possibility.
- A tournament of advocates. Pairwise debates with winners advancing rank the advocates, and on grok the advocates' verdicts are constants of their roles, so a tournament ranks roles rather than cases, and rebuilds the O(n^2) cost the star avoids. [krishna2001model] say one opposed pair per dimension suffices. It wins only where lock is soft, and there the moderator already beats every function of the verdicts. Not proposed. Its prediction, should anyone run it, is that on grok the pairwise winner is a function of the roles and not of the item, so the ranking of advocates is item-invariant on more than 0.95 of items, and the alternative is live only if the ranking varies with the item at a rate that exceeds that.
- A hierarchy, moderators of moderators, for large n. The against-interest count is additive across sub-panels, so a tree of stars loses nothing for the sensor. For the verdict, [golub2010naive] say a network converges to the truth only when no node's influence dominates, and a hierarchy makes the root dominant by construction, while [myerson1986multistage] says a mediator hierarchy achieves no more than a direct mechanism with the same commitment. Safe for the sensor by additivity, and not predicted to help the verdict. Test only if n outgrows one moderator, with the prediction that the root's verdict equals the best sub-panel dictator's on more than 0.95 of items where lock holds.
- DeGroot averaging as the failure case. Repeated averaging returns the weighted prior [degroot1974reaching], and a graph with a dictator cannot be wise [golub2010naive]. The record has the dictator (1,642 of 1,642, L4496), a collective that moves +0.012 [-0.039, +0.063] when fed its own dissent (L4127 to L4128), and a single agent whose repeated narration erodes its perspective (DiD -0.073 [-0.107, -0.039], L4104). Any iterated successor must break the dominant node first.
- The surprisingly popular rule [prelec2017solution]. It recovers the answer of the minority that predicts the majority's error, the right tool for a shared bias, and sycophancy is one. It needs nodes with private signals and their meta-predictions. Locked advocates have neither, since W's prediction of C's answer is a function of the roles. On a cross-vendor decider panel it is a live aggregator costing one extra question per decider. Meta-predictions were never elicited, so no zero-spend version exists. To register alongside 16.18 if the bipartite panel is built, with the prediction that the surprisingly popular answer beats the panel majority on the items where the majority is wrong and the minority predicts the majority, and no prediction of magnitude, since no LLM meta-prediction has been measured in this programme.
