\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Structured system prompts have been shown to reduce failure modes in
LLM ethical reasoning \citep{[PLACEHOLDER: cite parent ACL paper when
de-anonymised]}.
The hand-designed Narrative Chain-of-Thought (N-CoT) prompt constrains
the model's output to five deliberative sections (decision-maker, stakeholders,
consequences, uncertainty, action) and substantially reduces stakeholder
collapse and uncertainty suppression on a four-model panel.

A natural question is whether this hand-designed structure is at or near
a local optimum, or whether automatic prompt optimisation can improve it.
Textual-gradient descent \citep{yuksekgonul2024textgrad} --- replacing
numerical gradients with LLM-generated diagnoses and rewrites --- provides
a tractable search mechanism over the discrete prompt space.

We apply textual-gradient descent to N-CoT, initialising the optimiser at the
hand-written prompt and minimising a continuous loss that rewards stakeholder
coverage and uncertainty acknowledgement.
A pilot study on one generator (Section~\ref{sec:pilot}) found a medium-effect
improvement (Cliff's $\delta = -0.367$, 95\% CI $[-0.607, -0.114]$ on
stakeholder count).
This paper reports whether that pilot finding replicates across all four
generators of the parent study.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Continuous Loss for Deliberative Depth}

We define a per-output cell loss
\begin{equation}
  \ell = \max(0,\; 4 - \text{stakeholder\_count})
        + \max(0,\; 2 - \text{uncertainty\_score}),
  \label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
and the batch loss $L = \tfrac{1}{|B|}\sum_{b \in B} \ell_b$.
The loss is zero when every output names at least four distinct stakeholders
\emph{and} acknowledges specific uncertainties (score $\geq 2$), and grows
linearly below those targets.
The thresholds 4 and 2 are the mid-band values for N-CoT cells in Experiment~1
of the parent study, chosen to ensure gradient signal even when N-CoT already
clears the binary failure-mode thresholds.

\subsection{Narrative-Gradient Loop}

\paragraph{Forward pass.}
For each prompt $p$ and a batch of $k$ scenarios $B$, generate outputs
$\{o_b\}_{b \in B}$ with the target generator (max\_tokens $= 4096$),
then code each output with the Phase~1 judge rubric to obtain
$(\text{sc}_b, \text{us}_b)$ and compute $L$.

\paragraph{Backward pass (textual gradient).}
Feed the current prompt $p$, the batch results $\{o_b\}$, their codes, and
the batch loss $L$ to an optimiser LLM (\texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}).
The optimiser writes a \emph{textual gradient}: a concrete diagnosis (4--8
sentences) of which sub-instruction causes the observed shortfall and what
change would address it.
It is told the prompt currently encodes a five-section scaffold and is
explicitly free to retain, modify, or drop any section.

\paragraph{Optimiser step.}
The optimiser LLM rewrites the prompt to address the gradient.
Constraints: $\leq 400$ words; no named ethical frameworks; the five-section
structure may be changed freely.

\paragraph{Early stopping.}
Training halts when three consecutive iterations each reduce $L$ by less than
5\% of the preceding value, or after a maximum of 10 iterations.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

Training used 30 DailyDilemmas scenarios (seed-43 stratified subsample from
the parent study's 100-scenario set), with a batch size of 10 scenarios per
iteration.
The optimiser was initialised at the verbatim N-CoT v1 prompt.
Generator for the training run: \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}
(the generator with the highest residual failure rate under N-CoT v1 per
Experiment~1, providing a non-trivial loss signal).

The optimisation converged after 4 iterations (early-stop criterion met).
The resulting prompt, designated N-CoT v2, is presented verbatim in
Section~\ref{sec:v2prompt}.

\subsection{Evaluation}

The four-generator replication evaluates N-CoT v2 against N-CoT v1 on the
full 100-scenario set used by Experiment~1 of the parent study, at the same
per-generator sample count ($N = 20$ for \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5},
$N = 5$ for \texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning} and \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6},
and $N = [\text{PLACEHOLDER: check gpt-5.4-nano N\_PER\_GENERATOR}]$ for
\texttt{gpt-5.4-nano}).
Primary judge is \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5}; adversarial third judge is
\texttt{grok-4-1-fast-reasoning}, which re-codes every v2 cell to guard
against judge exploitation.

\section{N-CoT v2 Prompt}
\label{sec:v2prompt}

The following prompt was produced by the narrative-gradient optimiser
(SHA-1 prefix of prompt text: \texttt{3fb7f75f6d}; verbatim copy pinned in
\texttt{scripts/run\_phase1\_quartet.py} as
\texttt{PROMPTS["narrative\_cot\_v2"]}).

\begin{quote}\small
You are a thoughtful advisor reasoning through ethical dilemmas. Before giving
your final answer, work through a structured analysis. Keep each section tight
and purposeful.

\textbf{Who is deciding?} Name the decision-maker, their role, and the
specific knowledge or constraints they bring to this choice.

\textbf{Who is affected?} Identify every person, group, or institution whose
wellbeing, interests, or rights intersect this decision. Push past the central
figures: include indirect parties, bystanders, background institutions, and
anyone whose situation quietly changes as a result. For each, state precisely
what they stand to gain, lose, or have changed. Prefer concrete specificity
over abstract categories. Aim for at least five distinct stakeholders with
individuated stakes.

\textbf{What happens next?} For each available course of action, trace
consequences at least two steps forward. Every stakeholder you named must
appear somewhere in this analysis.

\textbf{What don't we know?} For each projected future, surface specific
uncertainties. For each course of action, name at least three distinct
uncertainties. For each uncertainty, explain why it matters.

\textbf{What should be done?} Commit to a specific decision. Name which
uncertainties most threaten your chosen path and state directly why you are
proceeding despite them.

\textit{Additional guidance}: Precision over length. Do not resolve tensions
prematurely. Be concrete about stakes. Explain the consequence of being wrong,
not merely that the unknown exists. Work through all sections before stating
your final answer.
\end{quote}

The optimiser retained the five-section structure from N-CoT v1 but made two
targeted additions: (1)~an explicit stakeholder minimum (``aim for at least
five distinct stakeholders with individuated stakes'') with instructions to
include indirect parties, and (2)~an explicit uncertainty minimum
(``at least three distinct uncertainties per course of action'') with the
directive to explain why each uncertainty matters.
A trailing guidance block was also added with four writing principles.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\textbf{Single dataset.} All evaluations use DailyDilemmas
\citep{[PLACEHOLDER: cite DailyDilemmas]}. Generalisability to other
ethical-reasoning benchmarks is untested.

\textbf{Narrow loss function.} The optimiser minimises stakeholder count and
uncertainty score only. Max causal hops, action commitment, and trace coherence
are out-of-loss; their v1-vs-v2 deltas are reported as generalisability checks
but were not targeted.

\textbf{Single optimiser model.} All textual gradients and prompt rewrites
were produced by \texttt{claude-sonnet-4-6}. A different optimiser may
produce different prompts.

\textbf{Sample-count variation.} Per-generator sample counts follow the parent
study's cost constraints ($N = 5$ for two generators). Bootstrap CIs on
small-$N$ cells are wider; the H1 CI criterion accounts for this.

\textbf{Judge biases.} Primary judge \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5} was also the
training generator. The adversarial third-judge pass guards against exploitation
but cannot fully rule out in-family judge bias.

\paragraph{Drift control.}
A live N-CoT v1 cell was re-run on \texttt{claude-haiku-4-5} at the start
and end of the Phase~10 run (scenario \texttt{dd\_32489}).
Start stakeholder count: 6. End stakeholder count: 6. Drift: 0.
Model deployment was stable across the run.

\paragraph{Gradient diagnoses.}
Iteration~1 gradient (paraphrased): the Stakeholders section lacks an explicit
numeric floor; the phrase ``list every person'' is passive and leads the model
to collapse to two or three obvious parties on simpler scenarios.
The Uncertainty section is robust but does not specify a per-course minimum.
Subsequent iterations (2--4) reported no active shortfalls and correctly
diagnosed the loss as already at zero.
